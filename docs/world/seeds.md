# Seeds

## 1. Twenty hooks

Each names who asks, what is wrong, and the turn. Ranks are the party's; scale the gate to them.

1. **Four on a Thursday.** Sun-hee Marlowe of the Lantern Company overbid on six D gates and four break the same
   day. She needs one cleared quietly by people not on her books. *The turn:* it is a double, and the second door
   reads two ranks higher.
2. **The first offer.** A pleasant scout buys the party dinner in Saltmarket and slides over a model contract.
   *The turn:* clauses 1, 3 and 6 of `careers.md`. A lawyer costs 120 gold an hour; do they pay it?
3. **The door in the classroom.** A hunter's personal door has opened in the school where they teach. It cannot be
   moved and waits a day. *The turn:* a child saw it. Children cannot see personal doors.
4. **Potions for the ward.** Pim Aldous, seat clerk, will move the party up the queue for ten Health Potions.
   *The turn:* follow the potions to a Scarside ward and find out who is in the beds: unregistered kindled children,
   hidden from the Reading.
5. **Ripe.** Helix is holding a C gate to its last day, two streets from a school. Reclaimers' Local 7 will pay
   the party to clear it early and take the fine (two Clears each). *The turn:* the boss is no longer the rank on
   the board.
6. **Seat insurance.** Kofi Stein guarantees the party a gate this month. *The turn:* the seat is on a night crew's
   poached gate, and the rights holder's party arrives while they are inside.
7. **The body in the unentered gate.** A posted, sealed E gate. A dead hunter inside, three days gone, with a dark
   title on the corpse's window that reads to any hunter. *The turn:* the dead one is the killer. Who killed them?
8. **Call-up.** The siren's rising note, mid-session, mid-anything. Colonel Yun's cordon needs every licence in
   the district at Tidewall in twenty minutes. *The turn:* the list of who does not come.
9. **The Spotter's secret.** Mikkel Strand has found a forty-second gate. He asks the party to walk him home.
   *The turn:* he is told where they will open, by a voice that is not his window's.
10. **Eleven crystals.** Ironwake's quartermaster hires outsiders to find where eleven C crystals went. *The turn:*
    they were used. On reclaimers. It worked on two of them.
11. **The second line.** "Six" of Aurelian wants one gate with their own name on it, and needs a party no camera
    knows. *The turn:* Dax Verrault asks to come, and means it.
12. **Day six.** Twelve of the Open Hand sit in a cellar in front of a D gate nobody reported. The cordon will
    storm it in an hour. *The turn:* Baptiste Roux asks the party to go in with him, unarmed, and look.
13. **The attic.** Councillor Baskin needs her son read without the Register knowing, by a hunter who can say
    truthfully what his window shows. *The turn:* his window shows a job nobody has seen before.
14. **Three dawns.** A friend of the party died in a gate that then went red and sealed. Two dawns remain. A
    crystal of the right rank is ten Clears, and Auntie Quill will lend it. *The turn:* her price is not gold.
15. **The standing gate.** The B gate under the Scar's flooded car park never closes and is never the same inside.
    Renzo Okabe wants it read from within. *The turn:* it reads differently each hour, and it reads the party.
16. **Changed Daily.** One of the party wakes to a Daily that is not exercise: *stand at the Quiet Mile at dusk.*
    *The turn:* Mother Whitlow is there. So are forty other hunters who have never met.
17. **The round.** A B-rank lies in the hunter hospital with a wound potions will not close. The Plain Men have a
    new bullet. *The turn:* the crystal in it comes from one gate, and Helix holds its rights.
18. **Exit permit.** A C-rank healer wants out of the country to a state that will pay her triple. Smuggling costs
    ten Clears; she offers a year of her service after. *The turn:* the Corps already knows, and would rather she
    led them to the smuggler.
19. **The Risen comes to town.** Sabine Okoro is in Carrow, running public gates alone and asking assessors one
    question: which gates changed after they were read? *The turn:* she offers the party a seat, above the Rank
    Floor, for nothing.
20. **03:14.** Arlo Finch's reactor core hums at the minute the gates opened, every night, and last night
    something in the standing gate hummed back. *The turn:* whichever truth of `power.md` section 8 the GM chose,
    this is the first time the party hears it.

## 2. Ten places

1. **The Halfway House, Ledger Row.** The Bureau's hall. A stone floor worn in a line to a block of crystal, a
   public gallery with a brass rail, forty queue windows. *Use it for:* the first scene of any campaign. *Detail:*
   the rail is polished on the left side only, where the scouts lean.
2. **Stonemarket.** Three covered streets behind the Helix gate where crystal is assayed and sold. Scales, blue
   dust on everything, cutters with missing fingertips. *Use it for:* selling a core, hearing prices, being
   followed. *Detail:* the exchange bell rings when an A core is weighed, and the whole street stops.
3. **The Scar.** Two kilometres of rubble through the east of the city, from the B break of Year 1. Grass, sheds,
   a market on Sundays, people who kindled young. A flooded car park with a gate in its lowest level that has stood
   for nineteen years. *Use it for:* anything unlicensed. *Detail:* nobody in Scarside owns a go-bag; where would
   they go?
4. **The Quiet Mile.** A straight street in the Crown where a D pack came through a school run on the seventh
   day. No traffic since; plane trees and nine hundred small brass plates. *Use it for:* a meeting nobody will
   interrupt. *Detail:* hunters do not wear weapons on it. Nobody made a rule.
5. **The Ninth Hour's tower.** Forty storeys of grey stone, no sign, a public square at its foot with the best
   response time on Earth. *Use it for:* awe, and a door that opens once. *Detail:* a light on the top floor means
   Amara Nkemelu is home. The city checks.
6. **Founders' Park at dawn.** Two thousand people doing squats in rows, the Patient in grey at the front,
   commuters at the back, tea sellers along the path. *Use it for:* the Ledger, a quiet word, comedy. *Detail:*
   Mother Whitlow counts aloud, and has never lost count.
7. **The Last Lantern.** Redline's bar on Tidewall. Dispatch board behind the counter, day rates chalked up, a
   back room where a healer keeps hours. *Use it for:* hiring a fifth, being hired. *Detail:* one stool is always
   empty, with a full glass. Ask whose and the room goes quiet.
8. **A gate's mouth.** Wherever it is: a stairwell, a tram depot, a wheat field. A door of light a little too
   tall, a rope, a cordon tent, a board with a letter and a number of days, lanterns waiting. *Use it for:* every
   session. *Detail:* it is cold within a metre of it and watches stop.
9. **The hunter hospital car park.** Between two buildings: one where potions work, one where they work thin.
   Smokers from both. A League vigil most nights, candles in jars. *Use it for:* the world's central argument, said
   quietly by tired people.
10. **Port Sarn.** Six hundred kilometres up the coast. A harbour city of two million, empty for twenty years,
    intact where it is not crushed, with an S gate standing in the fish market and a fence around the whole of it
    that the Accord's inspectors walk. *Use it for:* the end of a campaign. *Detail:* the tide tables posted on
    the harbour office are still correct.

## 3. The primer

Read this aloud. It takes three minutes.

> Twenty years ago, on an ordinary night, doors of light opened all over the world. Nothing came out. People
> filmed them.
>
> Two days later the largest one broke, and a city of two million was gone by dark. The next day a hundred more.
> By the seventh day there were things in every town on Earth. We call that the Long Week. One person in twenty
> did not live through the three years that followed.
>
> We lived because some of us changed. A fever, three days, and then a body that could fight. One in five hundred.
> We call them hunters. Nobody knows why them.
>
> The doors are called gates and they never stopped opening. Each has a rank, E up to S, and a clock: an E gate
> breaks in seven days, an S gate in two. Go in, kill what rules it, and it closes. Leave it, and it comes to you.
>
> Something pays hunters for this. It puts gold in a place only they can reach, and sells them things no one on
> Earth can make: a potion that closes a wound, a crystal that brings back the dead. Hunters only. For everyone
> else, dead is dead. We call the something the System. For seventeen years it never said a word.
>
> Three years ago it spoke. Every hunter woke to a window: *your Level is.* Now a hunter's rank can rise. Kill, and
> you grow. Do the Daily it sets you, and you grow. Three times a day it opens a door only you can enter, and if
> you come back out, you grow. And the higher you rise, the slower you age.
>
> So this is the world. The lights run on crystal cut from gates. Rent depends on how fast a hunter can reach your
> street. The money is the System's gold: one coin buys a bowl of noodles, thirty is a day's hard work, and an
> E-rank gets a hundred for a gate that may kill them. Guilds own the gates. The Bureau owns the queue. Most
> hunters are poor, a few are richer than countries, and all of them, as of three years ago, are looking at a
> ladder.
>
> You live in Carrow, six million people on a river mouth. You kindled. You stood in the Halfway House with your
> hand on the stone and it gave you a letter. You have a licence, a window, and a seat in a gate on Thursday.
>
> What do you do?
