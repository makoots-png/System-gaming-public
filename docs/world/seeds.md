# Seeds

## 1. Twenty hooks

Each hook names who asks, what the problem is, and a complication for the GM to reveal during play. Scale each
gate to the party's rank.

1. **Four gates on one day.** Sun-hee Marlowe of the Lantern Company overbid on six D gates, and four break on the
   same day. She pays the party to clear one without telling anyone. *Complication:* the gate is a double gate, and
   the second area is two ranks higher.
2. **The first offer.** A guild scout buys the party dinner and offers each member the model contract.
   *Complication:* it contains clauses 1, 3 and 4 of `careers.md`, section 4. A lawyer costs 120 gold an hour.
3. **A personal gate in a classroom.** A teacher who is a capped E-rank has a personal gate open in her
   classroom for a day. She asks the party what to do, because it is a colour she has never seen.
   *Complication:* it is a trial gate (`power.md`, section 5), and she is afraid to enter it.
4. **Potions for a clerk.** Pim Aldous, a Bureau seat clerk, moves the party up the queue for ten Health Potions.
   *Complication:* he gives the potions to a ward in Scarside. The patients are awakened children whose parents
   have not registered them.
5. **A gate held too long.** Helix is holding a C gate until its last day, two streets from a school. Reclaimers'
   Local 7 pays the party to clear it early and accept the fine of two Clears each. *Complication:* the boss is now
   one rank above the rank on the board.
6. **A guaranteed seat.** Kofi Stein of Redline sells the party a seat in a gate this month. *Complication:* an
   illegal crew is stripping that gate, and the rights holder's party arrives while the party is inside.
7. **A body in an unentered gate.** The party enters an E gate that the Bureau listed as unentered and finds a
   hunter who has been dead for three days. *Complication:* the dead hunter is registered as E and wears gear that only an A-rank could use. Someone found
   an uncapped hunter and killed them.
8. **The call-up.** The break siren sounds during another scene. Colonel Yun orders every licensed hunter in the
   district to Tidewall within twenty minutes. *Complication:* he records who does not come.
9. **How the Spotter finds gates.** Mikkel Strand has found his forty-second gate and asks the party to walk him
   home. *Complication:* a voice tells him where gates will open, and it is not the System's voice.
10. **Eleven missing crystals.** Ironwake's quartermaster hires the party to find eleven C crystals missing from
    the vault. *Complication:* someone used them on dead reclaimers, who are unawakened. Two of the eleven came
    back to life.
11. **A gate for Six.** The Aurelian hunter known as Six wants credit for one gate and needs a party that no
    camera crew recognises. *Complication:* Dax Verrault asks to join them and intends to fight.
12. **Day 6.** Twelve members of the Open Hand are sitting in a cellar in front of an unreported D gate. The
    cordon will storm the cellar in an hour. *Complication:* Baptiste Roux asks the party to enter the gate with
    him, unarmed.
13. **The councillor's son.** Councillor Baskin asks the party to judge her unregistered son's rank without a
    reading. *Complication:* he says his window reads "Cap: none".
14. **One hour.** A friend of the party dies in a gate, and nobody in the party carries a crystal of the
    friend's rank. The nearest one belongs to Rosalind Quill, twenty minutes away. *Complication:* she wants a
    service, and will not take gold.
15. **The gate that never closes.** Renzo Okabe hires the party to enter the B gate under the Scar and report what
    rank it reads from inside. *Complication:* the rank changes from hour to hour. It rises when an uncapped hunter is inside.
16. **A changed daily quest.** A member of the party wakes to a daily quest that says "Stand at the Quiet Mile at
    dusk." *Complication:* three strangers are there. Each is uncapped, and each received the same quest.
17. **The new bullet.** A B-rank is in the hunter hospital with a wound that potions do not close. The Plain Men
    shot him. *Complication:* the crystal in the bullet came from one gate, and Helix holds that gate's rights.
18. **An exit permit.** A C-rank healer wants to leave for a country that will pay her three times as much.
    Smuggling her costs ten Clears. She offers a year of her service in return. *Complication:* the Corps knows
    and is waiting for her to lead them to the smuggler.
19. **Sabine Okoro in Carrow.** The only hunter recorded as rising from D to S is clearing public gates alone.
    *Complication:* she asks the party, in private, what colour their gate was.
20. **03:14.** Arlo Finch's reactor core hums every night at the minute the gates opened. Last night the gate
    under the Scar made the same sound. *Complication:* the sound is the broadcast's signal (`story.md`, section 7).

## 2. Ten places

1. **The Halfway House, Ledger Row.** The Bureau's hall. It has a stone floor, a block of crystal for readings, a
   public gallery with a brass rail, and forty queue windows. Start a campaign here. Scouts stand at the left end
   of the rail, so that end is worn bright.
2. **Stonemarket.** Three covered streets behind the Helix works where dealers assay and sell crystal. Blue
   crystal dust covers every surface. Use it when the party sells a core or wants to learn prices. The exchange
   rings a bell when someone weighs an A core, and trading stops while people look.
3. **The Scar.** Two kilometres of ruins in the east of the city, left by the B break of Year 1. People live there
   in sheds and hold a market on Sundays. The gate that never closes is on the lowest level of a flooded car park.
   Use the Scar for any unlicensed business.
4. **The Quiet Mile.** A straight street in the Crown where a pack of D-rank monsters killed nine hundred people
   on day 7 of the Long Week. It is closed to traffic. Each victim has a small brass plate in the pavement. Use it
   for a private meeting. By custom hunters carry no weapons there.
5. **The Ninth Hour's tower.** Forty storeys of grey stone with no sign, above a public square. The square has
   the shortest response time in the city. A light on the top floor shows that Amara Nkemelu is at home, and
   residents check for it.
6. **Founders' Park at dawn.** The Ledger holds a service here at dawn for about two thousand people. Use it to introduce the Ledger or for a
   conversation that nobody will overhear. Several hundred capped hunters do the daily quest here together afterwards.
7. **The Last Lantern.** Redline's bar in Tidewall. A board behind the counter lists jobs and day rates. A healer
   sees patients in the back room. Use it when the party hires a fifth member or looks for work.
8. **The entrance to a gate.** A gate is a sheet of light somewhat taller than a door. Around it are a rope, a
   cordon tent and a board with the gate's rank and days left. The air within a metre of it is cold, and watches
   stop there. Most sessions include one.
9. **The hunter hospital car park.** It lies between the hunter hospital and the general hospital. Staff from
   both use it, and the Once-Born League holds a vigil there most nights. Use it for a scene about who can be
   raised and who cannot.
10. **Port Sarn.** A harbour city six hundred kilometres up the coast. Two million people lived there until day 2
    of Year 0. Many buildings still stand. The S gate is in the fish market. The Accord's inspectors patrol a
    fence around the whole city. Use it late in a campaign.

## 3. The primer

Read this to new players. It takes about three minutes.

> Twenty years ago, gates opened all over the world. They looked like doors made of light. Nothing came out of
> them at first.
>
> Two days later the largest gate broke. Monsters came out and destroyed a city of two million people in a day.
> Over the next five days every other gate broke too, in every country. About one person in twenty died in the
> three years that followed.
>
> Some people changed. They had a fever for three days and came out of it stronger and faster than anyone else.
> About one person in five hundred changes this way. They are called hunters, and nobody knows why it happens to
> them.
>
> New gates still open every day. Each gate has a rank from E to S and a deadline. An E gate breaks after seven
> days and an S gate after two. If hunters go in and kill the boss, the gate closes. If nobody does, the monsters
> come out.
>
> Something pays hunters for this work. It gives them gold and sells them things that nobody can make, such as a
> potion that closes a wound and a crystal that brings a dead hunter back to life if it is used within the hour.
> The crystal works only on hunters. People call this something the System.
>
> Everyone has the System. On the first night every person alive saw a window that only they could see. For most
> people it says "Resident, level 0" and does nothing. A hunter's window shows a level, quests and a rank. It
> also shows a cap. An E-rank's level stops at 9, and nothing they do will raise it. The System has never said
> what the game is for or why it stops people where it does.
>
> Cities run on crystal that crews cut out of cleared gates. Rent depends on how quickly hunters can reach your
> street. Everyone uses the System's gold as money. One gold buys a bowl of noodles, a labourer earns thirty a
> day, and an E-rank hunter earns a hundred for clearing a gate. Guilds control most of the gates, and a
> government office called the Bureau controls the rest. Most hunters are poor and a few are very rich.
>
> You live in Carrow, a port city of six million people. You became a hunter recently. The Bureau has given you
> a rank and a licence. Your window says level 1 and tells you where you will stop. You have a seat in a gate on
> Thursday.
>
> What do you do?
