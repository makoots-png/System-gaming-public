# History: from Day Zero to Year 20

The world is real Earth. The campaign setting is San Francisco twenty years after the first gate opened in London.
London and England were lost to monsters on day 7, New York was nuked to stop a break, and major cities around the
world endured the second wave. A GM who wants the gates over their own town can move the local setting while keeping
this global history.

## 1. The calendar

People count years from the night the first gate opened in London. That day is Day Zero, the first day of Year 0. The
present is Year 20. Set Day Zero twenty years before your table's present, and everyday technology matches ours except
where this file says otherwise.

Two dates matter most:

- **Day Zero, Year 0.** At 03:14 UTC the first gate opens in London, and every person alive sees a System window.
- **Year 3.** States sign the San Francisco Accord, which sets the law on gates and hunters.

Every human has had a System window since the night the first gate opened. For ordinary people it reads
`"Resident, level 0"` and, since the first hunter awakened, gives access to the Resident Store for everyday goods and
essentials. A hunter's level is capped at the top of the rank they awaken with, and every law, contract and guild in
this file assumes that. `power.md`, section 5, covers the exception.

## 2. Day Zero, the Long Week, and the second wave

At 03:14 UTC on Day Zero a single gate opened in London: a gaping maw thousands of feet wide, the largest gate Earth
has seen and the only **SS** gate ever recorded. At the same minute every person alive saw a private window reading
`"Resident, level 0"`. The world stopped. People argued over aliens, secret weapons and divine judgment.

For seven days — **the Long Week** — nothing came out. World governments put a committee together (the body that later
grew into the San Francisco Accord) and tried to probe the rift. Cameras and robots shut down halfway through the gate,
the first proof that electronics die at a gate's threshold. Teams of elite special operators went in with the best
weapons on Earth and never came back. Governments deliberated over more troops, explosives and even nuclear strikes,
and did nothing decisive before the seventh day ended.

**Day 7: the fall of London.** Seven days after opening, London's SS gate broke open on live broadcast. The first
monster out was a mythic land dragon towering over the skyscrapers. Everything inside the SS dungeon funnelled out into
Britain, and **the gate closed behind them** — a broken gate always closes once its monsters emerge, and nothing goes
back in. Over the next five days everyone in London and across England who did not escape by boat or plane died. Over
97% of the population died. England remains under monster control to this day, with the London dragon holding the
island and Accord sea and air patrols watching the Channel.

**The second wave.** After London fell, gates began opening in major cities around the world: Shanghai, San Francisco,
Tokyo, Melbourne, Madrid, New York and hundreds more. Every gate followed the same rule: **always seven days after it
opened, if nobody cleared it, it broke**, its monsters funnelled out and the gate closed behind them.

**Conventional weapons and New York.** Armies fought what came out and learned the limits of mundane firepower:
small-arms fire kills small monsters, tank shells and missiles kill medium ones, and nothing short of a nuclear strike
could halt the heaviest breaks. When a large-ish gate broke in New York, the United States nuked the city to stop the
creatures from overrunning the eastern seaboard.

**The first hunters and the Resident Store.** Around the same time as the second wave, people began falling into a
three-day fever and walking out stronger and faster than any unawakened human — about one person in five hundred.
Hunters proved able to fight high-rank monsters and, by entering a gate and killing its boss before day 7, close the
gate before it broke. As soon as the first hunter awakened, the Resident window's shop — the **Resident Store** —
switched on worldwide, offering everyday food and essentials for System gold.

## 3. The Break Years, Year 0 to Year 3

The second wave was only the start. Over the next three years the rate climbed steeply into hundreds of thousands of
new gates worldwide every month (reaching roughly 260 a month in a port of San Francisco's size by Year 5, and 400 a
month by Year 20 with the 3% Tide). For three years states could not clear them fast enough.

- **Deaths and early hunger.** About one person in twenty died in those three years. Many died in city breaks and
  military cordons; the rest died of hunger and untreated illness during Year 0 and Year 1 when supply chains failed,
  before hunters had cleared enough gates for System gold to reach ordinary pockets and unlock the Resident Store's
  food supply. Once hunter gold circulated into everyday wages, the Store ended urban famine and began turning horror
  into comfort.
- **Armies.** Bullets kill small (E and D) monsters, tank shells and missiles kill medium (C) monsters, and a nuke
  stopped a large-ish break in New York at an unbearable cost. Only hunters can enter a gate to close it before day 7
  or stop high-rank breaks without levelling a city.
- **Money and the two shops.** Hunters left cleared gates with System gold and spent it in the hunter Shop on
  mana-imbued potions, gear and crystals. Residents spent the gold they earned in wages at the Resident Store on
  everyday food and essentials, while hiring licensed E-rank runners to buy Health Potions and Elixirs from the hunter
  shelf. By Year 1 a Shop potion that closed a wound in seconds was worth more than any banknote, and both shops took
  only gold.
- **Borders.** Every state stopped its hunters from leaving.
- **The countryside.** A gate that nobody finds for seven days breaks. A city finds a gate within an hour, and a
  village may not find one for days. Within three years most rural people had moved into surviving cities and defended
  hubs, accelerating the shift from traditional farming to Store food.
- **Religion.** Some people decided the gates were a judgment. Others decided that the Shop proved someone was
  keeping accounts. Both beliefs have organised followers today (`factions.md`, section 5).

Power stations and the internet kept working through most of the Break Years, and most people kept going to work.

## 4. Year 3 to Year 8: the rules are written

**The San Francisco Accord, Year 3.** Growing out of the committee governments assembled during the Long Week, ninety
states signed the Accord in San Francisco, the largest port on its coast that was still open. It has five rules, and
almost every country still follows them:

1. The state reads every gate within a day of finding it and rates it from E to S by the amount of mana in it.
   London's Day Zero gate was never read before it broke; the Bureaus class it **SS**, a class of one. Every standard
   gate breaks seven days after opening.
2. Every awakened person registers within 72 hours and is read the same way. The rank is public.
3. A gate belongs to the state where it opens. The state may sell the right to clear it and take what is inside.
4. The state may order any registered hunter to a break anywhere in the country. The hunter may not refuse.
5. No state sends hunters to fight another state's people. States kept this rule for eight years.

**The Bureaus.** Each state set up a Gate Bureau to read gates, register hunters and sell gate rights. A Bureau
clerk earns 460 gold a month and decides which party gets which gate.

**The guilds.** Parties that had fought together in the Break Years wrote the first guild charters in Year 2. They
wanted the first claim on gates, a share of the crystal cut out of them, and payments to the families of members
who died. Guilds still do those three things, and control most gates by buying rights from the Bureau.

**Crystal power, Year 4.** The crystal in a gate's walls burns clean and very hot. Helix Manaworks powered San
Francisco from one crystal reactor in Year 4. By Year 8 every surviving city ran on crystal. From then on a cleared
gate was worth money to whoever cut the crystal out before day 7 closed it.

**Gold.** By Year 8 shops in every country priced goods in gold (`economy.md`, section 2).

**The first public raising, Year 9.** The Shop had sold Resurrection Crystals since Year 0, at a price almost
nobody could pay. In Year 9 a guild raised a dead A-rank hunter on live television. That night a crowd gathered
outside the hospital and asked why a crystal works only on hunters. Nobody has answered. The crowd's organisers
founded the Once-Born League.

## 5. Year 8 to Year 20

- **Year 11, the Strait War.** Two states fought for nine days over a strait and the gates on its coast. Both sent
  hunters against soldiers, which broke the Accord's fifth rule. On the ninth day the S-ranks of both sides met on
  a tanker in the strait. They agreed that no S-rank would fight in a war between states and that the others would
  kill any S-rank who did. This agreement is called the Abstention. It was never written down, and S-ranks have
  kept it.
- **Year 14, the Hollowing.** An S gate opened above a city on another continent while that state's two S-ranks
  were abroad. No party cleared it in time; it broke after seven days, closed behind its monsters, and the city was
  lost. Since then every large city has paid an S-rank to live in it or has tried to.
- **Year 19, Sabine Okoro's second reading.** Sabine Okoro was read as D rank at seventeen. At twenty-eight she
  asked for a new reading and read as S. The Bureau recorded it as a re-awakening, which is a second awakening that
  raises a hunter's rank once. Fewer than thirty are on record worldwide, and none before hers rose more than one
  rank.
- **The Tide.** The number of new gates has grown about three percent a year since Year 5. Nobody knows the cause.
  At that rate the number doubles by Year 43.

## 6. The world in Year 20

The examples are from San Francisco, a port city of 800,000 on a bay.

**The mood: a boom built on a catastrophe.** Twenty years on, most people treat the gates as more of a boon than a
horror. Entire cities run on cheap, clean crystal power. Anyone with a few bits of gold buys fresh vegetables, cup
noodles or gourmet Michelin-level meals straight out of the Resident Store — better than anything from before the
gates — so ordinary grocery stores and restaurants survive only as novelties or monster-meat specialty kitchens.
Traditional farming and rural food convoys have largely vanished. Break sirens, Doorwatch boards and household
go-bags are still everywhere, treated like earthquake kits: routine background furniture in a city that thinks it has
won.

**A city.** Every map shows three zones. In a Green zone a ranked party reaches any address within ten minutes. The
Crown and Ledger Row are Green. In an Amber zone the party arrives within forty minutes. Most of San Francisco is
Amber. In a Red zone the Bureau promises no time. Scarside is Red. It lies along the Scar, two kilometres of ruins
left by a B break in Year 1 (that gate closed once its monsters funnelled out).

Every fourth lamp post has a siren. A steady tone means a gate has been found, and a rising tone means a break.
Every tram stop has a board that lists the district's open gates, with each gate's rank and the days left until day 7.
San Francisco has 1,600 hunters. About half have a regular seat in world gates. The rest get one gate in four months
and live on other jobs, crafting professions, or running hunter-shelf medicine from the Shop.

**A border.** Manufactured goods and raw crystal move by sea and escorted rail. A hunter needs an exit permit from
their own Bureau to leave the country, and the Bureau rarely gives one to a hunter above C rank. No state has ever
stopped an S-rank at a border.

**A job.** The gates created these jobs:

- A reclaimer cuts crystal and carcasses out of a cleared gate. The crew shares a quarter of the sale price.
- A runner buys hunter-shelf medicine (Health Potions and Elixirs) from the Shop for Residents, whose own store sells
  everyday goods and essentials only.
- An assessor reads gates and people.
- A spotter walks a district and looks for new gates.
- A cordon soldier guards a gate or a break.

Older jobs changed too. A builder sets prices by the block's response time. Novelty chefs and licensed monster-meat
butchers cook what the Store does not sell. A teacher runs a break drill every month.

**A school.** Children practise the break drill from age four. At fourteen every child is read at a Bureau hall,
and almost none have awakened. A person can awaken at any age, most often between fourteen and thirty. A child who
awakens usually leaves school within a week for a hunter academy that a guild pays for.

**A hospital.** A trauma ward stocks Health Potions, and few patients now die of blood loss. Cancer wards are full,
because an Elixir of Life cures only some cancers and a Resident can take one a week. Hunters use a separate hospital,
where potions work at full strength and where guild clerks handle debt revives and pre-bought Resurrection Crystals.
In San Francisco the two hospitals share a car park, and the Once-Born League holds a vigil there most nights.

**An army.** A state has two forces. The cordon is unawakened infantry backed by armour and missiles. It surrounds a
gate or a break, moves civilians out, kills small monsters with rifles and engages medium ones with heavy weapons.
The Hunter Corps is a few hundred ranked soldiers. It also has the legal right to call up every registered hunter in
the country. Neither force has fought another state since the Strait War.

**A family.** When a child awakens, the guild's signing bonus can pay off the family's mortgage, and the child
starts work in gates that the parents cannot enter. A B-rank ages one year in every two and a half, so a B-rank who
marries an unawakened person outlives them by about seventy years. More hunters marry other hunters each year for
that reason. A hunter's will takes effect an hour after death, when a crystal can no longer raise them.

## 7. What ordinary people need and fear

An unawakened household needs these things:

- A home with a short response time.
- Break cover, because a bank will not lend without it.
- A runner it trusts for Health Potions and Elixirs (everyday food and goods come straight from the Resident Store).
- A go-bag for each person.

Most families also want one hunter among their relatives, for the income and for access to the hunter Shop.

People fear these things, roughly in this order:

1. **An unfound gate.** A gate in a cellar or a drain that nobody reports breaks after seven days. The state
   pays spotters, and neighbours find most gates. The Bureau pays a finder's fee of a tenth of the gate's Clear. A
   person who hides a gate goes to prison.
2. **A gate held too long.** A rights holder earns more by waiting until the seventh day (`economy.md`, section 8).
   The gate may be three streets from a school.
3. **An S gate when no S-rank is in the city — or another SS gate.** The Hollowing showed what an uncleared S gate
   does; London showed what an SS gate does. Nobody has a plan for a second SS gate, or for anything higher.
4. **Hunters.** A C-rank can kill everyone in a room in seconds, and no ordinary prison holds a B-rank. Most
   hunters are careful. Every district has had a case of one who was not.
5. **Rising rent in Green zones.** Rents follow response times, so families on a median wage are moving out to
   Amber.
6. **S-ranks.** No court or army can force an S-rank to do anything. Each city depends on the goodwill of the few it has.

Many parents hope a child will awaken, for the income. Many of the same parents fear it, because hunters die
young.
