# What I read and what I took from it

For this folder I ran web searches and read the articles they returned. I combined those with what I already knew
of the works. I did not watch any show or reread any book. The search results on gate stories were mostly fan
wikis and list articles about one series, so the lessons in section 1 rely more on prior knowledge than the others
do. The owner has since ruled that the setting is Solo Leveling's world, so the game's built-in names from that series stay. The places, factions and people in this folder are new.

## 1. Gate and hunter stories

I read explainers of the best-known gate series' guilds, ranks and hunter association
([Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-hunters-guilds-explained),
[Sportskeeda on ranks](https://sportskeeda.com/anime/solo-leveling-s-hunter-ranking-system-explained),
[TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Anime/SoloLeveling)), a reader's overview of
[the genre](https://payhip.com/thekhaoticauthorzone/blog/traversing-the-universe/what-is-the-hunterverse), and a
[list of gate manhwa](https://myanimelist.net/stacks/3122). From prior knowledge I drew on other Korean gate and
tower serials.

| What the genre does | What I used | What I changed or left out |
|---|---|---|
| An association measures gates and hunters and sells gate rights to guilds | The Bureau, the reading and the auction | I gave the Bureau low-paid clerks with real power over seats, which creates bribery |
| Rank is fixed at awakening, and only the protagonist can level | By the owner's ruling every hunter levels up to a cap set by rank, and a trial gate removes the cap | I added a very few other hunters who are uncapped, as a secret for the GM |
| In Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint, higher beings watch the scenarios and pay the people in them | The audience, the sponsors and the scenarios of `story.md`, at the owner's suggestion | The gold that every Clear pays is the audience's money, and the monsters are the casts of earlier seasons. Those are mine |
| Hunters earn from monster cores and carcasses | The harvest | I split the income. The System's gold goes to the hunters and the harvest goes to the rights holder. This gives guilds a reason to exist |
| A gate that is left alone breaks | Every gate breaks seven days after it opens and closes behind its monsters | Used for Day Zero's Long Week in London and every gate since |
| S-ranks act as national weapons | The Abstention, and cities that pay an S-rank to live there | I left out power tiers above S |

## 2. Dungeon economies

I read a guide to a series about a city built on a dungeon, its guild and its magic stones
([Honey's Anime](https://honeysanime.com/danmachi-explained)). From prior knowledge I drew on a manga about food and
supply inside a dungeon, and on a Soviet novel about scavengers who take objects out of an alien zone.

I used four ideas:

- Every trade in the city depends on the dungeon.
- A small company falls into debt to a large one. In this setting the debt is for a Resurrection Crystal.
- The same goods pass through a legal institute and an illegal market. Here those are Helix, the Bureau's exchange
  and the Stair.
- The people who carry goods out matter as much as the fighters. Here they are the reclaimers.

## 3. Superhero societies

I read articles on a series about licensed heroes and on a satire of corporate superheroes
([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/superhero-anime-my-hero-academia-is-more-like-the-boys-than-shonen-anime-cape-comics/1100-6512307/),
[No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/boys-whats-wrong-superhero-culture),
[Anime Corner](https://animecorner.me/why-the-my-hero-academia-world-is-so-compelling/),
[TV Tropes](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/CapePunk)). From prior knowledge I drew on a web serial
about a government agency for people with powers, an anthology series about a virus that gives powers, and a manga
in which a hunter's licence is an exam.

I used four ideas:

- A public ranking rewards visible work more than useful work. Here it is the Hundred.
- A company presents heroes as a product while a hidden team does the work. Here it is Aurelian.
- Registration is the first legal fact of a powered person's life. Here it is the 72-hour rule.
- People without powers organise politically and have a real grievance. Here they are the Once-Born League.

I added one rule of my own. No camera works inside a gate, so a hunter's account of a raid needs a witness, and
the System's titles are the only record that nobody can alter.

## 4. How games build worlds

I read [World Anvil on the factions of a post-war RPG series](https://www.worldanvil.com/academy/worldbuilding-factions-5-secrets-from-fallout-lore),
[a former lead writer on designing a setting like a tabletop book](https://www.gamesradar.com/former-dragon-age-lead-explains-how-writing-lore-books-led-him-to-design-settings-like-a-tabletop-rpg-book),
[an RPG studio on its design bible](https://www.pcgamesn.com/pillars-of-eternity-2/obsidian-interview),
[the making of a detective RPG](https://www.gamesradar.com/uk/the-making-of-disco-elysium-how-zaum-created-one-of-the-most-original-rpgs-of-the-decade/)
and [an article on its narrative design](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/player-agency-politics-and-narrative-design-in-disco-elysium-).

| Lesson | Where the folder applies it |
|---|---|
| Set the story long enough after the disaster for factions to form | The present is Year 20. Guilds date from Year 2 and the Accord from Year 3 |
| Give each faction a long-term aim, a short-term aim and an internal disagreement | Every entry in `factions.md` has all three |
| Write a setting that a GM can run from | Every named person has a hook, and `seeds.md` has hooks, places and a primer |
| Describe one place in depth | San Francisco has six districts and about forty named people |
| Expect the document to change | `README.md` records the owner's five rulings, which changed four files |
| Give the GM the answer and the order in which to reveal it | `story.md` |

## 5. How game economies are anchored and where they break

I read about [sources and sinks of currency](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-f-words-of-mmos-faucets),
[stagnation in MMO economies](https://massivelyop.com/2015/09/13/mmo-mechanics-economic-stagnation-in-mmo-economies/),
[one space MMO's currency flows](https://www.engadget.com/2010-10-24-eve-evolved-isk-sinks-and-faucets.html),
[failed MMO economies](https://massivelyop.com/2023/08/10/massively-overthinking-rescuing-the-worst-economies-in-the-mmo-genre/)
and [virtual economies in general](https://publish.obsidian.md/dirksonguer/Virtual%20World%20Economies). For
tabletop pricing I read [a historian on fantasy coinage](https://acoup.blog/2025/01/03/collections-coinage-and-the-tyranny-of-fantasy-gold),
[Gnome Stew on a labourer's weekly budget](https://gnomestew.com/mo-gold-mo-problems/),
[an estimate of a gold piece's value](https://medium.com/@Swizzler/what-is-a-dungeons-and-dragons-gold-piece-worth-in-modern-dollars-fcd7670b285b)
and [a forum thread that prices from wages](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=7789519).

| Lesson | Where the folder applies it |
|---|---|
| Fix an unskilled day's wage first, then derive food and rent from it | `economy.md`, section 1 |
| A currency's supply is what enters minus what is destroyed. Trades between players destroy nothing | Section 2 |
| A vendor that buys and sells at fixed prices sets the lowest and highest price in the player market | Section 3 |
| An economy inflates when high-level players have nothing to spend on | Section 15, row 5: the game has no potion above epic |
| An economy breaks when players can buy the fastest progress | Section 15, row 1: this was a risk while every hunter in a gate took the whole XP. The owner has ruled that XP is split |
| Adventurers' income and ordinary wages match only if the designer makes them match | Section 14 compares four kinds of hunter income with rent, gear and a crystal |
| Publish the numbers so that others can rerun them | `prices.ts` |
