# What I read and what I took from it

How to read this page: "read" means web searches and the articles they returned, in this session, laid over what I
already knew of the works. I watched no show and reread no book for it. The search results on the genre were thin
(fan wikis and listicles, one series far more than the rest), so the genre lessons lean on prior knowledge and
are marked as such. Nothing in the world bible takes a name, character, place or line from any work below.

## 1. Gate and hunter stories

Read: explainers of the best-known gate series' guilds, ranks and association
([Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-hunters-guilds-explained),
[Sportskeeda on ranks](https://sportskeeda.com/anime/solo-leveling-s-hunter-ranking-system-explained),
[TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Anime/SoloLeveling)), a reader's overview of the
["hunterverse"](https://payhip.com/thekhaoticauthorzone/blog/traversing-the-universe/what-is-the-hunterverse), and a
[list of gate manhwa](https://myanimelist.net/stacks/3122). From prior knowledge: the wider run of Korean gate and
tower serials, and the "regression" and "constellation" variants.

| The genre does | I took | I left |
|---|---|---|
| An association measures gates and hunters and sells gate rights to guilds | The Bureau, the Reading, the auction. Then asked what a clerk on 1,100 a month does with that power | The association as plain background |
| Rank is fixed at awakening; one protagonist alone can level | The opposite, forced by the game: everyone levels. So the fixed world became the first seventeen years, and levelling is a three-year-old shock (the Speaking) | The single chosen leveller |
| Monster cores and carcasses are the hunter's income | Split in two: the System's gold to the hunters, the harvest to whoever holds the rights. That split is what guilds are | Stones as loot in the party's pockets |
| Breaks happen when a gate is left | The game's own break days turned into Day Zero: the S gate broke first | |
| S-ranks as national weapons | The Abstention, and cities paying an S-rank to live there | National-level power tiers above S |

## 2. Dungeon economies

Read: guides to a dungeon-city series' guild, stones and debts
([Honey's Anime](https://honeysanime.com/danmachi-explained)). From prior knowledge: a dungeon-ecology manga where
food and supply decide how deep a party goes, and a Soviet novel of scavengers in an alien zone, whose finds feed
an institute, a black market and a town.

Took: a city whose every trade faces the dungeon; debt as the thing that binds a small company to a large one
(crystal debt); the zone novel's shape of legal institute, illegal fence and the same goods passing between them
(Helix, the exchange, the Stair); and the idea that the people who carry things out matter as much as the people
who fight (reclaimers).

## 3. Superhero societies

Read: pieces on a licensed-hero school series and a corporate-superhero satire
([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/superhero-anime-my-hero-academia-is-more-like-the-boys-than-shonen-anime-cape-comics/1100-6512307/),
[No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/boys-whats-wrong-superhero-culture),
[Anime Corner](https://animecorner.me/why-the-my-hero-academia-world-is-so-compelling/),
[TV Tropes on cape punk](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/CapePunk)). From prior knowledge: a web
serial about a parahuman bureaucracy, a shared-world anthology of a virus that makes aces and jokers, and a manga
whose hunter licence is an exam.

Took: a public ranking that rewards being seen more than being useful (the Hundred); heroes as a company's product
with a hidden team doing the work (Aurelian's second line); registration as the first political fact of a powered
person's life (the 72 hours); and the resentment of the unpowered as a movement with a case, not a mob (the
Once-Born League). The change I made: nobody can film a gate, so fame needs a witness, and the System's titles
became the one thing that cannot be spun.

## 4. How games build worlds

Read: [World Anvil on faction lessons from a post-war RPG series](https://www.worldanvil.com/academy/worldbuilding-factions-5-secrets-from-fallout-lore),
a [former lead writer on designing a setting like a tabletop book](https://www.gamesradar.com/former-dragon-age-lead-explains-how-writing-lore-books-led-him-to-design-settings-like-a-tabletop-rpg-book),
an [RPG studio on its design bible as a living wiki](https://www.pcgamesn.com/pillars-of-eternity-2/obsidian-interview),
[the making of a detective RPG](https://www.gamesradar.com/uk/the-making-of-disco-elysium-how-zaum-created-one-of-the-most-original-rpgs-of-the-decade/)
and [its narrative design](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/player-agency-politics-and-narrative-design-in-disco-elysium-).

| Lesson | Where it is in the bible |
|---|---|
| Set the story long enough after the catastrophe for factions to have formed | Twenty years, with charters in Year 2 and an Accord in Year 3 |
| A faction is a product of its world, with a long aim, a near aim, and a quarrel inside | Every entry of `factions.md` has all three |
| Write the setting as a book a GM runs from, not a wiki | Each file ends in things to use: hooks on every figure, a table of who hates whom, the primer |
| Deep beats broad: one city with reasons to return | Carrow, six districts, the same forty people |
| A bible is not immutable | Five truths about the System and a list of what is the owner's to rule |
| Leave the central mystery open and seed signs of it | `power.md` section 8 |

## 5. How game economies are anchored, and where they break

Read: [faucets and sinks](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-f-words-of-mmos-faucets),
[stagnation in MMO economies](https://massivelyop.com/2015/09/13/mmo-mechanics-economic-stagnation-in-mmo-economies/),
[a space MMO's sinks and faucets](https://www.engadget.com/2010-10-24-eve-evolved-isk-sinks-and-faucets.html),
[the worst MMO economies](https://massivelyop.com/2023/08/10/massively-overthinking-rescuing-the-worst-economies-in-the-mmo-genre/),
[notes on virtual economies](https://publish.obsidian.md/dirksonguer/Virtual%20World%20Economies); and for tabletop
anchors, [a historian on fantasy coinage](https://acoup.blog/2025/01/03/collections-coinage-and-the-tyranny-of-fantasy-gold),
[Gnome Stew on a labourer's week](https://gnomestew.com/mo-gold-mo-problems/),
[what a gold piece is worth](https://medium.com/@Swizzler/what-is-a-dungeons-and-dragons-gold-piece-worth-in-modern-dollars-fcd7670b285b)
and [a forum's wage-first method](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=7789519).

| Lesson | Where it is in the bible |
|---|---|
| Anchor money to an unskilled day's wage first, then back into food and rent | `economy.md` section 1: a labourer's day is 0.3 of an E Clear, a meal is one gold |
| A currency is its faucets and sinks; transfers between players remove nothing | Section 2: the System as central bank, the Shop as the only drain, and ministries that watch potion sales |
| A vendor that buys and sells at fixed prices sets a floor and a ceiling for the player market | Section 3: the Shop's band, and the fences, stalls and runners who live in it |
| Economies break where a faucet has no matching sink at the top | Section 15, row 5: no potion above epic, so A and S stop spending |
| Economies break where the fastest progress can be bought | Section 15, row 1: whole XP to everyone inside is a seat market |
| The adventuring economy never matches the peasant one unless you make it | Section 14: four ways of working against rent, gear and a crystal, rank by rank |
| Publish the numbers and let them be rerun | `prices.ts` |
