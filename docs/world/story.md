# The story: what the game is, and how it ends

This file is for the GM only. It gives the truth behind the System, the campaign in six acts, and the story arcs
that carry the horror. The owner set the outline on Nov 20: every human has the System, nobody knows why, the players
grow strong, outgrow the game, fight what the game was preparing them for, and then kill the people who run it.
The tone is bleak. The players should enjoy getting stronger and hate what getting stronger shows them.

The structure borrows one idea from Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint, at the owner's suggestion: the game has an
audience. Everything else here is new.

## 1. The truth

Tell the players none of this. Each act reveals one part.

**The game is a broadcast.** A group called the Hosts runs it. They are not gods. They are the staff of a
business, and Earth is its current season. They have run the same game on other worlds before Earth.

**The audience pays, and their payment is the gold.** Viewers from many worlds watch hunters clear gates. The gold
a Clear pays is the viewers' money, less the Hosts' cut. The Shop is the Hosts' store, and every potion bought
there returns gold to them. Earth rebuilt its economy on tips from people who watch its citizens die.

**The caps are casting.** A broadcast needs a few leads and a large number of people who can be killed. The Hosts
assign each human a rank at awakening and cap the level at that rank. A person who never awakens is a Resident,
capped at level 0, and exists in the game to be in danger. A trial gate removes a cap. The Hosts open one when
they want to promote a hunter to the lead cast.

**The monsters are the casts of earlier seasons.** When a world loses its season, the Hosts keep its people. They
strip their windows, lock their bodies into monster forms, and place them in gates as the opposition for the next
world. An E-rank goblin warren is a street of some earlier world's Residents. A boss is one of its S-ranks. Most
no longer remember. Some do.

**The game does prepare its players for something real.** The Hosts call it the Finale. It is an enemy that
destroys worlds, and it reaches every world the Hosts broadcast from, because the Hosts lead it there. A season
ends when it arrives. The Hosts sell the Finale as the last episode. A world that wins is released. No world has
won.

**The Hosts could have armed every world and warned it.** They chose to sell tickets. That is the reason to kill
them.

## 2. What every human sees

Every person on Earth has had a window since 03:14 UTC on Day Zero. This is public knowledge, and it is the
strangest ordinary fact in the world.

| | A Resident (never awakened) | A hunter |
|---|---|---|
| Window | Name, "Resident", level 0, and a line that reads "Cap: 0" | Name, job, rank, level, stats, quests, and a line that reads "Cap:" with the top level of the rank |
| Quests | Only during a break nearby. The usual one is "Survive: 10:00" | A daily quest, the System's quests, personal gates |
| Rewards | Nothing a Resident can see | XP up to the cap, gold, titles |
| The Shop and Inventory | No | Yes |

Three facts follow, and people in the world have noticed the first two:

- **Awakening is a Resident quest.** A Resident who completes "Survive" during a break has about one chance in
  sixty of awakening within the year. This is why awakening is more common near breaks (`power.md`, section 1).
  Some people walk toward sirens on purpose.
- **A capped hunter still gets the daily quest.** A hunter at their cap gains nothing from it and is still
  Penalized for missing it. Hunters at their cap do a hundred push-ups every morning for no reward, for life.
- **The window counts something for Residents too.** A Resident's window has one more line that is blank until
  act 2 (section 4).

## 3. Death costs more than gold

A Resurrection Crystal costs ten Clears of the hunter's rank and must be used within the hour. The owner wants the
bill to be heavy. The gold is the smaller part of it.

**The System takes collateral.** When a crystal raises a hunter, the window shows one line, "Collateral taken", and
does not say what. The hunter finds out later. Use this table, or choose.

| Raising | What the System takes |
|---|---|
| First | A memory the hunter valued. Other people still remember it. |
| Second | Other people's memory of one thing the hunter did. The record of it remains, and nobody recalls it. |
| Third | One person forgets the hunter completely. The System chooses a person the hunter loves. |
| Fourth | A year of the hunter's life, taken at once. The hunter looks a year older. |
| Fifth and after | The hunter's own choice between the four above, with a ten-second timer. |

In act 4 the players learn where the collateral goes. The Hosts sell the memories to viewers as keepsakes.

**A suggested rule for the lead and the owner.** A raising could also cost the hunter one level, which can drop a
hunter below a rank line. This is a rule change and is not in the game. It would make the gold price the smaller
cost, as the story does.

## 4. The campaign in six acts

Each act matches a rank. The party's rank decides which act they are in.

### Act 1, ranks E and D: the job

The party works. They queue at the Bureau, clear small gates, pay entry fees and learn what a potion costs. They
are capped like everyone, at level 9, and believe they always will be.

- **What they enjoy:** the first levels, the first rare drop, a guild's offer.
- **What they see:** things in E gates that run from them. A monster that covers a smaller one. A boss that drops
  a child's shoe. A capped D-rank in his fifties who does his daily quest crying.
- **The bill:** the first death in the party, the crystal debt, and "Collateral taken".
- **How it ends:** the party reaches level 9. A personal gate opens that is the wrong colour. This is the trial
  gate.

### Act 2, rank C: the cap

The trial gate is built from the party's own memories of the worst day of their lives, and it is populated by
the people who were there. To pass, each hunter must finish the day again and change nothing. A hunter who tries
to save someone fails the trial and leaves it capped.

When the party leaves, their windows read "Cap: none". They also show a number that was not there before:
"Viewers", with a count in the low hundreds.

- **What they enjoy:** levels past 9. They are stronger than anyone of their registered rank and nobody knows.
- **What they see:** the Residents' blank line fills in for anyone uncapped. It reads "Value", with a number. It
  is the XP a monster receives for killing that person. Children are worth more.
- **What changes in the world:** guilds, the Bureau and the Ledger begin to notice the party (`power.md`,
  section 4).
- **How it ends:** a viewer sends the first message. It is a tip of 500 gold and four words: "Do the school
  next."

### Act 3, rank B: the cast

With uncapped hunters on Earth, the season enters its second half, and the System speaks to everyone for the
first time. It issues **scenarios**: quests for a whole city, with a timer and a penalty.

- **What they enjoy:** sponsors. A viewer who favours a hunter sends gold, items and private quests. The party
  can become rich.
- **What they see:** scenarios are written to be watched. "Carrow: clear twelve gates in three days. Penalty: one
  district loses Shop access." "Scarside: the district's population must fall by 400 before dawn. Method open."
  The party caused this by passing the trial.
- **The choice of the act:** sponsors pay more for cruelty. A hunter can refuse and fall behind, or accept and
  rise.
- **How it ends:** the party meets another uncapped hunter (Sabine Okoro or Marek Osei) who has kept a list of
  every scenario from another city. The list shows that scenarios repeat. They are a script.

### Act 4, rank A: the other seasons

A double gate leads to a place that is not a dungeon. It is a ruined city under a different sky, and the boss
speaks.

- **What they learn:** the boss was the strongest hunter of her world. Her world lost its season. Every monster
  the party has killed was one of her people. She asks them to kill her and tells them how many seasons there
  have been.
- **What they see:** a Shop terminal in the ruins, still open, selling items labelled "Earth, season
  keepsakes". The party's collateral is on the list.
- **What they enjoy:** her kit. She can be extracted as a shadow, and she consents.
- **How it ends:** the party knows what the Finale is and that it is close. The Tide, the three percent more
  gates each year, is the countdown.

### Act 5, rank S: the Finale

The enemy arrives through Port Sarn's gate, which has stood open since day 2 for this purpose. It is the same
enemy that every earlier season lost to.

- **What it is:** the GM chooses. The plain choice is a world-eating thing that the Hosts found long ago and
  learned to steer. The crueller choice is the cast of the first season, who won, were not released, and have
  been used as the Finale ever since.
- **What the party must do:** unite hunters who have every reason to hate them, and win a fight that the game
  designed to be lost on camera.
- **The cost:** Carrow, or most of it. The Finale should take a place the players care about.
- **How it ends:** Earth wins. The System displays "Season complete. Thank you for playing." Nothing else
  changes, and no one is released. The viewer count is the highest it has ever been.

### Act 6, past S: the control room

There is no rank past S, and the party's levels keep rising. A last gate opens for them alone. Behind it is the
place the Hosts work.

- **What they find:** offices. A schedule board with the next world on it. A ratings chart. Hosts who are bored,
  tired and frightened of their own employers. One offers the party a contract as next season's Finale.
- **What they do:** kill the Hosts. This should be a slaughter and should not feel clean. Some Hosts beg, and
  one of them designed the potion that saved a party member's life in act 1.
- **The last choice:** the controls are there. If the party turns the game off, every gate closes and every
  monster in every gate dies with it. Gold stops, the Shop closes, potions and crystals are gone, and every
  capped hunter keeps their cap for ever. If they leave it on, the game continues and they run it.

## 5. Story arcs

Each is a set of sessions that can run inside the act named. Each gives the party something they want and shows
them something they do not.

| Arc | Act | What happens | What the party gains | What it shows them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The quiet warren | 1 | An E gate where nothing fights back. The monsters have barricaded a room | An easy Clear | The room holds their young. The Clear does not register until the room is empty |
| The crying man | 1 | A capped D-rank asks the party to do his daily quest's run with him. He has done it eleven thousand times | A friend and a guide to the Bureau | What a cap does to a person over thirty years |
| Collateral | 1 | A raised party member's mother does not know them | The hunter is alive | Section 3 |
| Survive: 10:00 | 1 or 2 | A break in Saltmarket. Every Resident's window shows a timer | XP and a break clip that makes them known | Residents who finish the timer cheering, and a man pushing his daughter back toward the monsters for a second chance at awakening |
| The wrong-coloured gate | 2 | The trial | No cap | Each hunter leaves someone to die a second time |
| Value | 2 | The party can now read a number on every Resident | Nothing | The System has priced everyone they know. A broker in the Stair learns they can read it and offers to pay for a list |
| Do the school next | 2 | The first viewer message, then more. Requests come with gold attached | A lot of gold | Someone is watching, and likes it |
| The Scarside count | 3 | A scenario requires 400 deaths in one district by dawn | A way to save most of them, at a price | Neighbours choosing who. The Plain Men and a guild both arrive to "help" |
| The sponsor | 3 | One viewer adopts a party member and is generous for months | The best gear of the campaign | The sponsor's private quest: "Let the healer die. I want to see what you do." |
| The script | 3 | Okoro's list of scenarios from another city | Foreknowledge of the next scenarios | They can profit from knowing which district is next |
| The speaking boss | 4 | Act 4's gate | A shadow who was a hero | Every kill they have made |
| Keepsakes | 4 | The Shop terminal that sells their memories | They can buy their collateral back, at a price in levels | Other people's memories are for sale too, and are cheap |
| The offer | 6 | A Host offers them the Finale contract | Immortality and an audience | What the last winners did |

## 6. Running the horror

- **Give the reward first.** Each arc pays out before it shows its cost. The players should be counting gold when
  they understand what they did.
- **Keep the System polite.** It never threatens. It says "Scenario issued", "Collateral taken" and "Thank you
  for playing" in the same voice it uses for a daily quest.
- **Let the numbers do it.** The app shows XP for every kill. After act 4 that number is the same number, and it
  means something else.
- **Do not explain early.** Until act 4 the party should be able to believe that the monsters are only monsters.
- **Make the audience small and specific.** Three named viewers with tastes are worse than a million. One likes
  mercy, one likes betrayal, and one only tips when a child is in the scene.

## 7. What the world believes

Nobody in the world knows any of section 1. `power.md`, section 9, gives the public's guesses. Three groups are
nearly right, and the party can meet each:

- **The Ledger** believes the System keeps accounts and that an audit is coming. It does keep accounts.
- **The Open Hand** believes monsters are refugees. They are closer to prisoners.
- **Helix's engineers** know that every crystal core hums at 03:14. It is the broadcast's carrier signal.

## 8. Questions for the owner

1. Should a raising cost a level in the game's rules, or should collateral stay a story effect?
2. In act 5, is the Finale a thing the Hosts steer, or the first season's winners?
3. How many viewers should have names, and should a player's sponsor be played by the GM or by another player?
4. At the end, can the party turn the game off? If they can, the world loses potions, crystals and gold.
5. Do the few other uncapped hunters (Okoro, Osei) help in act 5, or does one of them take the Hosts' offer?
