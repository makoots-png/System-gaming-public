# The story: what the game is, and how it ends

This file is for the GM only. It gives the truth behind the System, the campaign in six acts, and the story arcs
that carry the horror. The owner set the outline on Nov 20: every human has the System, nobody knows why, the players
grow strong, outgrow the game, fight what the game was preparing them for, and then kill the people who run it.
The tone is bleak. The players should enjoy getting stronger and hate what getting stronger shows them.

The structure borrows one idea from Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint, at the owner's suggestion: the game has an
audience. Everything else here is new.

## 1. The truth

Tell the players none of this. Each act reveals one part.

**The game is a broadcast.** A group called the Hosts runs it. They are not gods. They are the staff of a
business, and Earth is its current season. They have run the same game on other worlds before Earth.

**The audience pays, and their payment is the gold.** Viewers from many worlds watch hunters clear gates. The gold
a Clear pays is the viewers' money, less the Hosts' cut. The Shop is the Hosts' store, and every potion bought
there returns gold to them. Earth rebuilt its economy on tips from people who watch its citizens die.

**The caps are casting.** A broadcast needs a few leads and a large number of people who can be killed. The Hosts
assign each human a rank at awakening and cap the level at that rank. A person who never awakens is a Resident,
capped at level 0, and exists in the game to be in danger. A trial gate removes a cap (or raises it by one rank in a
step trial). The Hosts open an open trial when they want to promote hunters to the lead cast. On rare occasions, a
rogue sponsor pays a Host off the books to stage an unscheduled trial inside a routine gate before a party reaches its
cap, as happens in Mission 1 (`The Empty Seat`).

**The monsters are the casts of earlier seasons.** When a world loses its season, the Hosts keep its people. They
strip their windows, lock their bodies into monster forms, and place them in gates as the opposition for the next
world. An E-rank goblin warren is a street of some earlier world's Residents. A boss is one of its S-ranks. Most
no longer remember. Some do.

**The game does prepare its players for something real.** The Hosts call it the Finale. It is an enemy that
destroys worlds, and it reaches every world the Hosts broadcast from, because the Hosts lead it there. A season
ends when it arrives. The Hosts sell the Finale as the last episode and tell each world that a winner is released.
One world has won, the first, and the Hosts did not release it. They offered its champions a contract, and those
champions have been the Finale of every season since.

**The Hosts could have armed every world and warned it.** They chose to sell tickets. That is the reason to kill
them.

## 2. What every human sees

Every person on Earth has had a window since 03:14 UTC on Day Zero. This is public knowledge, and it is the
strangest ordinary fact in the world.

| | A Resident (never awakened) | A hunter |
|---|---|---|
| Window | Name, "Resident", level 0, and a line that reads "Cap: 0" | Name, job, rank, level, stats, quests, and a line that reads "Cap:" with the top level of the rank |
| Quests | Only during a break nearby. The usual one is "Survive: 10:00" | A daily quest, the System's quests, and on rare occasions a personal gate |
| Rewards | Nothing a Resident can see | XP up to the cap, gold, titles (dailies never heal or restore a hunter) |
| The Shop and Inventory | No | Yes |

Three facts follow, and people in the world have noticed the first two:

- **Awakening is a Resident quest.** A Resident who completes "Survive" during a break has about one chance in
  sixty of awakening within the year. This is why awakening is more common near breaks (`power.md`, section 1).
  Some people walk toward sirens on purpose.
- **A capped hunter still gets the daily quest.** A hunter at their cap gains nothing from it and is still
  Penalized for missing it. Completing it never heals HP, restores mana, or clears fatigue. Hunters at their cap
  do a hundred push-ups every morning for no reward, for life.
- **The window counts something for Residents too.** A Resident's window has one more line that is blank until
  act 2 (section 4). After Mission 1 lifts the party's cap, a single blank line also appears below `"Cap: none"` on
  each hunter's window and stays blank until Act 2's Network Audit turns on `"Viewers"`.

## 3. Death costs more than gold

Death follows the owner's revive ladder (`machines.md`, `Revive standing` and `A death`):

1. **First revive: free.** Within an hour of death, the System raises the hunter once for no gold and no crystal.
   The Hosts want new cast members to survive episode one and think the System is merciful.
2. **Second revive: one revive on debt.** Within an hour, the hunter may return once on debt for ten Clears of
   their rank. No second debt revive exists.
3. **Third death and after: pre-bought crystal or GM grant only.** After the debt revive is spent, the System
   offers no return. Only a Resurrection Crystal of the hunter's rank or higher, bought before the death and used
   within an hour, or a GM grant brings the hunter back. Gold held after death does not help.

**The System takes collateral on every raising, including the free first revive.** When a hunter rises, the window
shows one GM-approved line, `"Collateral taken"`, and does not say what. The hunter finds out later. Doctors in
Carrow call the first gap "first-return amnesia" (`power.md`, section 9). Use this table, or choose.

| Raising | Gold cost (revive ladder) | What the System takes |
|---|---|---|
| First | Free | A memory the hunter valued (a parent's voice, a childhood street, a song). Other people still remember it. |
| Second | One revive on debt (10 Clears) | Other people's memory of one thing the hunter did. The record of it remains, and nobody recalls it. |
| Third | Pre-bought crystal (10 Clears) or GM grant | One person forgets the hunter completely. The System chooses a person the hunter loves. |
| Fourth | Pre-bought crystal or GM grant | A year of the hunter's life, taken at once. The hunter looks a year older. |
| Fifth and after | Pre-bought crystal or GM grant | The hunter's own choice between the four above, with a ten-second timer. |

In act 4 the players learn where the collateral goes. The Hosts sell the memories to viewers as keepsakes
(`machines.md`, `A keepsake`).

**A raising also costs levels.** the owner ruled this on Nov 20: a hunter raised from death loses 3.33% of their
level, rounded up, so at least one level. It applies to every raising, the free one included. A raising can
therefore drop a hunter below a rank line (`docs/agent-handoff/features/economy/design.md`, section 10, ruling 9).

## 4. The campaign in six acts

**The cap is story, not a rule of XP math.** the owner ruled on Nov 20 that the app never enforces a hard level cap in
XP math (`gainXp` never stops a hunter at level 9). Instead, each hunter has a display-only cap chip (`Cap: 9`,
`Cap: none`, or hidden) that reflects the story state the GM runs. Under the owner's Act 1 redesign ruling, the party's
unique horror trial happens in **Mission 1 (`The Empty Seat`, inside `Tidewall Cannery Gate`)** at levels 1 to 3,
lifting their cap to `Cap: none` and opening Wave 1 unique classes (Mythic and Epic) right away. The Hosts' formal
wrong-colored gate and worst-hour memory room moves to **Act 2 (Level 20)** as a **Network Audit**. Nobody can be
stranded permanently at a cap.


`machines.md` charts `The season`, `A scenario`, `Sponsor patronage`, `A keepsake`, and `A System message or reward`.
Every System line, tip, quest, and reward below goes through GM editing and approval before players see it.

Each act matches a rank. The party's rank decides which act they are in.

### Act 1, ranks E and D: the trial and the job

Act 1 opens with **The Empty Seat** (Mission 1, levels 1 to 3) and then follows broke Level 3 E-ranks working Carrow's
gate economy while hiding `Cap: none` and impossible classes. High-depth nodes on Mythic and Epic trees cost heavy
gold, and the party still has E-rank HP, E-rank gear, and an E-rank Bureau license.

- **Mission 1, The Empty Seat (`Tidewall Cannery Gate`, levels 1 to 3):** a rogue sponsor (**The Bettor**, section 7;
  proposed buyer handle, needs the owner) pays a Season 12 Host off the books to run an unscheduled, old-format open
  trial inside a routine Bureau E-rank gate (`Tidewall Cannery Gate`, Day 6 of 7). The buyer prepays a seed package
  from the Hosts' vault: six Wave 1 unique class licenses and an early cap waiver.
  - **Beat 1 (The Halfway House):** Bureau seat clerk Pim Aldous stamps the party's E-rank license (10-gold entry fee
    per hunter, ~100-gold Clear against a 12-gold daily wage), reads the revive ladder off a card, and issues 2 Health
    Potions (E) per hunter. An unnamed man in his fifties does squats in the queue (`The crying man`). The stamped
    license lists one extra paid line with no name. Pim assumes someone is late.
  - **Beat 2 (The cannery tunnels, Fight 1):** inside the gate, the System window states that the party holds one more
    seat than it has hunters, the extra fee is paid, and the seat holder is present. During the fight against the
    goblin pack, the Goblin Chief backs away from an empty tile beside the party and counts one finger too many as it
    dies. After Fight 1 settles (Level 2), the extra seat declines its share of XP.
  - **Beat 3 (The stone hall and three questions):** the exit light stays cold and locked (`Party incomplete`). Beyond
    the boss room stands a dry stone hall with a long table, chairs fitted to each hunter with weapon slots, and one
    extra chair facing the wall. Three lamps burn overhead. The System issues three rules (take your seat, answer when
    asked, remain seated until the trial ends) with reward and penalty not stated. The GM asks three questions aloud,
    paying 20 gold per spoken answer (60 gold per hunter, giving the reward first): *"Who is waiting for you outside
    this gate?"*, *"What were you before the fever?"*, and *"What will you not do for gold?"* After each round, one
    lamp dies and the extra chair sits turned closer to the table unseen (stage rigging in the dark, while viewers still
    watch through the hunters' own eyes, never cameras). Record every answer for Act 2 Value ties, Collateral, and
    Act 3 temptations.
  - **Beat 4 (The dark and the Ushers, Fight 2):** the third lamp dies and the window displays the single `03:14` seed
    in the mission: *"When the lights return, one seat at this table must be empty. Time: 03:14."* No forced-stand roll
    is made. Standing or attacking the extra chair gives open footing. Staying seated lets an Usher open with one
    hunter grabbed (`restrained`). Tall figures in wall-colored coats (**Ushers** and the **Head Usher**, casts from
    an archived season speaking clipped fragments of an unknown language, preserving Act 4's reveal) step out of the
    dark to empty a chair by force.
  - **Beat 5 (The empty seat and outside):** when the Head Usher falls, the extra chair stands pushed back and the
    trial resolves literally (*"The seat holder left. One seat is empty. Remain seated: failed (no penalty stated). Cost
    paid by seat holder. Your cap is removed (Cap: none)."*). Each hunter suffers a brief second fever (`power.md`,
    section 5), claims one unique class from the Wave 1 offer pool (three Mythics: `Hemomancer`, `Bladefiend`,
    `Rifthunter`, and three Epics: `Seraph`, `Ironclad`, `Stormcaller`), receives the **Guest** title (`+1 Discipline`),
    and clears the gate to reach Level 3. No sponsor, viewer, camera, or broadcast is named in Session 1. Back in the
    boss room, a Reclaimers' Local 7 crew is cutting crystal where solid brick now stands. Outside, Pim says they were
    inside twelve minutes and his license has one line fewer. On each hunter's window, below `Cap: none`, sits a single
    new blank line with nothing written on it yet.
- **Session 2 (the morning after):** clinics and guild scouts monitor second fevers (`power.md`, section 5). Four
  second fevers in one night give scouts a thread to pull, and declaring the 60 extra gold from the three questions at
  the Bureau draws immediate mundane scrutiny.
- **What they enjoy:** first levels past E-rank pace, testing Mythic and Epic signatures inside private gates, rare
  drops, a guild's offer, and a clean payday.
- **Playable horror beat (Session 3, the quiet warren):** the party enters a cheap E-rank gate ready to test their new
  Mythic and Epic signatures. The monsters fight poorly, throwing pans and chairs, and fall in one hit. The gate's
  Clear does not register and the exit stays cold until the last room is empty. Stacked desks block the far doorway.
  When the party pulls them down, six small goblins huddle behind an overturned cot clutching a plastic child's shoe.
  Bringing new power into that room sharpens the horror. The System waits in silence until the room is empty, then pays
  400 gold and drops an uncommon dagger.
- **Other details and witness risk (Sessions 4 to 7):** running the daily ten kilometers beside a capped D-rank in his
  fifties who cries as he runs (~7,300 mornings over twenty years) lands harder when the party holds `Cap: none` for
  free and cannot tell him. They also see the "Missing Memories" corkboard at the Salt Locker (`seeds.md`, section 2).
  Because nobody can see inside a world gate, witness risk comes from street breaks (`Survive: 10:00` in Saltmarket,
  where firing a Mythic skill saves lives and exposes the party), call-ups, and fifth seats hired at The Last Lantern.
  Run `The first offer` (Marguerite Sato's list already holds one party member) and `A body in an unentered gate`
  (`seeds.md`, hook 7, proving someone hunts uncapped hunters).
- **The bill:** the first death in the party triggers the **free first revive**. The players feel relieved that it cost
  no gold, until `"Collateral taken"` flashes and, two sessions later, that hunter cannot recall their own mother's
  voice or a song their sibling hums. A second death brings the 10-Clear debt and the second collateral.
- **How it ends (Level 10):** crossing into D-rank while registered as E-rank produces four simultaneous
  "re-awakenings" against fewer than thirty on record (`power.md`, section 5). Act 1 ends with a Carrow cover-up
  choice: fake a single re-awakening, pay a five-Clear assessor bribe, or go underground with Redline or the Stair.

### Act 2, rank C: the audit and the audience

From levels 10 to 19, the party operates under whatever cover story ended Act 1 while guilds, the Bureau, Vantage
(Soren Kalt's impossible betting streak tied back to The Bettor's Mission 1 seat, `factions.md`, section 4), and the
Ledger begin to notice them (`power.md`, section 5).

**Level 20 (Network Audit):** crossing past D-rank into C-rank breaks the single re-awakening cover story (`power.md`,
section 5), because no recorded hunter has ever re-awakened twice. The Hosts flag the off-schedule uncapped file from
Mission 1 and pull the whole party together into a wrong-colored personal gate: a formal **Network Audit**.

**Running the Network Audit:** unlike a standard keyed personal gate, the audit admits the whole party together so each
hunter witnesses the others' worst hour. The gate builds a sequence from each hunter's worst day (using a worst day
played during Act 1 hits harder than backstory alone), populated by the people who were there. To pass, each hunter must
finish their day again and change nothing.

**Do not strand a PC (side-door cost; proposed, needs the owner):** if a hunter breaks the rule and tries to save
someone, reset that hunter's memory on a 60-second loop, harder each pass, until they either walk past the person they
tried to save (ratifying their grant with the weight of having chosen it) or take a side door out. Taking the side door
must carry a real cost, never a free pass off camera (proposed for the owner: restores a cap at the top of the current
rank with a later re-offer, or exacts collateral or sponsor debt).

**Passing the audit** ratifies the party's off-books Mission 1 grant, moves them from a quiet rogue bet onto the public
Season 12 channel, fills the blank line below `"Cap: none"` with `"Viewers"` in the low hundreds, and activates
`"Value: XX XP"` above every Resident.

- **What they enjoy:** levels past C-rank and full Mythic/Epic depth while managing Carrow's factions.
- **Playable horror beat (Value):** walking out of the Halfway House after the audit, the party sees a new line above
  every Resident's head: `"Value: 18 XP"` over Pim Aldous, `"Value: 45 XP"` over a child at the tram stop (and over the
  people they named in Mission 1's first question). Children are worth more because monsters get more XP for killing
  them. Before the week ends, a Stair broker notices them staring above people's heads and offers 2,000 gold for a map
  of Saltmarket's highest-Value blocks.
- **What changes in the world:** the party is now on the public Season 12 feed. Guilds, the Bureau, Vantage, and the
  Ledger escalate their pressure (`power.md`, section 5).
- **How it ends:** a viewer sends the first message (approved and timed by the GM). It arrives with a tip of 500 gold
  and four words from `Small Hands`: `"Do the school next."`

### Act 3, rank B: the cast

**Why the second half starts now:** players who meet Sabine Okoro or Marek Osei will ask
why scenarios did not start years ago when those two uncapped. The answer is broadcast economics. Lone hidden
hunters like Okoro and Osei never drew enough concurrent viewers to pay for city-wide scenarios. A full uncapped
party climbing fast in public pushes Earth's viewer count past the Hosts' 10,000-viewer threshold, greenlighting the
season's second half. The party's own rise triggered the scenarios.

The System speaks to every window in Carrow at once, issuing **scenarios** with a timer and a penalty.

- **What they enjoy:** sponsors. A viewer who adopts a hunter sends gold, gear, and private quests (`machines.md`,
  `Sponsor patronage`). By mid-act the party can afford B-rank gear, skill books, and pre-bought crystals.
- **How Carrow reacts when scenarios start (proposed, needs the owner):**
  - **The Bureau** denies the messages are binding for six hours, then treats scenarios like breaks and starts
    assigning parties to meet them.
  - **Ironwake** pulls its liners into Ledger Row and refuses to spend them on Scarside scenarios.
  - **Aurelian** films every scenario exit and tries to claim credit on television.
  - **The Once-Born League and Plain Men** blame uncapped hunters for drawing the System's attention and picket the
    Halfway House.
- **Playable horror beat (the Scarside count; proposed, needs the owner):** every window in Carrow displays
  `"Scenario: Scarside's population must fall by 400 before dawn. Penalty: Scarside loses power and Shop access for
  one year."` The wording says **population**, not deaths. Colonel Yun's Corps seals Scarside's bridges to prevent
  a stampede; a guild crew and the Plain Men both move in to settle the count in blood. The party can either force a
  bridge open and evacuate 400 families out of Scarside's boundary before dawn (losing their Bureau licences and
  making an enemy of Yun, while `The Bettor` tips against them and `Quiet Benefactor` tips for them) or let the
  district tear itself apart.
- **The choice of the act:** sponsors pay more for cruelty. A hunter can refuse and stay poor beside rivals, or
  accept and pull ahead.
- **Planting Act 6's twist (proposed, needs the owner):**
  - **Sabine Okoro** saves the party during a bad scenario and hands over her notebook of repeating scenario scripts
    from three other cities, proving the scenarios are a script. Notice two clues: she asks a raised PC what memory
    they lost, and she never speaks of her own mother (taken on her third raising).
  - **Marek Osei** acts as a greedy Stair broker, charging full price for forged licences and off-book gates. Twice
    when a scenario threatens children, however, the party catches him paying out of his own pocket to move a cellar
    full of families and warning the party never to tell anyone.

### Act 4, rank A: the other seasons

A double gate opens into a place that is not a dungeon. It is a ruined transit concourse under a sky with two pale
moons.

- **Playable horror beat (the concourse; proposed, needs the owner):** the first two rooms look like a standard A
  gate, except the safety signs on the tiled walls show familiar stick figures under an unreadable alphabet, and the
  "monsters" wear patched tactical vests and blow tin whistles to coordinate their retreat. Let the party win both
  fights and collect their XP and gold first. Only in the third room does the boss drop her spear and speak through
  the System's translation.
- **The speaking boss (proposed, needs the owner):** **Captain Yael Sorin**, once the strongest hunter of Season 9
  (Earth is Season 12). She tells them how her world lost to the five champions, how her survivors had their windows
  stripped and their bodies remade into gate packs, and that every monster the party has killed since Act 1 was a
  person from a lost season. She asks them to kill her and extract her shadow so she can watch the Hosts die through
  Earth's eyes. If the party has no Necromancer, she presses her battered spear and a cracked badge into a party
  member's hand and asks them to carry it into the control room.
- **The Keepsakes terminal (proposed, needs the owner):** beside the concourse wall stands an open Shop kiosk
  labelled `"Earth, Season 12 Keepsakes"`. Every piece of collateral taken from the party sits on the list with a
  live viewer bid timer ticking up. A hunter can buy their own memory back before a viewer wins it, at a price in
  levels: **1 level** for a personal memory (first raising), **2 levels** for a forgotten deed (second raising), or
  **3 levels** to restore a loved one's memory of them (third raising). In clearance bins below sit memories from
  Seasons 1 to 11 for **50 gold each** (a birthday song, a wedding vow, a street map of Season 1's capital). Buying
  a 50-gold keepsake gives a party member a flash of Season 1's five champions before they signed the contract.
- **How it ends:** Sorin's terminal log shows the Tide's 3% annual curve is not natural growth. It is the season's
  countdown clock, and Port Sarn's standing S gate is the stage door for the Finale.

### Act 5, rank S: the Finale

The countdown ends. Port Sarn's twenty-year-old S gate widens, and the first season's winners walk out. Every
earlier season except the first lost to them.

- **The five Season 1 champions (proposed, needs the owner):** each is past level 70, tired, polite, and trapped by a
  contract that keeps their own world alive as long as they end every later season. Run them across a multi-stage
  siege rather than one room brawl:
  1. **Kaelen Vane (Tanker, their leader):** carries a battered shield patched with four seasons' alloys. Calls out
     his heavy strikes a second early so defenders can drag civilians clear.
  2. **Sera Lin (Healer):** tends wounded Earth Residents between engagements if nobody shoots at her, then kills
     any hunter who closes in while apologising.
  3. **Dario Moss (Assassin):** freezes mid-fight when he recognises a party member's cloak or blade from his own
     dead sister's kit. Offers a five-minute parley where he lays out the exact terms of the Hosts' contract.
  4. **Tova Hren (Mage):** levels districts from a kilometre away to end the Finale quickly so Earth suffers less.
  5. **Branek (Necromancer):** refuses to extract a single shadow from Earth's dead, fighting only with the frayed,
     centuries-old shadows of his own original world.
- **Uniting Carrow (proposed, needs the owner):** Carrow's factions have every reason to distrust an uncapped party.
  Give each faction a concrete price for joining the line at Port Sarn:
  - **Ironwake:** Reinholt wants the Bureau to bury the Year 7 sealing (or, if Ines Carvalho replaced him, Ironwake
    marches at once on the promise that the party holds the front beside them).
  - **Aurelian:** Sato wants live broadcast rights until Six or Dax Verrault breaks her hold.
  - **The Ninth Hour:** Amara Nkemelu marches immediately if the party covers Carrow's seawall while she anchors the
    harbour line.
  - **The Once-Born League and Plain Men:** bring their crystal-tipped munitions only if the party swears that
    unawakened witnesses will hear whatever truth lies inside Port Sarn's gate.
- **The cost:** Carrow, or most of it. The Finale should destroy a district or place the players built ties to.
- **Branch A, Earth wins:** the five fall, thanking the party as they die. Every window on Earth displays
  `"Season 12 complete. Thank you for playing."` No gate closes and nobody is released. The viewer count hits its
  highest number of the campaign. Then a private window opens for every uncapped hunter on Earth: the same Finale
  contract.
- **Branch B, Earth is lost (`machines.md`, `The season`; proposed, needs the owner):** if the five break Earth's
  lines, Carrow burns and every window displays `"Season failed. Processing cast."` Before the Hosts can strip the
  survivors' windows, Marek Osei drags the battered party through a maintenance seam behind Port Sarn's fish-market
  gate directly into Act 6's control room.

### Act 6, past S: the control room

There is no rank past S, and the party's levels keep rising. A last gate opens for them alone. Behind it is a
fluorescent corridor and the floor where the Hosts work.

- **The betrayal and the sacrifice:** Sabine Okoro signs the contract. She shows the party one line in her window:
  `"Return of Asset #4091-C: Mother's memory"`. Having read every repeating script, she believes no world is ever
  freed, that signing saves Earth from becoming Season 13's monsters, and that it gives her mother back. She blocks
  the last door as Earth's first Finale champion. Marek Osei, whom the party paid in coin and never fully trusted,
  refuses the contract, steps into Okoro's path, and dies holding the service door open until the lock welds shut
  behind the party.
- **The control room encounter (proposed, needs the owner):** inside are cubicles, coffee rings, a schedule board with
  Season 13's world already dated, and a ratings chart with the party's names at the top. Twelve Hosts sit behind
  glass in headsets. They are weak fighters (about level 15), so they fight by pulling System levers mid-combat:
  cutting the party's Shop access, slapping fake `"Penalty Zone"` debuffs onto individual hunters, spawning emergency
  gates to block corridors, and shouting real-time bribes ("Take your memories back! Take a million gold! If you kill
  us, upstairs sends a clean-up crew!").
- **What they do:** kill the Hosts. Make it a slaughter and do not let it feel clean. Some beg. At desk four sits
  **Maren** (`proposed, needs the owner`), surrounded by handwritten balance tables for Health Potions; she asks which
  party member survived an E-rank warren in Act 1 on her 10-gold bottle, puts her hands on the desk, and does not
  fight back.
- **What they cannot do:** turn the game off. The console has no off switch. The gates, the gold, the Shop, and the
  windows run on machinery deeper than this office. The Hosts only chose the cast, wrote the scenarios, set the caps,
  and sold the broadcast.
- **The last choice (the three screens at the seat; proposed, needs the owner):** a single worn office chair faces
  three live panels: **Schedule** (Season 13 queued), **Cast & Caps** (Earth's cap switches), and **Ledger** (viewer
  accounts that fund the Shop). This choice bookends Mission 1's empty chair.
  - **Leave the seat empty:** the Hosts are dead and nobody runs the game. Gates keep opening, and no scenarios,
    caps, or sponsors are ever set again, which also means nobody can lift existing caps or cancel Season 13.
  - **Take the seat:** the party runs the office. They can lift every cap on Earth and pull Season 13 off the
    schedule, yet the Shop's potions that keep Earth's hospitals running depend on viewer gold, so shutting off the
    broadcast starves the Shop. Either way, the Hosts' employers will eventually send new staff.

## 6. Running the horror

- **Edit and approve every System message and reward first (the owner's ruling).** Every window line, viewer tip,
  scenario prompt, collateral notice, and reward passes through the GM before players see it (`machines.md`,
  `A System message or reward`). Tailor the wording to what actually happened at the table.
- **Give the reward first.** Each arc pays out before it shows its cost. The players should be counting gold when
  they understand what they did.
- **Keep the System polite.** It never threatens. It says "Scenario issued", "Collateral taken" and "Thank you
  for playing" in the same voice it uses for a daily quest.
- **Let the numbers do it.** The app shows XP for every kill. After act 4 that number is the same number, and it
  means something else.
- **Do not explain early.** Until act 4 the party should be able to believe that the monsters are only monsters.
- **Make the audience small and specific.** A few named viewers with tastes are worse than a million. Section 7
  lists them.

**A player as a sponsor.** One of the owner's friends can play the Patron in place of a hunter, or between
sessions. Give that player these rules:

- A budget of gold each session, about two Clears of the party's rank, to draft tips and one-line messages to any

- One private quest each arc, drafted for one hunter, with a reward from the Shop.
- **GM approval required (the owner's ruling):** every message, gold tip, private quest, and reward the Patron drafts
