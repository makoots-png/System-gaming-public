# Factions

Each entry has the same parts: what the faction wants, what it competes over, the disagreement inside it, its
notable figures with a hook for each, how it recruits, and how a party meets it. The entries describe San Francisco. Every
large city has factions like them.

## 1. What factions compete over

| Prize | Why it is scarce | Who competes for it |
|---|---|---|
| Seats in gates | San Francisco has 400 world gates a month with four seats each, and 1,600 hunters. A seat pays gold and drops | Guilds, each guild's own hunters, the Bureau |
| Gate rights | The Bureau auctions half of all gates. A guild's margin comes from the harvest | Guilds, Helix, illegal crews |
| Hunters | One person in five hundred awakens, and each is capped at their rank. A guild can hire strength and cannot train it past the cap | Guilds, the Hunter Corps, foreign states, the Stair |
| Healers | One hunter in eight | Every faction that sends a party into a gate |
| Resurrection Crystals | A crystal costs ten Clears. A hunter owes that sum to whoever paid for theirs | Guilds, insurers, lenders |
| Mana crystal | It powers the city, and one company buys nine tenths of it | Helix, the Bureau's exchange, fences |
| The account of a raid | Nobody can film a gate | Press officers, tellers, Vantage |
| Response time | It sets rent and land prices | Districts, the Bureau, any guild that is choosing where to build a hall |

## 2. Guilds

### Ironwake

Ironwake is the oldest guild in San Francisco. A party that defended the river bridges in the Break Years founded it in
Year 2. It has sixty-seven hunters and a hall on Ledger Row. An agreement older than the auction law gives it
first refusal on a fifth of the city's auctioned gates.

- **Wants:** to remain the largest guild. This year it wants to keep its B and A gates. In the long term it wants an S-rank of its own. It has never had one.
- **Competes over:** gate rights, healers, and its reputation from the Break Years. It often bids on a gate only to
  stop a rival from getting it.
- **Inside:** older members believe seats should go by years of service. Younger captains believe seats should go to the party most likely to clear the gate.
- **Figures:**
  - **Tobias Reinholt**, called Anvil. Guildmaster, A-rank Tanker, aged 61 with the body of someone 48. *Hook:* in
    Year 7 he sealed a gate that was about to break while thirty reclaimers were still inside. The district
    survived. Ironwake has paid those thirty families a large pension ever since and has never told them why.
  - **Ines Carvalho**, called Last Out. Raid captain, B-rank Tanker. *Hook:* half of the captains want her as the next guildmaster. Reinholt has stopped giving her seats.
  - **Osric Bell.** Quartermaster, unawakened. He keeps the vault. *Hook:* the vault holds eleven fewer C crystals
    than the books record.
- **Recruits:** from the academies it sponsors, with an offer within two weeks of a hunter's reading. It also buys
  out good hunters from smaller guilds.
- **A party meets it:** when the party outbids it for a gate, or when it offers the party a week's work as an extra
  party on a raid.

### The Lantern Company

The Lantern Company is a cooperative. Its 133 hunters, almost all E to C, own it in equal shares and vote
on which gate rights to bid for. It clears the E and D gates that other guilds do not want, in large numbers. Its
members voted to put a healer in every party.

- **Wants:** steady work and no deaths. In the long term it wants a seat on the board that runs the Bureau's
  auctions.
- **Competes over:** cheap gates, against freelancers and the Bureau's own crews. It also competes for healers and
  pays them more than it can afford.
- **Inside:** the Company's C-ranks and its few B-ranks want to bid for higher gates. Most members want to stay
  with E and D gates, where few hunters die.
- **Figures:**
  - **Sun-hee Marlowe.** Steward, C-rank Healer, 39. *Hook:* this month she overbid on six D gates to keep them
    from Ironwake, and four of them break on the same day. She needs a party she trusts and cannot hire one
    openly.
  - **Pavel Dunmore.** A founding member, an E-rank at fifty-eight. *Hook:* he passed a step trial and had a second
    fever last month (`power.md`, section 5) and has not reported it. His cap rose to D, he now fights like a D-rank,
    and a guild scout has noticed.
- **Recruits:** any licensed hunter whom two members vouch for. It pays no signing bonus. A new member gets a
  share.
- **A party meets it:** as sub-contractors on a gate the Company cannot cover, or in the queue at the Bureau hall.

### Aurelian

A holding company owns Aurelian. The guild has a glass tower, a media department and eight hunters whom the public
knows by name.

- **Wants:** places in the Hundred. This year it wants three of its hunters on the list. In the long term it wants to sign any re-awakened hunter before a rival does.
- **Competes over:** well-known hunters, sponsors, and A gates in places where cameras have a clear view.
- **Inside:** the hunters who appear in photographs do not clear most of the gates. A second team does, and its contract forbids it from saying so. The Bureau's clear record lists the names on
  the licence, and Aurelian's licences do not always name the hunters who went in.
- **Figures:**
  - **Dax Verrault.** A-rank Assassin, the guild's public face (`fame.md`, section 7). *Hook:* he wants to clear,
    himself, one of the gates he is credited with.
  - **Marguerite Sato.** Managing director, unawakened. *Hook:* she keeps a list of hunters whose results are better than their rank explains, and an offer for each.
    One member of the party is on the list.
  - **A hunter known as Six.** A-rank Ranger on the second team. The contract keeps the name secret. *Hook:* Six swore a full account of one raid before a notary, and Aurelian is paying to get the document back.
- **Recruits:** with large offers, lawyers and a photographer. Its contract includes every trap listed in
  `careers.md`, section 4.
- **A party meets it:** when a member of the party is filmed doing something notable, or when Six needs help from
  hunters who are on no guild's payroll.

### The Ninth Hour

The Ninth Hour is five hunters led by the city's only S-rank. It has no reserve team, no academy and no press
office. It takes the gates that could destroy the city, and the city pays it to do so.

- **Wants:** San Francisco to survive. This year it wants a second S-rank, from anywhere, before Amara Nkemelu leaves or
  dies. It states no long-term aim.
- **Competes over:** nothing. The city gives it what it asks for.
- **Inside:** its members disagree on whether the guild should follow the city or the Abstention when the two
  conflict.
- **Figures:**
  - **Amara Nkemelu**, called Tidebreaker. S-rank Mage (`fame.md`, section 7).
  - **Henrik Vale.** Her deputy, A-rank Healer, fifty. *Hook:* he has heard the rumour of hunters who grow and believes it. He is looking for one to train as her
    successor, and the Bureau has told him to stop.
- **Recruits:** by invitation only.
- **A party meets it:** at a break, where its five hunters take command and tell the party where to stand, or
  when Vale visits one of the party at home.

### The Gravewardens

The Gravewardens are a guild and a public register. The law requires every licensed Necromancer in San Francisco to
belong. Their hall is beside the old cemetery in Saltmarket.

- **Wants:** extraction to stay legal. This year it wants every shadow in the city on the register. In the long
  term it wants no public inquiry into what a shadow is.
- **Competes over:** corpses. A Necromancer and a reclaimer both have a claim on a boss's body, and the body is
  worth more to the Necromancer.
- **Inside:** the registrars want stricter limits. The Necromancers who work in gates want fewer. Each of them has
  stood beside a dead friend and chosen not to extract.
- **Figures:**
  - **Mireille Tazi.** Guildmaster, B-rank Necromancer, 44. *Hook:* one entry in the register has no kill recorded
    against it. She made the entry. The shadow is her brother's.
  - **Jonah Achterberg.** Registrar, unawakened. *Hook:* a shadow on the register spoke to him last week, in a
    language he did not know, and then in his own. It asked what world this was.
- **Recruits:** by law. A hunter whose window says Necromancer must report within thirty days.
- **A party meets it:** on the day a member of the party extracts a shadow, or when a client pays the party to
  leave a boss's corpse intact.

### Redline

Redline is a mercenary company. It has sixteen hunters on day rates, a dispatcher and a bar. It
specialises in red gates, work that guild contracts forbid, and "third-life escorts": matching veterans who have
spent their free and debt revives with parties that carry a pre-bought crystal, in exchange for first claim on any
named drop (proposed, needs the owner).

- **Wants:** payment in coin on the day of the job, and a cut of every named drop its third-life contracts pull out.
- **Competes over:** urgent work, such as a break, a sealed gate, or a guild that is one healer short at dawn.
- **Inside:** older mercenaries treat Redline as a guild-free refuge and honour the third-life bargain. Dispatcher
  Kofi Stein has begun taking quiet payments from the Stair to steer indebted third-lifers into high-risk red gates
  where pre-bought crystals disappear (proposed, needs the owner).
- **Figures:**
  - **Kofi Stein.** Dispatcher, B-rank Ranger, with one hand. *Hook:* for a fee he guarantees a hunter a seat in a
    gate of their rank within a month. He does not say where the seats come from, or why three of his third-life
    clients died without their pooled crystal last quarter.
  - **Dr Lale Demir.** A-rank Healer with no guild contract. *Hook:* she is the only healer in San Francisco who works for
    unsigned parties. Every guild has tried to hire her, and one guild now plans to force her.
- **Recruits:** hunters whom guilds dismissed, deserters from the Corps, third-life veterans without a guild vault,
  and hunters whom no guild will seat.
- **A party meets it:** the first time the party needs a fifth member, or when a veteran hires them as a crystal
  escort. `economy.md`, section 10, has the prices.

## 3. The state

### The Gate Bureau, San Francisco office

The Bureau reads gates and hunters, keeps the Register, runs Doorwatch, auctions gate rights, licenses parties,
collects the levy, and clears public gates with its own crews. It has about 270 staff, of whom five are
assessors who can read a gate. Its hall on Ledger Row is called the Halfway House. Every new hunter in San Francisco is
read there.

- **Wants:** no breaks. This month it wants every posted gate cleared. It also wants to know at once of any hunter whose rank changes.
- **Competes over:** authority. Guilds have more money than the Bureau, and it cannot give orders to an S-rank.
- **Inside:** the field service wants gates cleared by any party that will do it. The watch desk keeps a file on hunters suspected of growing and wants them held for study.
- **Figures:**
  - **Halima Voss.** Director, unawakened, 58. *Hook:* she has two forecasts of the Tide. The public one shows
    San Francisco coping. The one in her safe shows that by Year 31 San Francisco cannot clear its gates with the hunters it has. She has the
    watch desk's file on her desk and wants to know whether a hunter can be made stronger.
  - **Renzo Okabe.** Chief assessor, D-rank, the best reader in the country. *Hook:* in Year 15 he read a B gate as
    C, and eleven hunters died. He says the gate changed rank after he read it. Assessors hold that a gate cannot
    change rank.
  - **Pim Aldous.** Seat clerk for E and D gates. *Hook:* he takes bribes in Health Potions and gives them all to a
    hospital ward in Scarside. Parties know about the bribes and do not know about the ward.
- **Recruits:** by examination. About one hunter in a thousand can sense a gate's rank, and the Bureau hires those
  as assessors.
- **A party meets it:** every week, for the queue, the licence, the entry fee and the declaration of gold.

### The Hunter Corps

The Corps has forty ranked soldiers in San Francisco's garrison and the right to call up every other hunter. It
commands the unawakened infantry of the cordon.

- **Wants:** enough hunters at the next break. In the long term it wants its own A-ranks. It cannot match guild
  pay.
- **Competes over:** hunters. It can conscript a hunter for a day. It cannot afford to hire one for a year.
- **Figure:** **Colonel Dragan Yun.** C-rank Fighter and garrison commander. *Hook:* he keeps a list of hunters who
  ignored the last call-up. Three of them sit on Ironwake's board, and he has not decided whether to charge them.
- **Recruits:** volunteers, convicted hunters, and hunters in debt. It pays off a crystal debt in exchange for
  five years of service.
- **A party meets it:** when the break siren sounds. The GM should make the first call-up of a campaign interrupt
  something the party cares about.

### The Accord and the Abstention

The Accord is international law. Its secretariat is in San Francisco's old customs house, and its inspectors may enter
any Bureau. The Abstention is an unwritten agreement among the S-ranks, and each of them keeps it because the
others would kill one who broke it. *Hook:* both assume that rank is fixed. An Accord inspector has asked the San Francisco office for its file on hunters
who grow, and so has Tomas Reyes-Lindahl.

## 4. Corporations

### Helix Manaworks

Helix has powered San Francisco since Year 4. It buys nine tenths of the city's crystal, runs the reactors and owns the
grid.

- **Wants:** more crystal each year, and larger cores, because its reactors need larger cores as they age. This
  year it wants a law that gives a rights holder the right to hold any gate until its last day. In the long term it
  wants its own hunters and gates so that it does not have to buy from guilds.
- **Competes over:** gate rights, against the guilds that supply it, and influence at the Bureau.
- **Figures:**
  - **Priya Lindgren.** Chief executive. *Hook:* Helix's data shows that a gate held too long can change rank. She
    supports the law anyway, because without larger cores the city loses power in Year 26.
  - **Arlo Finch.** Reactor engineer. *Hook:* one of his cores hums at 03:14 every night, the minute the gates
    opened. He has kept notes for a year.
- **Recruits:** crystal cutters and engineers. Recently it has hired hunters at guild rates with company shares.
- **A party meets it:** when the party sells a core at Helix's buying office on Stonemarket, or when Helix hires
  the party in secret to stop another party from clearing a gate early.

### Paragon Mutual

Paragon insures hunters with crystal cover, homes with break cover, and also guilds and cities.

- **Wants:** to price risk more accurately than anyone else, which requires better information than the Bureau
  has. In the long term it wants to decide where the Green zones end.
- **Competes over:** information and crystals. Its vault holds more crystals than any other in the city.
- **Figures:**
  - **Elias Thorne.** Chief actuary. *Hook:* next year's rate map removes break cover from two Amber districts.
    Anyone who sees the map early can profit from property in Northfield.
  - **Cass Imani.** Claims assessor, E-rank. *Hook:* last month she refused a crystal claim because the licence shows that the hunter entered the gate alone. The hunter's party says four of them went
    in and that someone altered the licence.
- **Recruits:** E-ranks who are good with numbers and do not want to enter gates.
- **A party meets it:** the first time a member dies, or when Imani hires the party to investigate a claim.

### Vantage

Vantage publishes the Hundred. It employs four tellers and holds a betting licence on raid clears and Doorwatch
countdowns.

- **Wants:** a new story every week, and a monopoly on raid-survival betting.
- **Competes over:** exclusive raid accounts, against guild press officers, and betting liquidity, against illegal
  books in the Stair.
- **Inside:** the editorial desk wants to expose Aurelian's shadow team; the betting desk makes its highest margin on
  Aurelian's rigged odds and blocks the story.
- **Figures:**
  - **Coco Marchetti.** Editor of the Hundred. *Hook:* she plans a front-page story on a member of the party whose
    results do not match their rank, which will draw the Bureau's attention. She will hold the story in exchange for a
    better one.
  - **Soren Kalt.** Head oddsmaker, unawakened. *Hook, for the GM only:* an anonymous account has won forty-one
    straight bets on who walks out of sealed gates, paying in uncirculated System coins (proposed, needs the owner).
    The account belongs to a viewer ("The Bettor", `story.md`, section 7). Kalt thinks a Bureau clerk is rigging raids
    and will pay the party to find the leak.
- **A party meets it:** the morning after the party's first notable success, or when Kalt hires them to trace a
  punters' streak that no human could predict.

## 5. Cults

### The Ledger

The Ledger is a church. It teaches that the System keeps an account of each person and that one day the accounts
will be judged. It began in Year 1, among people who studied the Shop. It also teaches that the System will lift
the cap of anyone whose account is clean.

- **Wants:** every member's account to be clean. In the long term it wants to find a person whose cap has been
  lifted.
- **Competes over:** young hunters, against the guilds, and frightened people, against the Open Hand.
- **Inside:** members disagree on whether killing in a gate counts against a hunter's account.
- **Figures:**
  - **Mother Ansel Whitlow.** C-rank, 66, leader of the San Francisco congregation. She has been at level 34 for nineteen
    years and has done the daily quest every day. *Hook:* a member told her in confession that his window reads
    "Cap: none". She believes him and has not decided whom to tell.
  - **Brother Tully.** The church's treasurer. *Hook:* the church lends for crystals at two percent a month, one
    point below the guilds. 120 hunters owe it money.
- **Recruits:** at dawn in Founders' Park, where capped hunters do the daily quest together, and among hunters who
  have just been raised.
- **A party meets it:** when the Ledger hears that a member of the party has passed their cap. It treats that
  hunter as holy, in public.

### The Open Hand

The Open Hand believes that the monsters are fleeing something and that clearing a gate kills refugees. It has few
members. Its actions have led to about two hundred deaths in San Francisco.

- **Wants:** to let one gate break with nobody there to fight, to show that the monsters would not attack. This
  year it wants to hide a gate for a week.
- **Competes over:** unfound gates, against spotters and neighbours.
- **Figure:** **Baptiste Roux**, called Uncle. Unawakened, a retired schoolteacher. *Hook:* his class of
  eight-year-olds died on day 7 of the Long Week. He also says he once saw a monster come out of a gate and sit
  down without attacking anyone. Both statements are true.
- **Recruits:** people who have lost family to breaks.
- **A party meets it:** in a cellar, on day 6 of an unreported D gate, where twelve members are sitting in front
  of the gate.

## 6. Criminals

### The Stair

The Stair sells unlicensed seats. A hunter with no guild and no place in the Bureau's queue pays half a Clear of the
gate's rank and enters with a party the Stair has arranged. The Stair also forges licences, buys drops at 40% of the Shop's price,
launders gold through runners, and lends for crystals at five percent a month.

- **Wants:** the licence laws to stay in force, because an open market in seats would end its trade.
- **Competes over:** gates, by bribing guild staff for seats, and debtors, against the Ledger.
- **Inside:** the older members deal in stolen gear and want no attention. The seat brokers earn far more, and their trade brings Bureau investigators.
- **Figures:**
  - **Rosalind Quill**, called Auntie. Unawakened, seventy, a pawnbroker in Tidewall. *Hook:* forty hunters
    owe her for crystals. She has never demanded payment from any of them and intends to demand it from all of
    them on one day that she has chosen.
  - **Marek Osei**, called Landing. Seat broker, D-rank on the Register. *Hook, for the GM only:* he passed a trial gate and has no cap. He was read as D eight years ago and now
    fights like an A-rank. He sells seats so that he can enter gates without a licence check.
- **Recruits:** hunters in debt, hunters in a hurry, and rich families' children.
- **A party meets it:** when the party sells a drop it cannot explain, or when the only seat available this month
  is one that Osei sells.

### Illegal crews

An illegal crew of reclaimers strips a gate without holding its rights. Some crews enter after the Clear and
before the holder's crew arrives. Others pay a hunter to guard them while they strip a gate that has not been
cleared. *Hook:* Nasrin Gallo's union calls these crews strike-breakers. Half of the crews are her own members
working a second shift, and she knows.

### The Fallen

The Fallen are hunters who kill other hunters inside gates. Nothing records a killing there, and the body is lost
when the gate closes. The most feared Fallen are "crystal-strippers": hired crews who track veterans carrying a
pre-bought Resurrection Crystal (which never expires and can always be traded), steal the crystal first, and kill the
owner so they stay dead (proposed, needs the owner). *Hook:* a party finds a dead third-life hunter inside a gate that
the Bureau listed as unentered, with an empty crystal case strapped to their belt.

## 7. The unawakened

### The Once-Born League

The League campaigns for the rights of unawakened people. It was founded outside a hospital in Year 9.

- **Wants:** equal legal standing for people who cannot be raised. This year it wants a public trial of a crystal
  on an unawakened volunteer. In the long term it wants courts that can hold a hunter.
- **Competes over:** the city council, where it holds eleven of forty seats, and the levy on hunters, which it
  wants doubled.
- **Inside:** most members want change by law. A smaller group has noticed that people awaken more often near a
  break and is discussing how to use that.
- **Figures:**
  - **Councillor Teodora Baskin.** The League's leader in San Francisco. *Hook:* her seventeen-year-old son awakened last
    week and is in her attic. His window says B-rank. The 72 hours have passed, the late fine is 15,000 gold, and
    registering a B-rank son would break her coalition overnight.
  - **Dr Imre Solis.** A clinician who studies ageing. *Hook:* he has found a way to make a crystal begin to work
    on an unawakened body. A hunter must die beside the patient. He has not published it.
- **Recruits:** at funerals.
- **A party meets it:** at a League protest against hunters, or when Baskin asks the party for a favour that
  breaks the registration law.

### Reclaimers' Local 7

Local 7 is the union of the crews that cut crystal and carcasses out of gates. It has about 1,160 members in San Francisco.
They enter gates with no powers, for a quarter of the harvest, and die at four times the rate of miners.

- **Wants:** to keep the quarter share, to have a hunter on guard at every worksite, and to stop holders from
  delaying clears.
- **Competes over:** the share, against every rights holder, and the gate-holding law, against Helix.
- **Figure:** **Nasrin Gallo.** President. *Hook:* she can stop every harvest in San Francisco with one message, and the
  city stores four days of crystal. She has always known about the thirty reclaimers who died in Year 7.
- **A party meets it:** inside a gate, an hour after the boss dies, when a crew of twelve arrives with saws.

### The Plain Men

The Plain Men are an armed militia of unawakened people. They believe hunters rule the unawakened as an occupying
force. They make bullets tipped with ground crystal, which wound a D-rank and bruise a C-rank. *Hook:* they have a
new bullet. A B-rank is in the hunter hospital with a wound that potions do not close.
