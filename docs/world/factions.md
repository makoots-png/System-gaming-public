# Factions

Every faction below is given the same five lines: what it **wants**, what it **competes over**, its **figures**
(each with a hook), how it **recruits**, and how **a party meets it**. They are written for Carrow; each has a
cousin in every city.

Each has a long aim and a near one, and each is divided inside, because that is what makes a faction something a
party can deal with and not only fight.

## 1. What everyone competes over

| The prize | Why it is scarce | Who fights for it |
|---|---|---|
| **Seats** | 600 world gates a month in Carrow, four seats each, 12,000 hunters. A seat is gold, drops and, since the Speaking, levels | Guilds against guilds; guilds against their own hunters; the Bureau against everyone |
| **Rights** | Half of all gates are auctioned. The harvest is where a guild's margin is | Guilds, Helix, night crews |
| **Hunters** | One person in five hundred, and now any of them might be an S-rank in six years | Guilds, the Corps, foreign states, the Stair |
| **Healers** | One hunter in eight | Everybody |
| **Crystals** | The Shop sells without limit, but a crystal is ten Clears. Whoever holds one for you, holds you | Guilds, insurers, lenders |
| **Crystal (mana)** | The city's light. Nine tenths goes through one company | Helix, the Bureau exchange, fences |
| **The story** | Nobody can film a gate | Press officers, tellers, Vantage, the dead |
| **Response time** | It is what land is worth | Districts, the Bureau, any guild choosing where to build a hall |

## 2. Guilds

### Ironwake

The oldest guild in Carrow, chartered in Year 2 by a party that held the river bridges through the Break Years. Four
hundred hunters, a granite hall on Ledger Row, first refusal on a fifth of the city's auctioned gates by an
agreement older than the auction law.

- **Wants:** to stay first. Near aim: to keep the B and A gates it has always had. Long aim: to be the guild the
  first liner S-ranks belong to, so that the flood, when it comes, wears its colours.
- **Competes over:** rights (it bids to deny rivals as much as to win), healers, and the memory of the Break Years.
- **Inside:** the old guard, who think a hunter earns a seat by years, against the young captains, who can count
  levels.
- **Figures:**
  - **Tobias Reinholt, "Anvil"**, guildmaster, A-rank Tanker, 61 and looks 48. *Hook:* in Year 7 he sealed a gate
    that was about to break with thirty reclaimers still inside. It saved a district. Their families have been on
    Ironwake's pension roll ever since, and none of them know why the pension is so generous.
  - **Ines Carvalho, "Last Out"**, raid captain, B-rank Tanker. *Hook:* three levels from A, and starved of seats by
    a guildmaster who cannot afford a captain who outranks his deputies.
  - **Osric Bell**, quartermaster, unawakened, keeper of the vault. *Hook:* the vault holds eleven fewer C crystals
    than the books say.
- **Recruits:** from the academies it sponsors, with an offer inside a fortnight of a Reading; and by
  buying out anyone good from a smaller guild.
- **A party meets it:** bidding against it for a gate and winning; or being offered a week's work as a fifth
  party on a raid, to see how they stand in a line.

### The Lantern Company

A cooperative. Nine hundred hunters, almost all E to C, who own the guild in equal shares and vote on the rights it
bids for. They run the E and D gates nobody else wants, on volume, with a healer in every party because the members
voted that no gate is worth one of them.

- **Wants:** steady work and nobody dead. Long aim: a seat on the Bureau's rights board.
- **Competes over:** the cheap gates, against freelancers and against the Bureau's own crews; and for healers,
  whom it pays above its means.
- **Inside:** since the Speaking, the members who want to stay a safe E-and-D guild against the members who have
  climbed to C and B on the line and want to bid for gates that could kill their friends.
- **Figures:**
  - **Sun-hee Marlowe**, steward, C-rank Healer, 39. *Hook:* she overbid on six D gates this month to keep them from
    Ironwake, and four break on the same day. She needs a party she can trust and cannot be seen to hire.
  - **Pavel Dunmore**, a founding member, E-rank at fifty-eight, never missed a shift. *Hook:* he has a door open in
    his flat right now, one rank up, and has decided to go through it. He has not told his wife.
- **Recruits:** anyone with a licence and two members to vouch. No signing bonus; a share.
- **A party meets it:** as sub-contractors on a gate the Company cannot cover; or across a queue at the Bureau
  hall, where its members are the ones who know every clerk by name.

### Aurelian

A guild owned by a holding company, with a glass tower, a media floor, and sixty hunters the public can name.

- **Wants:** the Hundred. Near aim: three of its hunters in the list this year. Long aim: to sign the next Risen
  before they are Risen.
- **Competes over:** faces, titles, sponsors, and A gates in districts with good sight lines.
- **Inside:** the hunters who are photographed and the **second line** who clear the gates. The second line is paid
  well to be silent, and the Chronicle has made silence harder.
- **Figures:**
  - **Dax Verrault**, A-rank Assassin, the face. *Hook:* see `fame.md`. He knows what is said. He would like, once,
    to clear a gate that he is credited with.
  - **Marguerite Sato**, managing director, unawakened. *Hook:* she has a list of forty climbers under twenty-five
    and an offer for each. One of the party is on it.
  - **"Six"**, second-line Ranger, A-rank, name withheld by contract. *Hook:* wears a deed title Aurelian has paid
    eleven witnesses not to read aloud.
- **Recruits:** with money, lawyers and a photographer. Its contract is the one in `careers.md` with every trap
  switched on.
- **A party meets it:** when one of them does something on a street with cameras; or when "Six" needs people who
  are not on any payroll.

### The Ninth Hour

Forty hunters around the city's one S-rank. No bench, no academy, no press office. It takes the gates that would
otherwise end the city, and is paid by the city to exist.

- **Wants:** Carrow standing. Near aim: a second S-rank, of any origin, before Amara Nkemelu breaks. Long aim: none
  it will say.
- **Competes over:** nothing, which is its power. Everything it wants, it is given.
- **Inside:** whether the guild serves the city or the Abstention when the two differ.
- **Figures:**
  - **Amara Nkemelu, "Tidebreaker"**, S-rank Mage. *Hook:* see `fame.md`.
  - **Henrik Vale**, her second, A-rank Healer, fifty. *Hook:* he has been quietly reading every climber in the city
    for a successor, and has been told by the Bureau to stop.
- **Recruits:** by a knock on the door. Nobody applies.
- **A party meets it:** at a break, when forty people in grey arrive and the party is told, politely, where to
  stand; or when Vale knocks.

### The Gravewardens

Part guild, part registry, part order. Every licensed Necromancer in Carrow belongs, because the licence law of
Year 18 says so. A black-brick house beside the old cemetery in Saltmarket.

- **Wants:** for extraction to stay legal. Near aim: every shadow on the register. Long aim: that nobody ever asks
  what the System thinks a shadow is.
- **Competes over:** corpses (a boss's body is worth more to an extractor than to a reclaimer, and both have a
  claim), and the public's nerve.
- **Inside:** the registrars, who would ban more, against the field Necromancers, who have each stood over a dead
  friend with a skill that might keep something of them.
- **Figures:**
  - **Mireille Tazi**, Warden-Prime, B-rank Necromancer, 44. *Hook:* the register has one entry with no kill
    recorded against it. She made the entry. It is her brother.
  - **Jonah Achterberg**, registrar, unawakened. *Hook:* he can tell a registered shadow from an unregistered one
    by its paperwork alone, and he has seen three unregistered ones this month, all the same shape.
- **Recruits:** by law. A hunter whose window says Necromancer has thirty days to present themselves.
- **A party meets it:** the day one of them extracts a shadow; or when a client wants a boss's corpse left whole.

### Redline

A mercenary house: a hundred and twenty hunters on day rates, a dispatcher, and a bar. Specialists in red gates,
which cannot be left, and in everything else that ordinary guild contracts forbid.

- **Wants:** to be paid, on the day, in coin.
- **Competes over:** emergencies. A break, a sealed gate, a guild short one healer at dawn.
- **Inside:** nothing. People who disagree with Redline leave Redline.
- **Figures:**
  - **Kofi Stein**, dispatcher, B-rank Ranger, one hand. *Hook:* he sells **seat insurance**: for a fee he will
    guarantee a hunter a seat in a gate of their rank within the month. Ask him how.
  - **Dr Lale Demir**, A-rank Healer, on retainer to no one. *Hook:* the only healer in Carrow who works for
    unsigned parties. Every guild has tried to buy her. One has decided to stop asking.
- **Recruits:** from guild cast-offs, deserters from the Corps, and climbers nobody else will seat.
- **A party meets it:** the first time they need a fifth. Prices are in `economy.md` section 10.

## 3. The state

### The Gate Bureau, Carrow Office

Reads gates, reads people, keeps the Register, posts Doorwatch, auctions rights, licenses parties, collects the
levy, and runs the public gates with its own crews. Two thousand staff; forty assessors who can actually read a
gate; a hall on Ledger Row called the **Halfway House** where every new hunter in the city has stood with a hand
on the stone.

- **Wants:** no breaks. Near aim: this month's gates cleared. Long aim, since the Speaking, unspoken: to decide who
  climbs. It controls the seats in public gates, the Rank Floor, and the re-read, and it has started using all
  three.
- **Competes over:** authority, against guilds that are richer than it and S-ranks it cannot order.
- **Inside:** the field service, who want gates cleared by whoever will do it, against the watch desk, who keep a
  list of climbers and would prefer some of them slowed.
- **Figures:**
  - **Halima Voss**, director, unawakened, 58. *Hook:* she has a model of the Tide on her wall, and a second one in
    her safe. The second says that by Year 31 Carrow cannot clear its own gates without ten times the S-ranks it
    has. She is the only official in the city who wants the flood.
  - **Renzo Okabe**, chief assessor, D-rank, the best reader in the country. *Hook:* in Year 15 he read a B gate as
    C and eleven hunters died. He was right; the gate changed after he read it. Nobody believes gates can do that.
  - **Pim Aldous**, a seat clerk for E and D gates. *Hook:* takes his bribes in Health Potions and gives them all to
    a ward in Scarside. Every party in the city knows the first half of that sentence.
- **Recruits:** by examination. Assessors are found, not trained: one hunter in a thousand can feel a gate's
  height.
- **A party meets it:** every week. The queue, the licence, the door fee, the declaration.

### The Hunter Corps

Three hundred ranked regulars in Carrow's garrison and the right to call up the rest. Runs the cordons with
unawakened infantry.

- **Wants:** enough hunters at the next break. Long aim: a standing force of its own A-ranks, which guild pay makes
  impossible.
- **Competes over:** people. It can conscript for a day and cannot afford anyone for a year.
- **Figures:** **Colonel Dragan Yun**, C-rank Fighter, the garrison's commander. *Hook:* he keeps the list of who
  did not answer the last call-up. Three names on it are on Ironwake's board. He has not yet decided what the list
  is for.
- **Recruits:** the patriotic, the indebted (it pays crystal debts for a five-year term), and the convicted.
- **A party meets it:** by siren. The first call-up of a campaign should come at the worst possible moment.

### The Accord and the Abstention

Not offices, but every faction acts inside them. The **Accord** is law: its secretariat sits in Carrow, in the old
customs house, and its inspectors can enter any Bureau. The **Abstention** is a promise among the Fixed S-ranks,
kept by fear of each other. *Hook for both:* neither was written for a world in which rank can rise. The Accord's
inspectors are counting Risen. So is Tomas Reyes-Lindahl.

## 4. Corporations

### Helix Manaworks

Lit Carrow in Year 4. Buys nine tenths of its crystal, runs its reactors, owns the grid.

- **Wants:** crystal, more each year, of higher grade: the reactors need larger cores as they age. Near aim: the
  **Ripening Bill**, which would let a rights holder hold any gate to its last day by right. Long aim: its own
  hunters and its own gates, and no guilds between it and the stone.
- **Competes over:** rights, against the guilds it buys from; and the Bureau's ear.
- **Figures:**
  - **Priya Lindgren**, chief executive. *Hook:* she believes, on Helix's own data, that a gate held past a certain
    ripeness stops being that gate. She is lobbying for the Bill anyway, because the alternative is a dark city in
    Year 26.
  - **Arlo Finch**, a reactor engineer. *Hook:* he has a core that hums at 03:14 every morning, and a notebook.
- **Recruits:** cutters, engineers, and lately hunters, at guild rates plus shares.
- **A party meets it:** selling a core at its gate on Stonemarket; or hired, deniably, to hold a gate open.

### Paragon Mutual

Insures hunters (crystal cover), homes (break cover), guilds and cities.

- **Wants:** to price risk correctly, which means knowing more than the Bureau. Long aim: to be the body that
  decides where the Green line runs.
- **Competes over:** information, and crystals: its vault of them is the largest in the city.
- **Figures:**
  - **Elias Thorne**, chief actuary. *Hook:* next year's rate map withdraws break cover from two Amber districts.
    Whoever sees that map early can buy, or sell, half of Northfield.
  - **Cass Imani**, a claims assessor, E-rank. *Hook:* she denied a crystal claim last month because the hunter
    died behind a door. The hunter's party says it was a world gate, and that someone moved the body.
- **Recruits:** E-ranks who are good with numbers and bad with gates.
- **A party meets it:** the first time one of them dies; or when Imani needs people to go and look.

### Vantage

Publishes the Hundred and the Climb, owns four tellers and a betting licence.

- **Wants:** a story every week. **Figure:** **Coco Marchetti**, editor of the Hundred. *Hook:* she will put a
  climber on the Climb's front page the week they would least like the Bureau to notice them, unless given
  something better.
- **A party meets it:** the morning after their first good day.

## 5. Cults

### The Ledger, and the Patient

A church for a god that pays on time. The Ledger holds that the System is an account being kept of each hunter,
that every quest is an entry, and that a day will come when the books are read. It took its shape in Year 1, around
the Shop; the Speaking was, to its members, the first audit.

**The Patient** are its discipline: hunters who do the Daily every day and nothing else. No gates, no doors, no
blood. They began on the morning of the Speaking. Those who have never missed are level 23. They will reach S in
Year 43, and they are in no hurry.

- **Wants:** a clean book. Near aim: every hunter at the dawn session. Long aim: to still be here when the fast
  roads have killed everyone who took them.
- **Competes over:** souls, with the guilds, for the young; and with the Open Hand, for the frightened.
- **Inside:** whether killing in a gate stains the book. The founder says no. The Patient say look at us.
- **Figures:**
  - **Mother Ansel Whitlow**, C-rank, 66, leader of the Patient, eleven hundred days without a miss. *Hook:* nine
    days ago her Daily changed. It is no longer exercise. She has done it every day and told no one what it asks.
  - **Brother Tully**, the Ledger's treasurer. *Hook:* the church lends at two percent a month for crystals, a
    point under the guilds, and holds the debts of nine hundred hunters. He calls it mercy.
- **Recruits:** at dawn in Founders' Park, and at the bedsides of the raised.
- **A party meets it:** when one of them is Penalized and a stranger in grey says, kindly, that there is a way to
  never be again.

### The Open Hand

They hold that the gates are doors in the plain sense, that what lives behind them is fleeing something, and that
every Clear is a massacre of refugees. They are few, gentle in person, and responsible for the deaths of about two
hundred people in Carrow.

- **Wants:** a gate to break in peace, with nobody to fight what comes out, to prove it would not attack. Near aim:
  to hide one for a week.
- **Competes over:** unfound gates, against spotters and neighbours.
- **Figures:** **"Uncle" Baptiste Roux**, unawakened, a retired schoolteacher. *Hook:* he lost a class of
  eight-year-olds on the seventh day of the Long Week, and he has seen something come out of a gate and sit down.
  He is not lying about either.
- **Recruits:** the bereaved.
- **A party meets it:** in a cellar, on day six of a D gate nobody reported, with twelve people sitting in front
  of it holding hands.

### The Mute

Hunters who have stopped answering. No Daily, no quests, no doors. They live Penalized, every day, as a vow, and
say that after a year the voice goes quiet. *Hook:* one of them, **Sister Odile Brant**, an A-rank of the Fixed,
says her window has gone dark altogether, and that she is still as strong as she was. If true, power does not need
the System. Every faction in this file would want to know.

## 6. Criminals

### The Stair

The seat market. A hunter pays two Clears of a gate's rank and walks in behind a party that has been paid to carry
them; they leave with the levels and nothing else. The Stair also forges licences, fences drops at 40% where the
Shop pays 25, launders gold through runners, and lends for crystals at five percent a month.

- **Wants:** the ladder kept short and the law kept in the way. Every restriction on seats is its margin.
- **Competes over:** gates, with the guilds it bribes for back-door seats; and debtors, with the church.
- **Inside:** the old fences, who liked a quiet trade in rings and daggers, against the seat-brokers, who are
  making S-ranks and will soon not need anyone's protection.
- **Figures:**
  - **Auntie Rosalind Quill**, unawakened, seventy, a pawnbroker on Tidewall. *Hook:* she holds the crystal debts
    of three hundred hunters and has never once called one in. She is waiting for a day, and she knows which.
  - **Marek "Landing" Osei**, seat-broker, D-rank on the Register. *Hook:* he has not been re-read since Year 17.
    He is not a D-rank.
- **Recruits:** the indebted, the impatient and the children of the rich.
- **A party meets it:** selling a drop they would rather not explain; or when the only seat this month is one of
  Landing's.

### Night crews

Reclaimers who strip a gate they hold no rights to, between its Clear and the holder's crew arriving, or before
its Clear with a bought hunter to hold the door. *Hook:* **Nasrin Gallo's** union (below) says night crews are
scabs. Half of them are her members on a second shift, and she knows it.

### The Fallen

Hunters who kill hunters inside gates, where nothing records and the Ebb takes the evidence. Some rob. Some are
paid. Since the Speaking some do it because the Chronicle counts it. They wear dark titles and cannot take them
off, so they work masked from other hunters' eyes, or among the unawakened, who cannot read them. *Hook:* a party
finds a body in a gate that was posted as unentered.

## 7. The unawakened

### The Once-Born League

A civil rights movement for the 499. Founded outside a hospital in Year 9.

- **Wants:** equal standing for those who die once. Near aim: a crystal trial on an unawakened volunteer, public,
  to prove it fails or to prove it does not. Long aim: that hunters answer to courts they cannot walk out of.
- **Competes over:** the city council, where it holds eleven of forty seats, and the levy, which it wants doubled.
- **Inside:** the constitutional wing against the people who have noticed that kindling follows breaks.
- **Figures:**
  - **Councillor Teodora Baskin**, its leader in Carrow. *Hook:* her son kindled last week. The 72 hours have
    passed. He is in her attic with a fever and a window, and the fine is 300 gold she could pay tomorrow.
  - **Dr Imre Solis**, a cell-age clinician. *Hook:* he has a method that makes a crystal *begin* to take on an
    unawakened body. It needs a hunter to die beside the patient. He has not published.
- **Recruits:** at funerals.
- **A party meets it:** as the hunters it pickets; or as the hunters Baskin can ask for something no hunter should
  be asked.

### Reclaimers' Local 7

The carcass and crystal crews: eleven thousand members, the only workers who go into gates without powers. They
work for a quarter of the harvest and die at four times the rate of miners.

- **Wants:** the quarter kept, a hunter at the door of every worksite, and no ripe gates. **Competes over:** the
  lay, with every rights holder; and the Ripening Bill, with Helix.
- **Figures:** **Nasrin Gallo**, president. *Hook:* she can stop every harvest in Carrow with one message, and the
  city has four days of crystal in store. She knows about the thirty in Year 7. She has always known.
- **A party meets it:** in the gate, an hour after the boss falls, when twelve people with saws walk past them
  into the dark.

### The Plain Men

Militia. Unawakened, armed, convinced that hunters are an occupying class and the Speaking was the moment they
became a species. They make rounds tipped with ground crystal, which work on a D-rank and annoy a C. *Hook:* they
have a new round. A B-rank is in the Crown's hunter hospital with a wound that potions will not close.
