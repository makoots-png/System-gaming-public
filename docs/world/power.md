# Power: awakening, rank, lifespan, the System

Tables between `<!-- prices:... -->` marks are printed by `bun docs/world/prices.ts --write` from the game's own
numbers (`xpPerLevel`, `rankLevels`, the daily's XP, a monster's XP by rank, the body's growth by level).

## 1. What makes a hunter

**The Kindling.** A fever of three days, 40 degrees and over, with a blue cast to the veins of the wrists on the
second day. Then it breaks, and the person can see a window nobody else can see. About one person in five hundred
has kindled. It comes most often between fourteen and thirty, has come to a child of six and a woman of seventy-one,
and has never come twice.

What is known about who:

- **It does not follow blood.** Two hunters' children kindle no more often than anyone's. Guild families hate this
  and spend fortunes pretending otherwise.
- **It follows gates.** A person who lives through a break within sight of it is about eight times as likely to
  kindle in the following year. This is why Scarside, the poorest district in Carrow, has the most hunters a street,
  and why some parents move toward the sirens.
- **It does not follow merit**, fitness, faith or need. Every study that found a pattern failed to repeat.

What a hunter gets, at once: a body that mana runs through (stronger, faster, slower to tire, harder to kill); an
**Inventory**, a space only they can reach; the **Shop**; and, since the Speaking, the **window**: a level, eight
stats, quests, and a voice.

## 2. What awakening costs

- **The fever kills** about one in sixty, more among the old. There is no treatment; potions do nothing until it
  breaks.
- **The register.** 72 hours to report to a Bureau hall. Your letter is public for life. Your employer, your
  landlord and your insurer will know by the weekend.
- **The call-up.** Any registered hunter can be ordered to a break. Refusal is a crime and, at a break, visible.
- **Ordinary insurance ends.** Life, health and motor policies void on kindling. Hunters buy hunter cover.
- **Ordinary sport, ordinary courts, ordinary jobs.** No league will field you. A hunter's assault is charged as
  armed. Many employers will not put a D-rank on a shop floor, because of what a bad day would cost.
- **The Daily.** Since the Speaking, the System sets every hunter a quest each day (the famous one: a hundred
  push-ups, a hundred sit-ups, a hundred squats, ten kilometres). Finish it and you grow. Miss it and you are
  **Penalized**: a day of everything being harder, which in a gate is how people die. It cannot be switched off.
- **The doors.** A personal gate opens for you every eight hours, wherever you are, and waits a day. You do not have
  to enter. You do have to walk past it: in your kitchen, in your child's school, on a tram.
- **Everyone you love ages faster than you.** See section 5.

## 3. Rank is level

Before the Speaking a hunter was read once and was that letter for life. The System has now shown the number that
was under the letter all along, and the number moves.

| Rank | From level | What it means in the street |
|---|---|---|
| E | 1 | Stronger than any athlete, and killable by a dog-sized monster on a bad day. Two in five hunters. |
| D | 10 | Bullet-resistant in good gear. The working rank: a third of all hunters. |
| C | 20 | A one-person platoon. Small arms are an annoyance. The rank at which a state starts refusing exit permits. |
| B | 35 | Walks through a wall. A city's B-ranks are known by name to its police. |
| A | 50 | A national asset: a couple of hundred in a city the size of Carrow. Can end a C break alone. |
| S | 70 | A strategic fact. About one for every five million people. Each is named in other states' defence papers. |

- **The Daily Road.** Twenty-five XP a day, every day, and nothing else. Safe, free, and twenty-six years long.
  Nobody has walked it for more than three; those who have never missed (the Patient, `factions.md`) are level 23.
- **The line.** What a guild's ordinary raider does: gates one rank under their own, in a full party, with a healer.
  About six years from E to S on a gate and a half a month. **The first liners who began at the Speaking are due at
  S around Year 23.** Everyone who plans anything knows this date.
- **The climb.** Gates at your own rank, a small party, every week, and the doors. Under a year and a half from E
  to S. This is the pace of a table's party. In the world a hunter who lives like this is called a **climber**, is
  watched by three guilds and the Bureau, and has roughly the life expectancy of a wartime pilot.

**The doors** are the System's own offer: three a day, most at your rank, one in four below it, about one in seven
above. Each is one or two monsters and no boss, and pays a quarter of a world gate's share. About half of those who
walk through one at their own rank lose. Losing is usually crawling out; sometimes not. Nobody can follow you in,
so a hunter who dies behind a door stays there unless they carried their own crystal.

## 4. Why S is still rare, and why it will not stay so

For seventeen years rank was a lottery ticket, and S came up for one hunter in ten thousand. Those are the
**Fixed**: about fourteen hundred S-ranks on Earth, most of them over forty, who built the guilds, the Abstention
and the price of everything.

For three years rank has been a ladder. The ladder is short (the table above) and the only things that slow it are
**seats** (a gate has room for a party, and guilds and the Bureau decide whose), **nerve** (the fast roads kill),
and **law** (the Rank Floor of Year 18: nobody enters a gate more than one rank above their own, written to stop
seat-selling and used to stop climbers).

So the world is at a hinge, and a campaign set in Year 20 stands on it:

- The Fixed are being joined by the **Risen**: a few dozen so far, all young, all climbers, none of them party to
  anything the Fixed agreed.
- Guilds ration seats to raise their own and starve their rivals. A guild seat is now worth more than its gold.
- States are quietly deciding whether they want ten times as many S-ranks or would prefer to stop the ladder, and
  whether anyone can.
- Nobody knows what the System does when the first of them passes whatever comes after seventy.

## 5. How long each rank lives, and why

Mana slows ageing in proportion to how much of it a body holds, and how much it holds grows with level exactly as
the body's toughness does (the game's growth, 1 + (level − 1) / 24). A hunter at level 25 has twice the body and
ages one year in two. This was measured for seventeen years on the Fixed, by the clinics that count cell-age, and
has been confirmed on climbers since: **levels are years.**

Read the fifth column as "if nothing kills them", which for a working hunter is a large if. The honest figures a
GM should hold:

- **Nobody has seen any of this to the end.** The oldest S-rank alive is ninety-one, kindled at seventy-one, and has aged five years in the twenty since. The figures
  are projections from twenty years of data.
- **Unawakened life expectancy is about 74**, down from the old world's, because of breaks and what they did to
  medicine's supply lines.
- **A working E-rank lives less long than a clerk.** No slowing of age, and a trade that kills. The median E-rank
  who stays active dies, for good, at 58.
- **Rising late still helps.** Ageing slows from the day the level is reached. A hunter who reaches S at sixty has
  about seventy-five more years in a body that will take that long to turn eighty.

What it does to the world: a prison sentence of "life" for a B-rank is a hundred and thirty years, and no state has
built the prison. Pension funds refuse hunters. An A-rank's marriage vows are usually rewritten. The Fixed S-ranks
expect to see the twenty-third century, and plan like it; an elected government plans for four years. And since the
Speaking, every hunter who looks at an ageing parent, or a mirror, knows that years can be earned, and where.

## 6. Jobs, shadows, titles and feats

**Jobs.** The System names what a hunter is: Fighter, Assassin, Mage, Tanker, Healer, Ranger, Necromancer. Before
the Speaking these were guesses made by guild trainers; now the window says it. About one hunter in eight is a
Healer and every party wants one, which is why a healer's retainer is half again anyone's. About one in three
hundred is a **Necromancer**.

**Shadows.** A Necromancer can extract a shadow from something they have killed (three tries a corpse; harder the
further it outranks them; harder again for a boss) and keep it in a roster that fights for them. The law, since Year
18: extraction needs a licence; every shadow is entered in the **Gravewardens'** registry; and no shadow may be
taken from a human being. The System has never refused the last one. The law does.

**Titles and feats.** Since the Speaking the System notices things. When a hunter does something it counts as a
**feat**, it writes it in their **Chronicle**, and for some feats it grants a **title** with a small real power.
A title cannot be bought, forged or hidden from another hunter's window. In a world where nobody can film a gate
(`fame.md`), titles are the only proof of what happened inside one, and a hunter's titles are read out before their
name.

**The Shop and the Inventory** are in `economy.md`.

## 7. Gates

- **World gates** open where they open, for everyone. The Bureau reads them (E to S), posts them, and sells or
  licenses them. Inside is a place: a warren, a cavern, a drowned temple, a fortress, never the same twice. A pack,
  and a boss. Kill the boss and the gate is **cleared**.
- **The break day.** A gate left alone breaks: E on day 7, D on 6, C on 5, B on 4, A on 3, S on 2. Everything in it
  comes out. A cleared gate does not break; it stands open and empty until the day it would have, then closes (the
  **Ebb**). Reclaimers work in that window.
- **Ripening.** The crystal thickens as the break nears. So does what lives there: a ripe gate's boss is the same
  rank and meaner. Holders wait. See `economy.md` section 8.
- **Red gates** (about one in ten) seal behind the party. Nobody leaves and nobody follows until the boss is dead or
  the party is. Everything inside is a notch harder. A red gate cannot be told from an ordinary one from outside.
- **Double gates** (about one in twenty) have a second place under the first: a door behind the boss's room, a
  different letter on the other side, nothing the Reading saw. The oldest guild rule: if a cleared gate has another
  door, you vote before anyone touches it.
- **Personal doors** are section 3's. The Bureau cannot read them, sell them or tax their harvest; they have none.
  They leave nothing behind but gold and, one time in four, an item.
- **A world gate run alone** pays the whole pot to one hunter: four Clears. It is legal. Guild contracts forbid it.
  Most of the Risen did it for a living.
- **The standing gate.** Port Sarn's S gate has never closed. A handful of others have not either. Nobody knows what
  makes a gate stand. The Scar in Carrow has one, a B, in the basement of a flooded car park: see `seeds.md`.

## 8. What the System is

**What people believe.** Polls in Carrow, Year 20: a third say a machine left by someone else; a quarter say God or
a god; a fifth say the Earth itself; the rest say a weapon, a test, or that they would rather not. The System has
been asked directly, by millions of hunters, and has never answered a question about itself. It answers questions
about quests.

**What every hunter knows from use:**

- It is never wrong about a number and never late with a payment.
- It keeps its word exactly. A quest's reward is what the quest said. A penalty is what the penalty said.
- It speaks plainly, without warmth or malice, in the hunter's own first language.
- It was silent for seventeen years and then it was not, and it has never said why.
- It rewards risk. Fewer hunters in a gate, more gold each. A gate above you, more of everything.

**Five truths.** Pick one, or two that do not agree, and never tell the table which.

1. **The Lifeboat.** The System is what is left of the last world the gates ate. Its makers lost, and built a
   machine to give the next world what they lacked: time (the break clock is the machine holding the doors shut as
   long as it can), arms (the Shop), and soldiers (the Kindling). It was silent for seventeen years because it was
   spent; the Speaking is the last of its reserves. *What it changes:* the System is on your side and it is dying.
   Quests get stranger. One day the Shop has one fewer shelf.
2. **The Farm.** Gates are feed. A hunter is a crop: every level is mana a body stores, and something intends to
   collect. The Fixed were a first planting that grew too slowly, so the farmer added the ladder. Long life is not
   a gift, it is keeping the stock on the hoof. *What it changes:* the higher a hunter climbs, the nearer the
   harvest. The first Risen to pass seventy does not come back from a door.
3. **The Treaty.** Two powers were at war before there was an Earth. The gates are one side's invasion; the System
   is the other side's answer; and there are rules, enforced by something older than both: a door must give notice
   (the break day), a defender must be paid (gold), a death may be appealed (the crystal). *What it changes:*
   everything in the game is a clause, and clauses can be argued. Somewhere there is a court. A hunter can have
   standing in it.
4. **The Immune Response.** The planet is alive in a way nobody suspected, the gates are an infection, and hunters
   are antibodies. The System is how a world thinks when it has to: slowly at first, then, once the infection was
   understood, with the Speaking. *What it changes:* the System does not care about people, only about the body.
   An order may come to let a city go.
5. **The Game.** It is exactly what it looks like. Someone built a game, with levels and loot and dailies, and
   connected it to a real world, and then stopped paying attention. The gates are content. The seventeen silent
   years were an outage. The Speaking was somebody finding the server still running and pressing a button. *What it
   changes:* there are other buttons. There are people who would kill for the console, and a few who have seen it.

**Signs to seed, whichever is true:** a quest that names a place nobody has found; a title in a language no window
translates; a hunter whose Daily changed one morning to something that is not exercise; the same sentence turning
up in three unrelated Chronicles; a door that opens for someone who never kindled.
