# The count: the opening mission, version B

For the owner. It is set in the world of Solo Leveling, with people and places of our own. It is written for four or five D-rank hunters at level 12 with no job, and a GM, over two sessions of about four hours and the first five minutes of a third.

The players will call it the pool job. The GM's name for the place under it is the well. Do not say that name at the table until a player does.

## 1. For the owner

### The premise

The hunters are D rank and they will die D rank, like everyone they know. The work they can get is a day of piece rate for Tri-County Reclaim. An E-rank gate has opened at the bottom of a drained public swimming pool, and the company pays six gold for every crystal shard they dig out of the goblin warren inside. Their crew lead, Odell Brandt, has been level 19 for twenty-two years. He is the strongest D-rank hunter in the district and he is paid the same six gold. In the lowest chamber the floor is a single sheet of crystal worth two hundred shards, and they dig it. It was the only thing between that room and what is under every room. They fall into a shaft too wide to see across, lined with every dungeon there is, and something in it counts what it holds every five hours. It counts them, finds them a row too deep, and corrects the mistake.

### What is truly wrong in this dungeon

The players can piece this together. Nobody says it to them.

1. Every dungeon is one room in the wall of a single shaft. The rooms sit in rows, and a room's row is its rank. An E-rank gate opens on the top row. Hunters have only ever seen rooms from the front door.
2. A rank is where the System has a thing filed. It files a hunter once, on the day they awaken, and it has never had a reason to look again. A gate is a visitor's pass. It lets a hunter into a room and returns them to their own row when they leave. A hunter who goes into a room below their row gets the squeeze, and dies of it in hours, because the pass lets them in and does nothing about the depth.
3. Long ago something came up through this column of rooms from far below and broke a hole in every floor. The stone around each hole is bent upward. In the top room the hole healed over with crystal. The Association's meter reads this gate as E because the room is E. Tri-County pays a deep-grade rate for the crystal in its floor, and nobody at Tri-County has asked why an E-rank room grows it.
4. In the shaft there is no gate and so no pass. Whatever is there is contents. Every five hours a line of light climbs the shaft and counts what holds still. When it finds a thing on the wrong row it corrects the record and then the body, so that the body can live where it was found. The System builds the difference out of whatever the thing has in its hands.
5. Each row holds a fixed number. For every hunter moved in, one thing is moved up a row to make room.

### Why this dungeon alone lets a hunter pass their rank

A hunter's rank never changes because a hunter is never found anywhere except where they are filed. This is the one room with a way out the back. A hunter who stands still in the shaft, one row below their own, when the count comes, is re-filed at that row and remade to fit it. The count asks no one's consent, and it does the same to a centipede.

### Why a hunter gets a class there

Every entry in the count has a rank and a kind. A hunter filed at awakening has a rank and a blank where the kind goes, and the Job Change the Association knows is the System filling that blank for a few hunters, for reasons of its own. The count does not leave blanks. It reads a kind from what it finds: what is in the hunter's two hands, then what they are doing, then what is on their back. Then it makes the entry true. What was in their hands is gone afterward, and they can do what it was for.

So the class is decided by a question the GM asks aloud at every count: "What is in your hands?" The players answer it carelessly the first time. Once they have seen what the answer does, they plan around it. They have a lamp, a rope, a sick surveyor and a sack of shards to hold, and two hands each.

### What is never answered

The story never says what came up through the floors or where it went, who cut the steps, or what the things are that move during the count. It does not say why one window is lit eleven rows below S, or why the System began filing people twelve years ago. Give none of them a name or a stat block that matters.

### The shape and the length

Chapters 1 and 2 are a bad shift at a bad job. In chapter 3 the job goes wrong. Chapters 4 to 8 are the well, where the fear is size and procedure and there is very little blood. Chapters 9 and 10 are the way out, through the back of a C-rank dungeon that belongs to somebody else. Chapter 11 is five minutes at the start of the next session.

Session one is chapters 1 to 6 and ends on the first count. Session two is chapters 7 to 10.

## 2. Before the first session

"Exists" means the game has it today. Everything else is made in a maker, about forty minutes in all.

### The people

- Odell Brandt, 51, D rank, level 19 since he was 29. Crew lead for Tri-County. His gear is worth more than his house. He is patient with new hunters and he checks their buckles. Run him as a Fighter with a Steel Longsword (exists), played from the GM's phone.
- Nell Vance, 26, E rank, the Association's surveyor for this gate. She carries a Hask meter, which reads how deep a gate is, and a spare. She does not fight.
- Lorna, Tri-County's dispatcher, a voice on the radio who writes down shard counts.
- Renata Cole, A rank, a raid captain of the Ironvane guild. Chapter 10 only.

### The gates

On the board: "Dorrance Park pool", rank E, type normal, 9 world days old. Monsters: Goblin 8, Goblin Shaman 1. Make a second gate and keep it hidden until chapter 9: "Kessler Avenue overpass", rank C, type normal, 3 days old.

## 3. The mission

### Chapter 1: No diving

**Read aloud.**

> The Dorrance Park Aquatic Center closed for the season in September, and the city drained the big pool. The gate opened at the bottom of the deep end nine days ago. It is the size of a garage door and the color of a pilot light. It stands on the drain, under a sign painted on the tile that says NO DIVING.
>
> At the front desk a woman in a rec center polo has each of you sign the visitor log. She gives you a paper sticker for your jacket. It says VISITOR, with a line for your name. She asks you not to track mud through the lobby on your way out.
>
> Tri-County Reclaim has a folding table on the pool deck, and the terms are on a laminated card. Six gold a shard, bagged and counted. The lamp is a rental and it comes out of your pay. Lose the lamp and you owe fifteen. The Association pays forty for the same shard, at a window where Tri-County has the contract and you never will.
>
> Your crew lead is Odell Brandt. He is fifty-one, and he has been level 19 since he was twenty-nine. He checks every buckle on your harness as if this gate could hurt you. Then he goes down the pool ladder first.

**What is really happening.** Nothing is wrong yet. The work of this chapter is to make the players feel the world's law, and Brandt does that. If a player asks, he tells it flatly while he coils rope: "Twenty-two years. I kill something and the window says zero. I can lift the back of a sedan. I walked into a C gate once on a dare and lasted forty seconds before my nose went. They call that the squeeze. Your letter is how deep you can go." He keeps any bitterness away from a crew. He has a framed letter at home from the Association thanking him for his service at rank D.

Nell reads the gate from the pool deck: E, one point two. She writes it on a form. Plant three things and do not lean on any of them: the VISITOR stickers, NO DIVING, and the depth painted on the pool wall, 12 FT.

**The decisions.**
- Who carries the lamp. There is one. Whoever signs for it has a hand full for the rest of the story.
- Who clips onto the home line, a 120 ft rope Brandt anchors at the gate. It is slow and it snags, and an E-rank gate does not need it. Brandt clips on.
- Whether to sign the card as written. Clause 9 gives Tri-County everything a crew recovers below the survey line. Nell can tell them the survey line is the gate. A hunter who strikes the clause is paid five a shard.

**The app.** Give each hunter a quest from the GM, "Piece rate": Bag shards, 0 of 40, reward 6 gold a shard paid at the end. Let the players tick their own count. One Tri-County lamp, one Tri-County radio and the home line go into bags (item maker, type misc).

**Ready to end when** every hunter holds the quest and someone holds the lamp.

**How to frighten.** Leave it for later. Be as dull as the laminated card, and dock someone two gold for a missing glove.

### Chapter 2: Piece rate

**Read aloud.**

> Inside is a goblin warren, low and wet, and it smells like a basement after a flood. The goblins are thin and they have already lost. Most of them run. The ones that fight are trying to get past you.
>
> The shards are in the walls, each about as long as a thumb, pale blue. You dig one out with the pick, wipe it on your leg and drop it in the sack. Then you say your number out loud, because Lorna on the radio writes the numbers down, and Lorna does not take a number she did not hear. By the second hour your knees are soaked through. Brandt has forty-one. He has not swung his pick hard once.
>
> At the back of the warren the tunnels all slope the same way. The goblins that run from you run uphill, toward the gate, every time. You have not seen one go down.

**What is really happening.** The goblins know what the bottom of their room is, and they would rather meet hunters. The shards grow thicker with every yard of slope because mana leaks up through the floor of the lowest chamber. Every kill pays the party XP and pays Brandt nothing, and he says "zero" under his breath each time, from habit.

**The decisions.**
- The Shaman. Tri-County pays a clear bonus of 100 gold a head if the boss is dead by five. A gate starts to close when its boss dies, and it takes about an hour. Kill it now and the clock is running while they dig. Leave it and Lorna asks about it every ten minutes.
- The runners. Tri-County docks 20 gold for each goblin that reaches the pool deck. Chasing one costs the chaser a quarter hour of digging.
- Whose count is whose. One player will find a vein. Brandt's rule is that a vein belongs to whoever found it. The card says nothing about that.

**The app.** A fight: Goblin 6. A second, when they choose it: Goblin Shaman 1, Goblin 2. Put a Mana Crystal Shard (exists) in a bag for every shard a player calls. When the Shaman dies, send every phone a window titled "CLEAR CONDITION MET". This is the last ordinary window in the story.

**Ready to end when** the party's count passes 40, or the Shaman is dead and they start down the slope.

**How to frighten.** Say once, in one sentence, that no goblin has gone downhill. If a player sends their hunter down to look, there is a round chamber and it is empty.

### Chapter 3: The floor

**Read aloud.**

> The lowest chamber is round, about the size of a school gym, and nothing lives in it. The floor is one sheet of blue crystal, ridged and cloudy. Around the edge, where it meets the rock, the crystal curls upward like the skin on a can of paint.
>
> Nell Vance holds her meter at her chest and it reads E, one point two. She crouches to tie her boot and it reads one point nine. She stays crouched, looking at it.
>
> Brandt taps the floor with the butt of his pick. It rings. The ring goes on longer than it should, and it seems to travel down. "That's two hundred shards," he says. "That's two hundred shards in one room."
>
> Lorna comes on the radio. She says the deep-grade rate is fourteen.

**What is really happening.** The floor is the scab over the hole. It is about a foot thick at the edge and a hand thick in the middle. The meter reads deeper near the floor because the floor is the top of the shaft.

**The decisions.**
- Dig or stop. Nell's procedure is to stop work on a reading that moves, and to log it from outside. Tri-County's card says a crew that stops before the count is called in forfeits the day. Every hunter stands to lose what they dug since morning. Brandt looks at the card for a while and starts digging at the edge, where it is thick.
- Where to dig. The middle comes up in plates as wide as a door, ten shards to a swing. The edge is slow.
- Who is roped. The home line reaches this chamber with twenty feet to spare.

Ask each player how many swings they take and where. Write it down. When the party has taken six swings in the middle, or twelve anywhere, the floor goes.

> It breaks all at once, the way a windshield does. For one second you are standing on a sheet of cracks with light coming up through them, and it is the wrong color for daylight.

**The app.** Send every phone a window titled "NOTICE" with the words "Pass ended." Put No pass on every sheet. Each hunter loses a quarter of their HP in the fall. A hunter on the home line loses none. The line holds for a moment, then the anchor comes out of the rock, and they land last with the rope. Whoever dug most in the middle lands with the sack of deep-grade shards (item maker: material, 90 gold at the Association's window) in their hands.

**Ready to end when** they have fallen.

**How to frighten.** Let the ring last. Read the window's two words and add nothing. If someone asks what pass, say their hunter has never seen that window before, and neither has Brandt.

### Chapter 4: The drowned room

**Read aloud.**

> You land in water. It is waist deep and warm, and the floor under it is rubble from the ceiling you came through. Your lamp shows a hall of pillars, flooded to the same height all the way back. The hole is high above you, with a ragged edge of blue around it.
>
> There is a second hole, in the floor of this room, straight under the first. It is wide enough to drop a bus through. The water pours over its edge, and you cannot hear it land. The stone around that hole is bent upward.
>
> Every one of you feels well. The ache from the dig is gone and your breath comes easily. Brandt rolls his bad shoulder and it does not click.
>
> Nell's nose is bleeding. She has not noticed yet.

**What is really happening.** This is a D-rank room, one row down, and no gate has opened on it in a very long time. The hunters are D rank, so they are in place here, and being in place feels good. Nell is E rank and has begun to die. Brandt knows the squeeze when he sees it. He looks from her to the hole in the ceiling and says, "She's E. So this is a D room." Explain no more than that.

One wall is broken where a pillar fell through it. Air moves through the gap.

**The decisions.**
- The climb. It is sixty feet up an overhang of wet crystal to a room that is closing. A hard check for D rank (DC 20) gets one hunter halfway, far enough to see the light in the chamber above start to flicker.
- The Lizardmen. They stand between the party and the broken wall. The party can fight them or wade around through the dark half of the hall with the lamp shut.
- Nell. Someone has to keep her on her feet. She has two stacks of Squeezed and gains one an hour.

**The app.** A fight: Lizardman 3, Hobgoblin 2. Pay the XP in full. It is the last easy fight. Put Squeezed at 2 on Nell if she has a sheet.

**Ready to end when** the fight is over, or avoided, and someone steps through the broken wall.

**How to frighten.** Tell them how good they feel, and mean it. The second hole needs one sentence. If a player asks which way the stone is bent, say it again in the same words.

### Chapter 5: The tip

**Read aloud.**

> The broken wall lets you out onto a ledge forty feet wide. Past the ledge there is nothing.
>
> You are standing on the inside of a shaft. The far side is too far away to see as a wall. You see it as lights, small and blue, in rows, hundreds to a row, and the rows go all the way around. There are rows above you and rows below you. The ones below go down until they are too faint to count. Every light is the color of a gate.
>
> The ledge is a ramp. It runs along the wall and down, past the next blue light about six hundred paces off, and on around the shaft. It has steps cut in it. Each step is as high as your hip, and each is worn in the middle.
>
> Above you, close, the light of the room you fell out of goes dark. Then the room empties. Everything in it comes down past the ledge: the dead goblins, the picks, the sacks, Tri-County's folding chairs. It takes a long time to stop.

**What is really happening.** When a gate closes, its room is tipped out the back. This is where the bodies go that nobody carried out of a closing gate. Under every window the ledge holds a drift of what fell and caught: on top, hunter gear from the last twelve years, and under that, older things.

Most windows are dark. Brandt works it out by looking at the room they left: "Lit means open. That one's somebody's gate, right now." On their own row the nearest lit window is more than two hundred windows around, which is days on foot with no food. One row down and thirty-one windows along, there is a lit one. It is six hours' walk, and one row down is C.

**The decisions.**
- Wait or walk. A dark window near them might light in an hour or in a year. There is water in the drowned room and nothing to eat. Walking means six hours of the squeeze for everyone but Nell, who will have it worse.
- What to take from the drift. Section 5 has the list. Everything has a weight, and whatever they carry fills a hand.
- Nell. She cannot walk six hours. Carrying her takes one hunter's two hands. She asks nobody to do it.

**The app.** Send every phone a window titled "NOTICE": "Room closed. Contents removed: 43. Not found: 7." Make the second number the hunters plus two. On the board the pool gate is now closed. Award the drift's items as they are picked up.

**Ready to end when** they have chosen a direction and a load.

**How to frighten.** Give the size in paces and windows, which they can count. Then stop talking and let somebody work out who the seven are.

### Chapter 6: The first count

**Read aloud.**

> It starts as a sound, low, from a long way down the shaft. You feel it in your back teeth before you hear it. It rises. On the far wall a line of white light is climbing, level, the whole way around, at about the speed a person walks upstairs.
>
> The centipedes in the drift stop, all of them at once, in the middle of a step. They turn to face the wall and they hold there.
>
> The line reaches the row below you. Then it is at your feet.

Stop reading there. Ask each player in turn, in these words: "What is in your hands? Are you moving or are you still?" Write the answers down. Explain nothing. Then:

> The light goes through you like a camera flash with no click. While it is on you every edge is sharp. You can see each thread in your sleeve and each hair on the back of Brandt's hand. Then it is above you, climbing.
>
> Nell was holding her meter in both hands. Her hands are still held that way, and they are empty. Her nose has stopped bleeding. She looks down the shaft and says, "Two point zero." She shuts her eyes and says it again. "I can't make it stop reading."
>
> On the far side of the shaft, on your row, something very small comes out of a window and starts up the ramp toward the row above.

**What is really happening.** The count found one thing over on row 2. It moved Nell in, and it moved one thing up to make room. Nell is D rank now and her kind is Sounder. She has become the instrument she was holding. The hunters were in place, so the count had nothing to correct in them.

Brandt understands before anyone. He says nothing for a minute. Then he asks Nell, very politely, how far down C is.

**The decisions.** Nobody knows these are decisions yet.
- Still or moving. A hunter who walked or swung a pick through the pass was not counted.
- The centipedes. For the minute the light takes to pass they do not defend themselves. Each is 25 XP.

**The app.** A hunter who was still gets a window on their phone alone: "COUNT: Found in place." Put Counted on their sheet. A hunter who moved gets no window. Then every phone gets "COUNT: Row 2. Filed 9,602. Found 9,603. Over 1," and after it "CORRECTED: 1 moved in. 1 moved up." If someone attacks the centipedes, run it as a fight: Giant Centipede 6, and pass every one of their turns.

**Ready to end when** a player says aloud what happened to Nell. End the session there.

**How to frighten.** When the light arrives, stop moving yourself. Hold it for ten seconds of real time and answer nobody. Let them see which phones lit and which did not.

### Chapter 7: Below your row

**Read aloud.**

> One row down, the air has weight. It sits on your chest like a hand. Brandt has a thread of blood on his lip. He keeps touching it and looking at his finger, and he looks pleased.
>
> The drift is older here. Under the hunter gear there are things made for other hands: a hook on a pole nine feet long, a buckle the size of a dinner plate.
>
> Ahead of you the ramp is gone for thirty feet. A lip of stone a boot wide runs along the wall, and under it is the shaft. On the far side of the gap a High Orc is sitting against the wall with its eyes shut.
>
> Then each of you gets the same window, and this one has a clock on it.

**What is really happening.** The count is twelve minutes off, and the System is telling its contents so, as it tells them everything. The hunters are at one stack of Squeezed, two if they dawdled. Brandt means to stand in the open for it with everything he owns in his hands.

Everything the party needs takes hands. The gap needs a rope and somebody to anchor it. The lamp must be held or they cross in the dark. Nell, squeezed again, must be carried. The sack is worth more than their month. Each hunter has a weapon, and there is an orc.

**The decisions.**
- Stand or move. The count corrects a hunter who holds still. The squeeze lifts, the letter changes, and the count takes whatever is in their hands and uses it. A hunter who keeps moving stays as they are, keeps the squeeze, and learns what else moves during a count.
- What to hold. Section 4 says what each answer makes.
- Whether to break. A hunter who is still can watch a friend be dragged toward the edge and do nothing for three rounds. If they act, they have moved.
- The orc. It holds still for the count like everything else. It is 250 XP with its eyes shut. Killing it means moving.
- Nell. If her carrier stands, the count takes them both. If her carrier runs, the things on the ramp are chasing two people on one pair of legs.

When the clock runs out, ask the question, one player at a time, slowly. Then:

> Something is coming along the ramp behind you, and it is moving while nothing else does. Then there are more of them. Each one is about the size of a person carrying another person. They make no sound on the stone. They go past the ones who are holding still.

And for each hunter who stood:

> It takes about as long as a sneeze. The weight comes off your chest. What you were holding is gone, and your hands still feel it. A window opens with your rank on it, and the letter is wrong.

**The app.** The clock is an urgent quest sent to every phone: "COUNT", one objective, "Hold still, 0 of 1", due in 12 real minutes, missed line "Not found." At the count, run a fight of three rounds. Put Still on each hunter who stands and add two Unfiled for each hunter who moves. A player may drop Still on any turn by saying so, and then the count has missed them. A hunter who goes down among the Unfiled is carried 60 ft down the ramp each round. At the end of round 3, Remove the Unfiled.

For each hunter corrected, complete their quest, put Counted on their sheet, clear Squeezed, and set their level to 20 with the GM's level edit, which makes them C rank. Fill their HP and mana, and take what they held out of their bag. Send their phone "CORRECTED: Rank C. Kind: Fighter", or whichever kind it is, and give the kind as a job with its skills and title in one award. Then every phone gets "COUNT: Row 3. Filed 4,118. Over 4," with the number who stood, and "CORRECTED: 4 moved in. 4 moved up." If anyone moved, add "Not found: 1. Recount." Read the next lines with the same number.

> Across the shaft, on your row, four small things come out of four windows. They start up the ramp, one behind another.

**Ready to end when** the fight has ended and every corrected hunter has read their window.

**How to frighten.** Start the clock and then answer questions slowly. Ask about hands in the same words as before. During the pass, hold still and say nothing. The four things climbing the far wall get one sentence, and no answer if anyone asks what they are.

### Chapter 8: Making room

**Read aloud.**

> Brandt is standing at the edge with his arms out from his sides. He stood in the open for it, with his pack on, his pick in one hand and his whole sack of shards in the other. All of it is gone. He turns his hands over and looks at them, and he is laughing with almost no sound.
>
> "C," he says. "Twenty-two years." He looks down the ramp at the next row, and the one under that. "It comes about every five hours. I can make the next row by then."
>
> Nell is looking past him, straight down. "Six rows have letters," she says. "I can read eleven more under S before it gets too faint. One window is lit down there."

**What is really happening.** Brandt is going down, and he will stand for every count he can reach. He is happy and he is kind about it. He gives the nearest hunter his truck keys and his sword, because the count would only take them. He cannot be talked out of it. He can be held by force, and he is level 20 now with the best gear in the district.

The hunters who stood are C rank and feel as well here as they did in the drowned room. The ones who moved are at three stacks of Squeezed with a nosebleed, and have hours still to walk.

**The decisions.**
- Brandt. Let him go, fight him, or go with him.
- Row 4. Nell says something in the drift one row down reads like a person and is the wrong color for one. It is a Resurrection Crystal (exists). The owner's ruling keeps them rare, so this one is five hours down the ramp and five back up the hip-high steps. The squeeze starts again for C rank on row 4. A count will pass while they are there.
- Looking down. Someone will lean over the edge to see the lit window under S.
- What to tell the hunters who moved. The recount is coming. They can be talked into it, or out of it.

**The app.** Send one phone, the hunter who looks down longest, a window titled "FOUND" with nothing in the body. Never explain it. Set a hunter who is still for a count on row 4 to level 35, which is rank B, and give the kind Deepfiled (section 4). Take Brandt off the GM's phone when he is out of sight.

**Ready to end when** Brandt is gone, or stopped, and the party is walking toward the lit window.

**How to frighten.** Play Brandt as the happiest man they have met. Let his last words to them be ordinary: "Tell Lorna my count was forty-one."

### Chapter 9: The back of the room

**Read aloud.**

> From behind, a gate is a sheet of blue glass with a room on the other side of it. You step through, and it is cold. You are at the back of a hall cut from black stone, behind a row of pillars, and everything in the hall has its back to you.
>
> There are orcs at the fires. There is a thing of piled rock beside the far arch. Past the arch, on the floor of the last chamber, three heads lie on two paws, asleep, facing the only door this room was built to have.
>
> Those of you with the new letter are breathing easily. The rest of you are not.

**What is really happening.** If anyone moved in chapter 7, the recount comes as the party reaches the lit window, as the same quest with eight real minutes on the clock. It is the last chance for a hunter who moved. A hunter counted here alone, after the rest, is the only one in that count.

The room is a C-rank dungeon whose gate opened under the Kessler Avenue overpass three days ago. The Ironvane guild paid the Association for the right to raid it and goes in at dawn. Its monsters are all watching the front.

**The decisions.**
- The last count. Stand now, or go into a C-rank room at five stacks of Squeezed and be carried through.
- Sneak or fight. Sneaking is three checks at C-rank difficulty (DC 20, 24, 28). A hunter with a drift item in each hand takes the hard one.
- Cerberus. It is asleep. The corrected hunters are level 20, and for the first time in this story a kill above their old rank will pay them. Its death starts the gate closing and takes from Ironvane a raid they paid for.
- What comes out with them. Everything from the drift is contents. Nell says so, and cannot say how she knows.

**The app.** Show the Kessler Avenue gate on the board. A fight if it comes to one: High Orc 3, Ice Elf 2, Stone Golem 1. Cerberus joins only if someone wakes it. Pay the XP, and let the level-20 hunters watch it land. The orcs and the elves pass their first turn against a corrected hunter (section 4, the terms).

**Ready to end when** the first hunter touches the front of the gate.

**How to frighten.** The fear here is how easy it is. Things that would have killed them yesterday do not look up when they pass.

### Chapter 10: Contents out

**Read aloud.**

> You come out under the Kessler Avenue overpass, thirty-one miles from the pool, in the rain, at four in the morning. There are floodlights on a chain-link fence, and a trailer with IRONVANE painted on the side.
>
> A siren starts. It is the break alarm, the one the city tests on the first Monday of every month. A woman in guild armor is walking toward you with one hand up and the other on her sword. Behind her somebody is shouting into a radio. "Five out. Five out of a sealed gate."
>
> She stops ten feet away. She looks at your boots, which are wet to the knee, and then at your faces. Then she reads the paper sticker on your jacket, out loud.

**What is really happening.** A gate's meter cannot tell a corrected hunter from the room's own contents, so it has logged a C-rank dungeon break of five. It will do that at every gate they leave, for the rest of their lives. Renata Cole has a party of strangers with VISITOR on their chests who came out of a room nobody went into.

By noon the Association has measured them again. The crystal says C. There is no form for it. The assessor writes that the first measurement must have been wrong, twelve years ago, for all of them at once.

Tri-County pays for the shards Lorna heard. It docks fifteen gold for the lamp.

**The decisions.**
- What to say, and who says it first. The truth sends every D-rank and E-rank hunter in the country digging through a floor. A lie has to be the same from five mouths. Silence is an answer Cole will remember.
- The second measurement. A hunter can refuse it and stay D rank on paper.
- The drift items. Clause 9 says they are Tri-County's. Ironvane says anything that came out of its gate is Ironvane's.
- What to do with Brandt's keys.

**The app.** The number shouted into the radio is the meter's, the corrected hunters plus Nell. Send every phone the same number as "NOTICE: Contents out: 5". A hunter who was never still at any count is standing in the rain with the rest and is not in that number. Send that hunter, alone, "NOTICE: Not found." Complete "Piece rate" and pay it.

**Ready to end when** every hunter has a letter, or has refused one.

**How to frighten.** Let the siren run under the whole scene. Have Cole count them twice.

### Chapter 11: Reshelved

## 4. The decisions that make a rank and a class

### How the count decides

Keep one sheet of paper with a row for each hunter. At each count write down their answer to the question.

- Rank is the row where a count finds the hunter still. Row 3 makes them C. Row 4 makes them B. A hunter found still only on row 2 stays D.
- Kind is read from the hands first, then from what the hunter is doing, then from what is on their back. The tables below say what each answer makes.
- A count enters one of each kind. If two hunters give the same answer, the one lower on the ramp takes it, because the light reaches them first, and the other is read by their other hand.
- What is read is used. It leaves the bag.
- Empty hands and standing idle is an answer. That hunter's window says "Kind: " and nothing after it. They are C rank with no job, and may take a Job Change the ordinary way.

### The terms

Every hunter a count has corrected carries these.

1. No pass. Every gate they walk out of logs a dungeon break of their rank.
2. Monsters of a lower rank than theirs hold still as they pass, until one is struck. In a fight, such a monster passes its first turn. The hunters will find this useful, and then they will remember where they saw it first.
3. A gate of their rank opens near them on a clock, in places a gate should not fit (the game's personal gates).
4. The daily is "Hold still, one minute." A missed daily sends a hunter to the Penalty Zone, as it always has. The Penalty Zone has always had Giant Centipedes in it. Run it once on a ledge with a drift on it, and describe nothing else.
5. One thing was moved up a row for each of them.

### A hunter who refuses everything

This hunter moved at every count, the first one included. They had the squeeze at five stacks through a C-rank room, on someone else's back. The Unfiled came for them twice.

They come out D rank, level 12, with no job. Their last window is "Not found." After that the System has no row for them. No daily arrives and no gate comes looking. At the second measurement the Association's crystal reads nothing at all, as it does for a person who never awakened, though their skills still work.

With no row of their own, they cannot be on the wrong one. They never get the squeeze again, in a gate of any rank. They are the only hunter alive who could walk into an S-rank gate and breathe, at the strength of a D. Give them the title "Not found" (Agility +2). Do not tell them the other thing. They can work out for themselves what else in the shaft was never counted, and how big it was.

### A hunter who takes everything

This hunter stood for every count with both hands full, followed Brandt to row 4, took the crystal and stood there too.

They come out B rank, level 35, kind Deepfiled. The count used everything they carried, so they hold a little of every kind. Give them one built-in skill from each of three jobs, and a title, "Filed deep" (Strength +3, Vitality +3, Presence -4). People step back from them in elevators.

Their terms are heavier. Their gate is B rank and comes every three days, and a B-rank gate breaks in four. It opens wherever they sleep. The crystal is contents from row 4, and what comes out of those gates is looking for it.

### Ten new kinds

Each is a job the reward names as text, a skill from the skill maker, and a title whose bonus has a cost in it.

**Seal.** Found holding the sack of shards. Skill "Close" (support, 30 ft): a shield on an ally, and it ends Bleeding. **Crier.** Found holding the radio with the key down, speaking. Skill "Sound off" (support, allies within 30 ft): each is Hasted for a round. **Teller.** Found holding the count stone, or counting aloud with their eyes open. **Misfile.** Found half still: a hunter who was moving when the light arrived and stopped inside it. Reaction "Elsewhere", twice a fight: before a use lands on them they blink 15 ft and it misses. ## 6. Walking out a rank higher

Nobody has done this before. The Association has measured every awakened person for twelve years and has never re-measured one to a different letter.

**Who notices.** Ironvane notices first, because five people came out of its gate, and if Cerberus is dead it paid for a raid it cannot run. The meter techs notice next. Every gate in the district has a meter, and every one will log a break when these hunters walk out. Within a month someone at the Association lays the break logs beside the gate rosters and sees the same five names on both.

**Who wants to know how.** Every guild wants to know, since a guild that can turn a D into a C can turn a B into an A. So does every hunter stuck at their letter, which is nearly every hunter. If the truth gets out, crews will be digging through the floors of E-rank gates inside a week. Those floors are solid, and the crews will dig until their gates close on them. Brandt's wife wants his truck back and an answer.

**Who will not believe it.** The assessor will not, having written "initial measurement in error" five times in one morning, and the Association will defend that sentence because the other explanation ends the ranking system. Other D-rank hunters will not, and will say these five paid for it. Lorna will not. She has the day's counts, and they stop at 2:40 in the afternoon.

### Seeds

1. The four gates. Each thing moved up is a C-rank monster in a D-rank room under a D-rank reading. There is one for each corrected hunter, and its archetype matches the kind that hunter was given. The Association sends its newest C-rank hunters.
2. Brandt. A B-rank gate opens in the spring, and the boss in its last room is wearing a Tri-County harness. He is glad to see them.
3. Nell. She can read any gate's row from the street, and she can read people. She has started keeping a list of hunters whose number does not match their card. There are more than five names on it.
4. The pool room. Gates reuse rooms. Next month an E-rank gate opens two states away onto a goblin warren with a hole in its lowest floor.
5. The lit window eleven rows under S. Lit means open, so somewhere there is a gate onto that room.
6. The bent stone. Something came up through six floors and went out through an E-rank room, before there were hunters to see it leave.
7. The Unfiled. Each was the size of a person carrying another person. The hunter who is Not found has begun to dream about holding still.
8. The count stone has one notch left to cut on its last row.

## 7. Running it

### Dread at a table with phones

Keep phones face down until one buzzes. Send no window to fill a silence, so that a buzz always means the System has something to say.

Ask the question the same way every time: "What is in your hands?" Go around the table one at a time, and write each answer down where they can see you writing. By the third count the players dread the question.

When a count passes, hold still yourself. Stop in the middle of a sentence if you have to. Players will copy you without being told, and the ones who keep fidgeting will notice they are the only ones.

Give size in numbers a person can check: six hundred paces between windows, thirty-one windows, steps as high as a hip. Then stop describing.

Say each wrong thing once, in a plain sentence, and go on. If asked, repeat the same words. "You don't know" is a complete answer in this story, and a true one.

Nothing here hates them. Brandt is decent, Nell is frightened and exact, and the count is a procedure. Play all of it level, and leave the players to notice which things they did themselves.

### Using the app to frighten

- The urgent quest in chapter 7 runs a real clock. It counts down twelve true minutes while five people argue over a rope, a lamp and a surveyor.
- Some phones get a window and some do not. In chapter 6 the hunters who moved get nothing while the others read "Found in place." In chapter 10 the window says "Contents out: 5" to a table whose hunters number six with Nell.
- "Not found: 7" and "Over 4" are numbers that mean the party. Let the players do the subtraction.
- No pass is a status nobody asked for, which does nothing and never comes off. Squeezed climbs by the hour on the sheets of the hunters who would not hold still.
- The fight log shows six centipedes passing every turn while someone kills them. Later it shows a High Orc passing its turn against a hunter it has stopped treating as a visitor.
- The level edit is the one the GM uses to fix a typo. A hunter goes from 12 to 20 while the table watches the number change and the HP bar fill.
- On the board the pool gate closes. Later a C-rank gate nobody has heard of appears, and they are inside it.
- From chapter 11 the daily arrives every morning of the campaign.

### If they wait on row 2

Some tables will refuse to walk down. Let them. Give it two counts, ten hours, with the centipedes and the dark. A window forty paces off lights at the eleventh hour, a D-rank gate somewhere. They walk out D rank, in place, with Counted and No pass on their sheets, the daily every morning, and no class. Brandt went down without them at the second hour. Then run the four gates in section 6 with other people's names on them, and let them hear what C-rank hunters are suddenly being paid.

### Before you run it

This story has a long fall, a great height, trouble breathing, and friends who must choose whether to leave each other. Ask the table beforehand whether any of that is a bad idea for someone. Put a card on the table that anyone can tap to skip a moment without giving a reason.

### Two players, or six

With two, Nell carries the lamp until she cannot, and Brandt anchors the rope and stays one count longer. Set "Not found" to 4. With six, put two lamps on Tri-County's table and keep one rope. The gap in chapter 7 then takes two anchors.
