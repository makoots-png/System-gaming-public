# The small door: the opening mission, second version

A second version for the owner, written after reading the first draft (`intro-story.md`), and his to change. It is for four D-rank hunters (level 10) and a GM, over two sessions of about four hours. Section 7 says how to run it for two or six.

The players' name for the job is **the Linden Way sweep**. The GM's name for the thing at the bottom is **the Guest**. Never say the second name at the table.

**What I kept from the first draft and what I did not.** I replaced its premise. The first draft's gate measures hunters and fits them to a dungeon's empty jobs. It is clever, and its fear is the fear of being assessed. This version's fear is older and closer to home: something is dying in the next room, it knows you are there, and it does not want to be alone. I kept the first draft's craft where it was right: one wrong detail a chapter, a fight log full of turns where nothing attacks, a quest that arrives already counted, windows sent to one phone, and the three sentences on safety.

## 1. For the owner

### The premise

The hunters are D rank and broke. The only posting that will take them reads "E-rank or unranked labor acceptable": sweep a nine-day-old E-rank gate in a storage unit, keep whatever shards they find, and bring back the moving blankets the goblins have been stealing from the other units, at two gold a blanket. An old porter named Gus comes with them, carrying far too much water, and gives them three rules. The goblin cave is as dull as promised. Under it, in a room the size of a church, something enormous is lying under every blanket that ever went missing, and it is dying, and it has been dying for longer than the building has stood. Four people are sitting at its bedside. They will not leave. One of them is Gus's wife.

### What is truly wrong with this gate

This is the truth. The players can put it together. Nobody ever says it to them.

1. **It is not an E-rank gate.** Something very large and very old came in through a small door to die, the way an animal crawls under a porch. The Association's meter reads what comes out of a gate. Nothing comes out of this one. It is keeping everything in.
2. **It cannot die while anyone is with it.** The dying wait until you step out of the room. So the goblins, who are its hosts, have never left it alone, and neither has any person who found it. It has been kept at the last hour of its life for decades by kindness. The gate opens, gets listed as new, takes a sitter or two, and closes over again.
3. **No one who sits can make themselves leave.** Not because it holds them. Because it asks.
4. **The way to end it is to walk out on it.** Every living thing has to leave that room, and somebody has to be the last one through the curtain while it asks them not to go. That is the whole mission. A party of D-rank hunters kills something of the highest rank by leaving it alone.

The clues, in the order they arrive: four cans of water for one afternoon; a folding chair and a pair of reading glasses inside the unit; goblins who do not attack, with knives still in their belts and bowls in their hands; a tunnel rubbed smooth far above head height; scavengers lined along the walls, all facing down, waiting; shards that are warm; air that moves like breath, with pauses; a quest nobody wrote that says "It is thirsty now"; chairs with the hunters' names on them in Gus's handwriting; Ruth saying "They always wait until you step out"; a status on its row called Attended, whose number is the people in the room; and the last breath, which goes down the tunnel and does not come back.

What it is, what it was, and who it has been waiting for are never answered. It gets no stat block that matters, no name and no face that the table is shown.

### Why hunters get classes at all

**What the Association teaches.** At level 10 the System recognizes a hunter's growth and confirms it with a Job Change. Survive three waves in the Job Change dungeon, defeat the Knight Captain, receive a class.

**What this story shows.** Nothing with power dies cleanly. What it could do does not go out with it. It passes, part by part, to the living who were close, and each part goes where it was invited: to the hand that took from it, the mouth that answered it, the back that carried something out of its room, the one who was still there at the end. The System reads the will aloud. Hunters call that a Job Change.

So a class is a bequest. A Fighter holds something's arm. A Ranger holds its sight. It works because the part still works. It does not know whose it is now.

**Why the standard chain has a Knight Captain in it.** The Association's Job Change dungeon is one death, kept and repeated. The same Knight Captain dies for every applicant and leaves the same seven thin parts each time. That is why most hunters are one of seven things. After this story, when a window says "Defeat the Knight Captain," the players will wonder how many times it has been made to do this, and who sits with it between applicants so that it never finishes.

**Why it is frightening to hold.**
- You know exactly what you did at that bedside to get it. So does everyone who was there.
- The part remembers who it thought you were.
- Things that wait for something to die have noticed you.
- You now know how you will be inherited. When a classed hunter dies, what they hold goes to whoever is nearest. Someday somebody will be in the room with you, deciding what to take.

**The three rules.** Gus gives them in the first five minutes, as porter's superstition:

1. Nothing warm goes in the bag.
2. If you hear your name down there, it isn't for you.
3. Nobody walks out last. We leave in twos.

They are the exact method for coming out with nothing. Every class in section 4 is a rule somebody broke: by taking, by answering, or by being the last one out. The players are told this on the first page and will not understand it until the last. When they do, they will know that every one of them chose.

**One part, one heir.** It has one arm to leave, one voice, one death. So from this gate each class goes to one hunter, and five players come out five different ways.

### The shape

Dull, then humbling, then wrong, then hellish, then out. The first hour is laundry. The middle is a visit to a sickroom, which is quiet and warm and the worst part. The hell is short and the party causes it. Violence is quick and plain. There is very little blood. The people in the room are gentle, and nobody in this story is evil, including the thing in the bed.

### How long it takes

Session one is chapters 1 to 5 and ends on the last line of Gus's speech at the head of chapter 6. Session two is chapters 6 to 10. Chapter 11 opens the session after and takes five minutes.

- "Leave", kind urgent, due 15 minutes of real time after you send it, description "The door is closing.", one objective "Out" with a target of 1, no reward.

## 3. The mission

### Chapter 1: Two gold a blanket

**Told** (read aloud)

> Linden Way Self Storage is four rows of orange roll-up doors beside a highway off-ramp. The gate is in unit C-114. It opened nine days ago, the Association rated it E, and nobody bid on it. So it went to the sweep list, which is where you come in.
>
> The posting reads: "E-rank or unranked labor acceptable." You are D rank. You took it anyway.
>
> The manager, Denise Kowal, does not look up from her screen. "Sixty a head for the sweep. Shards are yours, the Association pays forty. And the blankets. Something comes out of that unit at night and takes the moving blankets out of my other units. Only the blankets. Two gold for every one you bring back." She pushes a box across the counter with her foot. "You have to wear the vest."
>
> The vests are orange. On the back, in black capitals: E-SWEEP.
>
> Your porter is waiting at C-114 with a hand truck. He is a gray man of about sixty in a vest washed so many times the letters are nearly gone. On the hand truck are four twenty-liter cans of water, which is a lot of water for one afternoon.
>
> "Gus," he says. "I'll sign you in, save you the pen." He prints each of your names on the clipboard in careful block capitals. "Three rules and then we go. One. Nothing warm goes in the bag. Two. If you hear your name down there, it isn't for you. Three. Nobody walks out last. We leave in twos."

No System window. The System says nothing true until chapter 9.

**What is really happening.** Gus Varnell has been down this gate eleven times in nine years, each time it has been listed as new. His wife Ruth is at the bottom. He came down alone at dawn today with two more cans and set out a chair for each name on the posting. He is not a liar. He will answer any question with something true and small. The rules are real, they are how he has stayed clean for nine years, and he gives them because he needs whoever sits to have chosen it.

**Beats.**
- Kowal, the vests, the rate. A guild crew in good gear crossing the lot to a real gate up the road. One of them reads the backs of the vests aloud.
- The posting pays an E-rank porter a half share. Kowal asks the crew whether Gus gets the half or a full one. It comes out of theirs.
- Gus's kit. Seven things, and each hunter carries one: the pry bar, a water can, the porter's sack, the tally book, the lamp, the knife "for cutting blankets loose", the rope.
- Inside the unit: somebody's whole household in boxes, a mattress on its side, and behind it the gate, low and gray.

**The wrong detail.** Just inside the unit, facing the gate, there is a folding chair with a pair of reading glasses on the seat. Gus picks up the glasses, folds them and puts them in his shirt pocket. He does not say anything and neither should you.

**The decisions.**
- **Gus's share.** A full share for the old man costs each hunter about twenty gold they need. The half share is the rule, and nobody would blame them. Gus says "Whatever's usual" and means it. He will remember.
- **Crew lead.** Somebody has to sign as lead. The lead is who Kowal blames, and who Gus talks to first.
- **What to carry.** One thing each. Nobody wants the water. It is twenty kilograms and it is obviously pointless.
- **The rules.** Laugh, or ask. If asked what the water is for: "You'll see people get thirsty." If asked about rule three: "It's bad luck to be last. Old porters' thing."

**In the app.**
- The gate is on the board. Enter it when the chapter ends.
- Give each hunter their piece of kit as an Award of the item (section 5).
- Mark on paper who voted which way on the share, who signed, who carries what.

**Ready sign** (GM only): none. Move on when the last vest is on.

**How to frighten them here.** Do not. Make it funny and a little shaming. Make the players say out loud that their hunters put the vests on. Play Gus as the nicest man they have met all week. Say the three rules once, lightly, the way a man says "lefty loosey." The only cold thing in the chapter is the glasses going into his pocket.

### Chapter 2: Pickers

**Told** (read aloud)

> Through the gate it is an E-rank cave, the kind in the training pamphlet. Low roof, wet floor, a smell of old dishwater. The shards are in the walls like grit in a tire: blue, dull, the size of a fingernail. You could work an hour and fill one pocket.
>
> The blankets are everywhere. Quilted moving blankets, blue on one side and black on the other, dragged off into the dark. You came down here to fight monsters, and you are picking up laundry.
>
> At the second bend, the goblins. Six of them, and an old one with a staff. They are very thin. They stand shoulder to shoulder across the passage and they do not come forward. Each one is holding something in both hands.

When the fight is over, read:

> The passage is wet, and not with blood. They were carrying bowls. Clay bowls, pressed out with thumbs, every one of them full to the brim before it fell. Their knives are still in their belts.
>
> Past them the floor slopes down. It is wet all the way, the way a path is worn: as if this has been carried along here many times a day for a very long time.

**What is really happening.** The goblins are the Guest's hosts. It came into their home to die and they have given it everything since: their food, their water, every blanket they could steal, and somebody at its side at all hours. They are starving because it gets fed first and does not eat. This was the afternoon water. They will not start a fight while they have a bowl in their hands, and they will not put a bowl down.

**Beats.**
- Shard work. Each hunter finds 1d6 Mana Crystal Shards in the hour. Each folds blankets. Say the running count of blankets aloud.
- The goblins block. They hiss. They do not move.
- The fight, if there is one. After the first goblin falls, the shaman strikes back. The others still do not.
- The bowls. The wet floor. Gus, looking at it, saying nothing for a while. Then: "Well. Shards won't pick themselves."

**The wrong detail.** The knives still in their belts.

**The decisions.**
- **Clear them or wait.** The job is to clear the gate, and goblins kill people. They are also not attacking. Waiting is unpaid and feels stupid. If the party waits long enough, the goblins edge past along the wall, bowls held high, and go down. Most tables will not wait.
- **Who strikes first.** Somebody does. Everybody sees who.
- **The nest.** The goblins' beds are moving blankets, eighteen of them, thirty-six gold. Some of the dead are lying on them.
- **The bodies.** Leave them, lay them out, or cover them with two-gold blankets.
- **The bowls.** One did not break. Pick it up or step over it.
- **The staff.** The shaman's staff has a blue stone bound to its tip, bigger than any shard in these walls. It is warm. Gus watches whoever picks it up and says nothing. Rule one is their business.

**In the app.**
- Fight: Goblin 6, Goblin Shaman 1. Pass every goblin's turn. The log fills with goblins doing nothing while the hunters work. Give the shaman its turns only after a goblin has fallen.
- Write down how many goblins died. That number is the target of the Water quest.
- Awards: Mana Crystal Shards as rolled, Moving Blankets as counted, Clay Bowl and Warm-tipped Staff to whoever took them. The staff is one stack of Warm.

**Ready sign** (GM only): a fight in the gate ended. If nobody fought, there is none.

**How to frighten them here.** Run the fight flat and fast, like a chore. Do not hint. Let them finish and feel fine about it. Then read the second passage slowly. Do not use the word "water" yourself until a player does. If a player says "they were bringing it to something," look at them and move on.

### Chapter 3: Nothing warm goes in the bag

**Told** (read aloud)

> The way down is not goblin work. It is too wide and too high, and the walls are rubbed smooth in a long stripe far above your heads, the way a doorframe gets rubbed where a big dog goes through it every day.
>
> The centipedes are along both walls. Dozens of them, each as long as your leg, every one with its head pointing down the tunnel. They do not move when your light crosses them. They do not move when you walk between them.
>
> "Leave them," Gus says. "They're waiting. Same as birds on a fence."
>
> Then the air moves. All of it, down the tunnel past you, warm, like a door opening on a kitchen. It goes on for a long time. Then it stops. Then, after longer than you would like, it comes back up.
>
> In the walls down here the shards are the size of your thumb, and they are warm.

**What is really happening.** The tunnel is the way it came in. The centipedes are scavengers and have been waiting a long time for a very large meal. The air is its breathing. The warm shards are what is settling out of it as it fails, the way salt comes out of water. Prying one out hurts it: the breath catches. The quest that arrives is the Guest asking for its afternoon water in the only voice it has that the hunters can read.

**Beats.**
- The breath. From this moment you breathe it for the table (section 7).
- The warm shards. Each is worth about 400 gold, ten times a cold one. There are as many as the hunters want to take.
- The first time one is pried loose, the air going down the tunnel stops short, like a caught breath. Every centipede on the wall lifts its head, and then lowers it.
- Halfway down, a heap against the wall: food. Goblin flatbread, dried meat, mushrooms, laid out on a blanket and never touched. Days of it. The goblins at the top were skin and bone.
- The quest arrives on every phone.
- One hunter, whoever is walking last, hears their name.

**The wrong detail.** The quest. Nobody at the table wrote it, and its target is the number of goblins they killed.

**The decisions.**
- **The warm shards.** This is the scrap they came for, turned into real money: three of them is a month's rent, and they are lying in the wall for the taking. Gus will not touch one and will not stop anyone. "Rule one's my rule. You're grown." After the first pry they have felt what it does. After that, every shard is taken knowing.
- **The centipedes.** They are E rank and they are not moving. It would be the easiest XP any of them ever made. Or leave them, and walk the rest of the way with them at your back.
- **The food.** It is good and nobody is eating it. The hunters have been working for hours.
- **The water.** Gus has two cans on the truck and the hunter who took a can has a third. The quest wants a bowl for each goblin they killed. There are bowls in the wet passage above, if somebody goes back up alone for them. Or drink it: the tunnel is very warm.
- **The name.** Tell the others, keep it quiet, or answer.

**In the app.**
- Send "Water" to every hunter at once, the urgent quest, with its countdown. Say nothing. If asked who sent it: "It says System."
- A System window to the last hunter in line alone: tag "!", the title that hunter's own name in capitals, no body.
- Each warm shard taken: an Award of a Warm Shard and one stack of Warm. Let them find the status and find that it makes them stronger.
- Optional fight: Giant Centipede 6. They fight back only when struck. Others come down off the walls to replace them, and do not attack.
- Food eaten: a Goblin Offering (section 5).

**Ready sign** (GM only): every hunter holds the Water quest.

**How to frighten them here.** Breathe. Out, slowly, through your mouth, while you narrate. Then stop, and keep talking, and let them wait for the next one. When a shard is pried, cut your own breath short in the middle of a word. Do not explain it. Do it every time, and by the third shard somebody at the table will say "stop taking those," and somebody else will not stop. When the countdown is at ten minutes, glance at your phone.

### Chapter 4: The house rule

**Told** (read aloud)

> The tunnel ends at a curtain. Moving blankets, thirty or forty of them, lashed together with packing twine and hung across an opening the size of a garage door. The warm air goes in and out through it, and the whole curtain leans and settles, leans and settles.
>
> A hobgoblin stands in front of it in iron that hangs loose on him. He was a big animal once. He holds his shield across the way and looks at your hands.
>
> To one side is a stack of folding chairs, the gray steel kind, with LINDEN WAY stenciled on the backs. Some of them have been unfolded and set in a row facing the curtain. One for each of you. On the back of each chair is a strip of masking tape, and on each strip, in ballpoint, in careful block capitals, a name.

**What is really happening.** The hobgoblin keeps the one rule of the house: nobody goes in to it empty-handed. He will let anyone through who carries water. He has not eaten in a week. The chairs are Gus's work, set out this morning. The handwriting on the tape is the handwriting on the sign-in sheet.

**Beats.**
- The hobgoblin looks at hands. A hunter with a full bowl or a can walks past him. Anyone else meets the shield.
- Offered food, he takes it, and carries it inside, and comes back without it.
- The chairs. The names.
- Gus, if asked: "I came down first thing. Saves time later." If asked what the chairs are for: "Sitting." He will say the rest in the room and not before.

**The wrong detail.** The block capitals. Let them work out for themselves where they saw that hand.

**The decisions.**
- **The door.** Carry water in like a goblin, or fight a starving guard at his post. He is D rank and alone and they can take him. One way costs pride and the other costs something else.
- **The bowl.** There may be only one, and several hunters. Who carries it, and who goes back up the tunnel alone for more.
- **The chair.** Sit, to rest, because the walk was long and the chair is right there. Or tear the tape off. Or leave it and wonder who will read it later.
- **Gus.** Ask him in front of everyone, take him aside, or say nothing and watch him.

**In the app.**
- Optional fight: Hobgoblin 1. He uses Shield Wall and gives ground. He does not leave the curtain.
- A hunter who carries water through: tick one on their Water quest yourself, or let them.
- No window.

**Ready sign** (GM only): none.

**How to frighten them here.** Hold up your own notebook from chapter 1, where you wrote their hunters' names in block capitals as Gus signed them in. Do not say why. If the party fights the hobgoblin, he makes no sound, and when he goes down he falls across the opening, still in the way, and somebody has to move him.

### Chapter 5: Visiting hours

**Told** (read aloud)

> Past the curtain the cave is the size of a church and as warm as a bath. You cannot see the far wall. You can see the near part of what is lying in it.
>
> It is under blankets. Hundreds of them, every blanket that ever went missing from Linden Way, laid over it and tucked in, and under them a shape as long as a train car that rises and falls. That is where the air goes.
>
> One part of it is not covered. It lies palm up on the stone, as wide as a door. It has too many fingers, and they are curled the way a sleeper's are.
>
> There are people here. Four chairs in a half ring at the hand, and someone in each. A woman in a cardigan. A big man in guild plate with a captain's bar. A young woman with an Association lanyard and a survey meter in her lap. A very old goblin on an upturned bucket. The woman in the cardigan is holding one of the fingers in both her hands, the way you hold a hand at a bedside.
>
> She looks up, and smiles at your porter.
>
> "Gus," she says. "You brought so many this time."

**What is really happening.** These are the sitters. Ruth Varnell, nine years. Captain Imre Sobol of the Kestrel Guild, B rank, two months: he came in alone to kill it and had his sword raised. Noor Castellan, Association assessor, three weeks: she was sent to find out why a B-rank captain vanished in an E-rank gate. The old goblin, longer than any of them. There is a fifth the hunters cannot see: Marek Dufresne, an electrician who wired this building thirty-one years ago, asleep under the blankets against its side. None of them is held. All of them are well: warm, unhurried, not hungry. Each of them has been taken for someone, and has let it stand.

The Guest is awake. It knows who came in. It looks at each hunter and takes each one for somebody it knew, by what they did on the way down.

**Beats.**
- The room. The hand. The breath, close now: breathe it louder.
- The sitters are kind and a little slow, like people woken from a nap. They know Gus. They ask the hunters' names and already seem to know them.
- Ruth: "Sit, sit. There's a chair for each of you. Gus is good about that."
- Noor, holding up the meter: "It reads E. I've set it right twice. It reads what comes out of a gate, and nothing comes out of this one anymore."
- Sobol: "I came in to kill it. I want that understood. I had the sword up."
- If anyone asks why they stay, Ruth says it, pleasantly, as a thing everyone knows: "Oh, you can't leave them on their own at the end. They always wait until you step out of the room, you know. My mother did. I went for a coffee. So we don't step out."
- The windows arrive, one phone at a time.
- At the wrist, where the blankets end, the warm shards are the size of fists.

**The wrong detail.** Each hunter's phone, face up on the table, with a status on the sheet that nobody put there.

**The names.** Give each hunter one, by what you saw them do. It does not see well, and two hunters may get the same one.

| It calls them | Because | The window, sent to that hunter alone |
|---|---|---|
| Knife | Struck the first blow, or killed the most | **YOU CAME**. "I thought it would be you. You always did the hard things for me." |
| Thief | Carries the most that is warm | **TAKE IT**. "Take what you like. I kept it for you. I only wanted to watch you choose." |
| Water | Carried water through the curtain | **THANK YOU**. "Is it morning? You always came in the morning." |
| Kin | Is the first to touch the hand | **YOU GREW**. "Your hand was smaller." |
| Witness | Walked last, kept a count, or stood by the curtain | **I SEE YOU TOO**. "You are standing where you always stood. By the door." |
| Late | Came through the curtain last, or fought the guard | **YOU ARE LATE**. "It is all right. I waited. I am still waiting for the other one." |
| Stranger | None of these | Send nothing to this hunter. Send every other player: **WHO IS THAT**. "There is someone in the room I do not know." |

**The decisions.**
- **Answer it.** Say yes, it's me, to something dying that is glad to see you. It costs nothing and it is the kindest thing anyone could do. It is also a lie, and rule two.
- **Correct it.** Tell it the truth: you are not who it thinks. The window that comes back is **OH**, with no body. The status leaves the sheet.
- **Use it.** It thinks you are someone it loves. Ask it for something.
- **Share the window, or do not.** Each player knows what theirs said. Nobody knows what the others' said.
- **The hand.** Touch it. Take a turn holding it, so Ruth can stretch. It is warm and dry and it closes, very slightly.
- **The blanket at its face.** There is a place where the blankets come to a rise. Somebody could lift one and look.
- **The wrist.** Shards worth a year of work, an arm's length from four people who will watch you pry them out of it. They will not stop you. Ruth will say, "Oh, don't, dear. It feels that."
- **The under-blankets.** The ones against its body are warm through. Taking one uncovers it.
- **The sitters.** Their lips are cracked. Sobol has sores from the plate. Tend them, question them, or try to talk one into leaving. None will, today.

**In the app.**
- Put each hunter's name status on their sheet, outside any fight, without a word. In a later fight the whole table sees every chip. The one that says Thief is seen by all.

## 4. The decisions that make a class

### How a class is decided

Keep the sheet out of sight. Each choice in the table below puts a mark beside a part of the Guest, and each part is a class. When it dies, read the sheet in this order.

1. **The chair.** A hunter who stayed in the room is not read. See "Stays", below.
2. **The three rules.** A hunter who broke none of them is Unnamed. A hunter who broke all three, and carried five or more stacks of Warm when it died, is the Heir.
3. **Last out, alone.** That hunter is the Undertaker, whatever else they did. If the last two went through together, or Gus went last, no hunter is.
4. **Everyone else** gets the part with the most marks. Holding a part's own thing when it died (section 5 says which) counts as two more marks.
5. **One part, one heir.** If two hunters lead the same part, it goes to the one who left the room later, and the other takes their next. Tell nobody this rule.

The rules, as the sheet counts them. Rule one is broken by having anything warm in the bag when it died. Rule two is broken by answering it in any way: yes, no, or a question. Only silence is clean. Rule three is broken by being last out alone.

The players can see most of this themselves. Warm is a number on their sheet. The name it gave them is a chip the whole table can read. And Gus told them the rules at the door.

Each is made today as a quest of kind job whose reward names the class, gives its skill (the skill maker) and its mark (a title with its bonus). Add the ten names to the rules' list of jobs so the reward editor offers them.

## 7. Running it

### Dread at a table with phones

- **Breathe.** From chapter 3, breathe out aloud, slowly, while you talk. Then stop, and make them wait for the next one. Catch it short when a shard comes out. Take it early when someone says something in the room. Stop for good in chapter 8. It costs nothing and it is the whole story.
- **Phones face down** until one buzzes. Then a buzz means something. Never send a window to fill a silence.
- **The table is in the room.** From chapter 5, whatever the players say at the table while their hunters stand in the sickroom, it hears. Do not tell them. Use it once, in chapter 6. After that they will get up and go to the kitchen to plan, and you should let them.
- **Slow for seven chapters, fast for one.** Chapter 9 is the only chapter that hurries.
- **Say the wrong thing once.** One plain sentence, and move on. If asked, answer flatly. Never underline.
- **Never explain it.** What it is, what the tall ones are, what stood at the far wall. "You don't know" is the most frightening thing a GM can say, and here it is also true.
- **Nobody is the villain.** Gus is decent. Ruth is kind. The goblins were carrying water. The thing in the bed is afraid. Play all of them straight, and let the players be the ones who did things.

### Using the app to frighten

- **A message only one player gets.** Their own name as a window's title, in the tunnel. What it called them, at the bedside. What it said as they left. And the best one in the story: a window sent to everyone except one player.
- **A status nobody asked for.** A chip that says Thief, which the hunter did not choose and the whole table can read. Warm, which makes them stronger with every thing they should not have taken, and is the number the scavengers go by.
- **A number that counts people.** Attended, on the row of something that never takes a turn. Let them work out what it counts. Let them watch it go down as they leave.
- **A quest that should not exist.** Water, counting the goblins they killed, with a real clock on it. Leave, the only true one, which looks like all the false ones. A daily nobody set.
- **The board.** The gate reads E for two sessions. Change it in the middle of a fight and say nothing.
- **Death saves.** A hunter down, ringed on the map by tokens that pass their turns. The player rolls with the things that are waiting in view.
- **The log.** Six goblins passing every turn while the party cuts them down. Something of rank S passing every turn, forever.

### Before you run it

This story is about sitting with someone who is dying, and about leaving them. Some players have done that, recently. Ask each player beforehand, alone, whether a deathbed is something they want at a game night, and change the story if it is not: the sitters can be asleep, and the Guest need not speak. Put a card on the table that anyone can tap to skip a moment, no reason given, and honor it at once. At the break and at the end, ask how everyone is, and mean it.

- With two hunters, going out as a pair empties the room. Make sure they see that before they choose.
