# Scrap Rate: the opening mission

A first draft for the owner, and his to change. It is written for four D-rank hunters (level 10) and a GM, over two sessions. Section 7 says how to run it for two or six.

The players' name for it is **Scrap Rate**. The GM's name for the place under the gate is **the Fitting**. Never say the second name at the table.

## 1. For the owner

### The premise

The hunters are D rank, new enough that the only work they can get is the work nobody wants. Ostrander Recovery hires them at piece rate to sweep a "dead" E-rank gate in a shut cannery: bag the crystal shards, bring back the gear the last crew left inside, and confirm the gate is empty so it can be closed. The last crew, seven hunters under a woman named Ines Dray, went in eleven days ago, and June Ostrander says they quit. Under the cleared goblin warren is a second place that is on no survey, warm and lit and smelling of pressed cloth, where something has been taking hunters' measurements and cutting them into the staff a dungeon needs. The hunters come out with their classes because that is what was being done to them, and it was not finished.

### What is wrong with the gate

This is the truth. The players can piece it together. Nobody ever tells them.

A dungeon is not a cave with monsters in it. It is an arrangement of offices: a captain, a door, a mender, a lamp, a watch, a quiet one, a keeper of the dead, and the hands that do the work. When a gate's monsters are all killed and the gate is left open, it does not die. It hires. It fits whatever walks in to its empty offices, and it chooses each one's office by watching what that one does. The Dray crew were fitted. The players are being fitted from the moment they write their height on Ostrander's form, and every choice they make inside is a measurement.

The System's Job Change is the same thing seen from the outside. A class is an office in a gate that a hunter walked out of before the fitting was done. The gate and the System use the same words.

The clues, in the order they arrive: seven pairs of boots nobody came back for; chalk lines around the goblins' wrists; goblins in hemmed hunter clothes; a handrail worn smooth in a two-week-old gate; coats in the hunters' sizes with their names on paper tickets; a quest called Fitting that already has numbers in it; Margit saying "it's been taking your measure since you signed"; a table set with their names; and, in the yard, a Job Change window whose title is the gate's own phrase.

Who cuts the patterns is never answered. The shears are heard. Nothing is seen. Do not explain it, and do not write it a stat block.

### Tone

Dull, then off, then wrong, then hellish, then quiet. The first hour is a bad shift at a bad job: wet knees, a radio asking for counts, pay docked for a lost flashlight. The fear is the fear of being assessed by something that does not care what you think of it. One wrong detail at a time. Violence, when it comes, is quick and plain, and there is very little blood. The people the players meet in the dark are kind, tired and polite, and that is worse than teeth.

### How long it takes

Two sessions of about four hours. Session one is chapters 1 to 6 and ends when Teo's fate is settled. Session two is chapters 7 to 11. Chapter 12 opens the session after, in a few minutes. As one long night of about seven hours: skip the Quiet fight in chapter 7 and run chapter 8 as two waves.

### What it sets up

Every hunter walks out with a class, a mark that came with it, and something they took or left behind. Ostrander knew more than she said. The Association's Job Change is not what the Association says it is. The gate on the board will not stay closed. Section 6 lists eight hooks, each a seed for a later story.

## 2. Why hunters get classes at all

**What the Association teaches.** At level 10 a hunter's growth is recognized by the System, and a Job Change confirms what the hunter has become. The standard chain asks for level 10, three waves survived in a Job Change dungeon, and a Knight Captain defeated. Most hunters believe this. It is mostly true, in the way a menu is mostly true about the kitchen.

**What this story shows.** A class is a vacancy. Every gate is a set of offices, and a gate that has lost its staff measures whoever comes in and starts to cut them to fit. When a hunter survives a fitting and walks out before it is finished, the office comes out with them, half sewn on. The System's window is how that is told from outside. A Job Change dungeon, with its waves and its Knight Captain, is a fitting room the Association keeps on purpose.

**Why it makes a class earned.** Nobody picks a class from a list. The gate watched what each hunter did when it counted: who went first, who carried someone, who ate, who lied, who closed a door. The class is the shape of those choices. A player can look at their class and name the moment it came from.

**Why it is frightening to hold.** The office is still an office. A Healer mends because somewhere a dungeon's mender's chair is the shape of them. The class works, and it pulls. Every class in section 4 has a mark: the small, permanent way the office still holds on. And the gate keeps their measurements on file.

**The one rule for the table.** From one gate, each office goes to one hunter. "It doesn't cut two coats from one pattern." So at a table of five, five hunters come out five different things.

This also gives every later Job Change in the campaign a chill for free. After this story, when the System says "Defeat the Knight Captain," the players will know what a Knight Captain is.

## 3. The mission

### Before the first session

### Chapter 1: Piece rate

**Told** (read aloud)

> The cannery on Pier Nine has been shut for eleven years. Two weeks ago a gate opened in its old freezer room: a gray ring, lower than a door, that hums like a refrigerator. The Association rated it E and sold the clearing rights for less than a used car.
>
> Ostrander Recovery bought them. June Ostrander meets you in the loading yard with a clipboard and a thermos of coffee she does not offer you.
>
> "Shards are fifteen apiece. Any gear with a name tape on it, a hundred. The last crew walked off the job and left their kit inside, and I want it back. You see goblins, you clear them. You see anything else, you radio me and you walk out. Sign here. Initial here, where it says you understand the gate is rated E."
>
> By the freezer room door, under the eave, out of the rain, there are seven pairs of street boots in a row. The laces are tucked in.

No System window. The System is silent until chapter 5, and its silence is part of the story.

**What is really going on.** June Ostrander has sent four crews into dead gates this year. Some came out with classes, and the Association's placement desk pays her a fee for each one (hook 2). She does not know what happens inside and does not want to. The boots are the Dray crew's. She sweeps around them every morning because she cannot bring herself to move them and cannot bring herself to throw them out. Clause 9 of her contract makes Ostrander Recovery the beneficiary of each hunter's Association death benefit "for the term of engagement."

**Beats.**
- The yard in drizzle. The pay: 15 a shard (the Association pays 40), 100 a name tape.
- The form. It asks for each hunter's name, rank, height, weight and shoe size, "for the insurance."
- The loaner bin. June lends each hunter one thing, docked from pay if it is not returned: an Iron Dagger, a Padded Cap, Hunter's Leather, a road flare, a shard clicker, or the one radio.
- The advance: 50 gold now, against the pay.
- The order of march: who goes through first.

**The wrong detail.** The seven pairs of boots. If asked, June says, "They'll be back for them." Then, a beat later: "I'm not paid to move boots."

**The decisions.**
- **The contract.** Read it or sign it. A hunter who reads it finds clause 9. They can sign anyway (they need the money, and she is the only one hiring D-ranks this week), or make her strike it. She strikes it at once, smiling, "Habit." But a hunter who made her strike it gets no advance. Signing unread is the easiest thing in the world.
- **The advance.** 50 gold is a week of rent. Taking it means owing June, and June keeps a ledger.
- **The loaner.** One each. Whatever they take is a lean (section 4). Whoever takes the radio will be the one June talks to.
- **The form.** Every player says their hunter's height and shoe size aloud. A player who refuses, or lies, is making a choice too.

**In the app.**
- The Cannery Gate is on the board, its break day past. Enter it at the end of the chapter.
- The advance is an Award of 50 gold to those who take it. The loaners are Awards of the loaner items. Write each on the tally sheet.
- No fight.

**Ready sign** (GM only): none. Move on when the last signature is down.

**How to scare them here.**
- Ask each player, out loud, for their hunter's height and shoe size. Write the answers down where they can see you do it. Do not say why.
- Play June warm and busy and a little funny. The warmer she is, the colder the boots. Describe the boots once and never again unless asked.
- When they head for the gate, have June call after the one with the radio, cheerfully: "Count everything."

### Chapter 2: Sweepers

**Told** (read aloud)

> Inside, the gate is an E-rank cave like any other. Wet stone. A low roof. A smell like a wet dog that ate something it shouldn't have. Your lamps find the shards at once: little blue chips in the walls, worth less than the batteries in your lamps.
>
> The last crew did their job. The goblins are dead, dragged into a heap at the first bend, stacked the way you'd stack firewood.
>
> The work is slow. Your knees get wet. Every few minutes the radio clicks, and June asks for a count.

**What is really going on.** The warren is the gate's real E-rank dungeon, and the Dray crew cleared it in an afternoon. After they went below, the gate tried the dead goblins for its empty offices first. They were too small.

**Beats.**
- Shard work. Each hunter finds 2d6 Mana Crystal Shards over the hour.
- Two Giant Centipedes in the drain channel, feeding on the heap. A dull, ugly fight, almost a relief.
- The radio, asking for the count, by name.

**The wrong detail.** Every goblin in the heap has a thin chalk line drawn around its wrist. Some have one around the neck. One has a line down its spine, straight as a seam.

**The decisions.**
- **The count.** June wants every shard. The contract says they are hers. A shard sells for 40 at the Association. The one with the clicker can report the true count, or not. Pocketing three shards is the difference between rent and no rent. It is also theft, and June is counting too.
- **The heap.** It stinks and it feeds the centipedes. June says don't burn anything, the vent is blocked, and she will dock for smoke. Burning it is cleaner and costs money.
- **The chalk.** Touch it, rub it off, or leave it. A hunter who touches it gets chalk on their fingers that does not wash off. Rubbing it off the goblins feels decent and is also destroying the only clue.
- **The ears.** June pays 5 gold a goblin ear as proof the gate is clear. These goblins were killed by someone else, eleven days ago.

**In the app.**
- Fight: Giant Centipede 2 (E, existing).
- Shards: an Award of Mana Crystal Shards to each hunter, the count they rolled. Note who reported honestly.
- Ears: a misc item "Goblin Ear" worth 5, or 5 gold each, given by Award.

**Ready sign** (GM only): a fight in the Cannery Gate ended.

**How to scare them here.**
- Make it boring on purpose. Spend ten real minutes on the shards, the wet, the radio. The table should be a little bored when the chalk lines come.
- Describe the chalk once, as you would describe a stain. If someone asks whether the crew did it: "Crews mark walls. You've never seen anyone mark a body." If they look closely: the lines are perfectly even.
- Have June ask for the count by the hunter's name, and repeat the number back wrong by one. When corrected: "Sorry. Long day."

### Chapter 3: The ones who hid

**Told** (read aloud)

> Past the heap, the cave narrows to a crawl. Something in there is breathing.
>
> There are goblins in the crawl. Five of them, packed into a hollow no bigger than the back seat of a car, with an older one at the front holding a bone staff. They are starving. They do not reach for their knives. When your light touches them they turn their faces to the wall.
>
> They are wearing clothes. Hunter clothes, cut down to their size. A canvas work jacket with the sleeves taken up. Cargo trousers hemmed at the ankle. On one jacket, a name tape: SOLE.

**What is really going on.** These goblins hid when the Dray crew came through, and lived. Then the gate dressed them in the crew's clothes, measured them, and set them aside. They know what is below. The shaman wants the hunters to go down. Whether it wants them to end the thing, or to be taken instead of its own, is never answered. One goblin has a chalk line around its throat, and it wants to die before the gate comes back for it.

**Beats.**
- The goblins do not fight. If attacked, they cover their faces.
- The shaman taps the floor twice, pointing down, then touches its own throat.
- The goblin with the chalked throat crawls forward and lifts its chin.
- The shaman holds out a knot of hair and twine to the first hunter who lowers a weapon.
- The radio: "Goblins clear?"

**The wrong detail.** The hems. Small, even stitches, a professional's work, done in a cave with no light.

**The decisions.**
- **The contract.** It says clear the gate, and June pays 5 an ear. They are monsters, and goblins kill people. They are also starving, and wearing the clothes of the crew the hunters were sent to find. Kill them, spare them, or try to drive them out of the gate (they will not go, and that should frighten someone).
- **The one that asks.** It wants to die. Do it quickly, refuse, or look away while someone else does it.
- **Food.** They are starving. A ration given is a ration not eaten later (the giver takes 10 fatigue at the end of the chapter). Eating your own ration in front of them is also a choice.
- **The name tape.** SOLE is worth 100 gold. It is sewn onto a jacket a shivering creature is wearing.
- **The knot.** Take it or not.
- **The shaman's way.** Follow where it points, now, before the others agree.

**In the app.**
- If they attack: a fight with Goblin 5 and Goblin Shaman 1 (E, existing). Pass every goblin turn. The goblins never act. The fight log fills with their passed turns.
- Write down how many goblins were killed. Chapter 8 needs the number.
- Items: Shaman's Knot (new), Crew Tag: Sole (new), Goblin Ears.
- Food given: 10 fatigue to the giver, by the HP tap, at the chapter's end.

**Ready sign** (GM only): none.

**How to scare them here.**
- If they attack, do not describe the goblins fighting. Read each passed turn aloud in the same flat voice: "The goblin covers its face." Let the fight be as long as it takes.
- If they spare them, the goblins follow at a distance for the rest of the session, always at the edge of the lamp, and never explain why.
- Let a player who knows clothes notice the hem is better than a tailor's. Then let the quiet sit.

### Chapter 4: Not on the survey

**Told** (read aloud)

> In the warren's last chamber, the goblin chief sits against the wall, dead: a big one in rusted plate. Someone has taken its helmet off and set it beside it, neatly.
>
> Behind the chief is a stair going down.
>
> It is not on June's survey. It is not on anyone's. The steps are cut from the cave rock, but each one is the right height for a person, every one the same. Along the wall runs a handrail, worn smooth and dark by hands.
>
> Warm air comes up the stair. It smells of hot iron and starch. It smells like a shirt being pressed.

When they radio June, read:

> A long silence. Then: "Come out. I'll pay half for what you've got. Come out now."

**What is really going on.** The stair leads to the Fitting. Two of June's crews have described it before. Both came out with classes. One crew did not come out. Her silence is her doing the math. She offers half because the contract makes her liable if she sends them down. She is hoping they go anyway.

**Beats.**
- The chief (Hobgoblin, dead). A dotted chalk pattern on its chest, like a pattern on cloth.
- The stair, the rail, the warm air.
- June's offer. June's rule, if they go: someone stays at the top with the radio.
- From below, quiet, the voice of someone at a counter: "Who's first?"

**The wrong detail.** The handrail, worn smooth by many hands, in a gate two weeks old.

**The decisions.**
- **Down or out.** Half pay is real money to a D-rank. Out is safe. Out also means the Dray crew stays down there, and the hunters are the last ones who will ever look.
- **Who stays at the top.** June's rule. Someone sits alone in a dark goblin cave holding a radio, for as long as the others are gone.
- **Who's first.** The voice asks once. Whoever answers, or whoever puts a foot on the first step, is first.
- **The rail.** Holding it is the safe way down a steep stair.
- **What to tell June.** That there is a voice. Or not.

**In the app.**
- Put Measured on the first hunter to touch the rail, outside a fight, with no window and no word. Let them find it on their sheet.
- The hunter at the top: from now until they come down, send them short private lines (as a System window to that player alone, tag "!", title "STATIC", the body a plain line such as "Footsteps on the stair below you. Not your party's.").
- No fight.

**Ready sign** (GM only): none.

**How to scare them here.**
- Say "Who's first?" in your normal voice, in the middle of an ordinary sentence, like a clerk. Then carry on as if you had not said it. Do not repeat it if asked.
- For the one at the top: when they finally go down, there are more steps going down than there were going up. Let them count if they want.
- If the party takes June's half pay and leaves: let them. In the yard, June pays, and asks "Did anybody go down?" When they say no, she looks at the boots. The story waits. Next session, she calls with double pay.

### Chapter 5: The coats

**Told** (read aloud)

> The stair ends in a corridor that is warm, dry and lit. There is no lamp. The light is simply there, the yellow of an old kitchen at night.
>
> Coats hang along both walls. Hundreds of them, each on its own brass hook, each a different size. Work coats. Rain coats. Long coats. Each one has a paper ticket pinned to its collar, like a dry cleaner's.
>
> Somewhere far ahead, someone is using a pair of shears. Snip. A pause. Snip.

When they look at the tickets nearest the stair, read:

> The tickets have names on them. Your names, in neat capitals, one on each of the coats nearest you. Each coat is your size.

**System window** (to everyone at once): tag "!", title **MEASUREMENTS RECEIVED**, body "Fitting in progress." Then the Fitting quests.

**What is really going on.** This is the gate's working floor. The coats are offices waiting to be filled; the tickets are its orders. In an alcove stands the Hand's bench: the empty office of the one who does the work, with a coat on a hook that fits one hunter exactly (the one highest in Hand). Chalk Hands keep the place tidy: pale hands that walk on their fingertips, carrying pins. They come to anyone alone.

**Beats.**
- The corridor of coats. The tickets. The names, spelled exactly as each hunter wrote them on June's form, including any mistake.
- The window and the quest, arriving together.
- The Hand's bench: shears, chalk, a tape measure, a pattern book, a bowl of shards with four small holes through each.
- A brass tap in the wall, running warm water.
- A voice beside one hunter only: "Arms up."
- Chalk Hands at the edge of the light.

**The wrong detail.** Their names on the tickets.

**The decisions.**
- **Your ticket.** Take it, tear it up, or leave it on the coat. Or burn every ticket in reach: the shears stop, and then start again, closer.
- **The coat.** It fits better than anything the hunter has ever worn. It is good armor, and its bonus is real.
- **The bench.** The shears, the chalk, the tape, the pattern book, the holed shards. Each is useful. Each is the gate's.
- **Arms up.** One hunter hears it, alone. Lifting your arms is the easiest thing to do when someone asks.
- **The tap.** They have been underground for hours. The water is warm and tastes faintly of starch.
- **The hands.** Fight them, or stand still and let them measure (Measured, no fight).

**In the app.**
- Send the System window above to everyone. Then send each hunter their Fitting quest, with the progress already filled in from the tally of chapters 1 to 4. This is the scare: the quest has numbers in it before they have done anything down here.
- A private System window to the hunter who hears it: tag "!", title **ARMS UP**, no body.
- Optional fight: Chalk Hand 6 (new, E). Statuses Measured and Chalked.
- Items (section 5): Fitting Ticket (one per hunter), The Hand's Coat, Tailor's Shears, Tape Measure, Tailor's Chalk, Pattern Book, Pressed Shards.
- On the board, change the Cannery Gate's type to double. Leave its rank at E.

**Ready sign** (GM only): every hunter holds a Fitting quest.

**How to scare them here.**
- Send the quests in silence, all at once, and say nothing. Let the phones buzz. Wait until someone reads theirs aloud. If anyone asks what the numbers mean: "You don't know."
- Say "snip" yourself, at uneven times, for the rest of the session. Stop for a long while. Then say it once, close: from the other side of the coat nearest whoever took their ticket.
- If a player ticks their own Fitting quest up on their phone, let them. Mark it on the tally. That is a measurement too.

### Chapter 6: The mender

**Told** (read aloud)

> A side room off the corridor: white tile, long tables, like a clinic. On each table a coat lies flat. On a stool at the last table, a man in a hunter's vest is sewing.
>
> His name tape says MARSH. He is thin and gray, and his hands never stop. He is mending a coat that has no tear in it. When he sees you, he smiles with real relief.
>
> "Oh, thank God. You're hurt. Sit down, I can fix that. Sit down. I'll be quick."
>
> A black thread runs from the needle in his hand, across the floor, and into the wall.

**What is really going on.** Teo Marsh is the Mender, most of the way fitted. The thread is his office. He can close any wound, perfectly, and every wound he closes is a fitting of the patient. He thinks he has been here one afternoon. He talks about the crew like gossip at work: Ines "went to the table." Hal "is holding a door." Bell "is in the light." Rudy "went quiet." Margit "is up in the windows." Oskar "is with the dead, he likes it there."

**Beats.**
- Teo, kind and quick. He means every word.
- A warm loaf on his table, sliced. He eats while he works and offers.
- In one clear moment, mid-sentence, Teo looks at his hands and says, very quietly: "Don't let them make me finish."

**The wrong detail.** The thread running into the wall.

**The decisions.**
- **Let him mend you.** Full HP and no fatigue, and a seam where the wound was, neater than skin.
- **Cut the thread.** Two hits with any blade. He says "oh," like someone waking. He cannot walk; his legs have not moved in eleven days.
- **Carry him.** Someone must, the rest of the way. That hunter is slowed and has one hand free.
- **End it.** He asked. He might not ask again. He might not have meant it.
- **Leave him, with a promise.** "We'll come back for you." He believes it. He says, "Same time tomorrow?"
- **The bread.**

**In the app.**
- No fight, unless they attack him: then The Mender (Teo Marsh) 1, and he does not fight back for the first round.
- Mended: set HP full and fatigue to 0 with the HP tap. Add 1 Measured.
- Carried: put Slowed on the carrier outside the fight, until removed. Remove it when they leave the gate.
- Items: Teo's Needle Case, Warm Loaf, Crew Tag: Marsh.
- Write down what happened to Teo. Chapters 9 and 10 need it.

**Ready sign** (GM only): none. You will know.

**How to scare them here.**
- Play Teo as the kindest person the hunters have ever met. Make his mending feel wonderful: the pain going out like a tide. Then describe the seam. "Neat. Better than your own skin."
- Later, whenever that hunter is near a coat, tell them the seam itches.
- End session one here, as the party leaves Teo's room, with "snip" from close behind them.

### Chapter 7: Number off

**Told** (read aloud)

> The corridor opens into a gallery, long and high, with tall windows down one side. Through the first window is the cannery yard. June's van. The drizzle. June herself, on her phone, pacing.
>
> The next window shows the same yard at night. The next, in snow.
>
> At the far end of the gallery, a woman sits in a chair facing the last window. There is a rifle across her knees. She has not blinked since you came in. Her name tape says SOLE.

When they reach the last window, read:

> The last window shows the yard, and the row of boots by the freezer door. Seven pairs. Then more pairs, after them, in a row. Yours. The laces tucked in.

Margit, when they reach her:

> "Don't stand where it can see the whole of you. Oh. Too late." She doesn't turn her head. "It's been taking your measure since you signed. Everything you've done. That's how it decides what you'll be." A pause. "Number off. Out loud. Do it now."

**What is really going on.** Margit Sole is the Watch. She cannot stop looking, and she sees the whole gate at once. She is still mostly herself. Rudy Fenn, the Quiet, has walked at the back of the party since the stair: he was the extra footsteps the hunter at the top heard. He does not attack unless a hunter is alone, or the party turns on itself. Margit can see him. She cannot close her own eyes, and she asks the hunters to do it for her. ### The two exceptions

**Unfitted: a hunter who refuses everything.** A hunter who took nothing from below the stair, refused at least three times (choices 3, 11, 32, 36, 74, or not eating when the others did) and has no office above 2 marks is Unfitted. The gate could not get a measurement. In chapter 11 their window reads **MEASUREMENT FAILED** instead of YOU WILL DO, and their job quest names no job.
- **What it does.** No class skill. A passive, "No Size on File": a worn status that negates the first status put on them in each fight.
- **Its mark.** Gates notice them. Every Job Change window that ever comes for them reads NO SIZE ON FILE, and the GM may offer a class later only through another gate's fitting. The Cannery Gate keeps trying: once a session, a Chalk Hand's touch, nowhere near a gate.

**Captain: a hunter who takes everything.** A hunter who took six or more things from below the stair, ate, wore the Hand's coat, answered "Who's first," and walked out in the gray coat is the Captain, whatever the tally says. Wearing the coat out (way 4) alone, without the rest, still gives Captain if Captain is their top office; otherwise it gives their top office and the Captain's mark as well.
- **Identity.** The one the gate wanted most.
- **Signature.** "Last Order" (support): every ally gains Hasted for 1 round, and one ally takes an extra action, once a fight. "Who's First" (curse): taunts a foe and puts frightened.
- **Its mark: the Vacancy.** They are the gate's captain, on leave. In every gate they enter, the monsters wait one round for an order before they fight. That helps, until the day they stop waiting. The gate will call them back (hook 1). Title "On Leave" (Presence +3, Discipline -2).

### The seven jobs that exist

Each existing job is one of the gate's offices. Its gate name is what the Fitting quest's objective says.

**Fighter (the Hand).** Went first, answered "Who's first," killed when it was asked for, struck the last blow.

**Tanker (the Door).** Took the cap or the leather, stood between, held the booth door, walked last, stood over the fallen.

**Healer (the Mender).** Fed the goblins, let Teo mend them, carried his needle case, closed wounds in the fights.
*The moment.* June has split a knuckle on the van door. You are across the yard before you decide to be. You press the cut closed with your thumb and you feel, very exactly, where the skin wants to go. When you let go, there is a seam. A neat one.

**Mage (the Lamp).** Took the flare, read the pattern book, fought with fire or Bell's lighter.

- *Mark.* You always feel a weight across your shoulders. It is heavier on the anniversary. Title "Carries the Weight" (Vitality +2, Agility -1).
- *Choices.* Carried Teo, stood between the goblins and a blade, stood over the fallen.
- *The moment.* You set down your pack in the yard. Your shoulders stay bowed. Whatever you were carrying, you are still carrying it.

**7. Latchkeeper** (gate name: Latch)
- *Identity.* You decide which side of a door a person is on.
- *Signature.* "Shut" (a field, a line 3 tiles long): a foe who enters it is rooted.
- *Mark.* You cannot leave a door open. You know without looking who is on the other side of the door you shut in the gate, and some nights they knock. Title "Behind the Door" (Discipline +2, Presence -1).
- *Choices.* Took the radio, stayed at the top alone, shut the booth door on someone, named someone else first.
- *The moment.* The van's door is open. You close it. On the other side, faint, someone knocks twice, the way you knock to say "I'm still here."

**8. Witness** (gate name: Eye)
- *Identity.* You looked, and you kept looking.
- *Signature.* "I See You" (support): reveals a hidden foe, and allies gain advantage against it for 1 round.
- *Mark.* You blink less. You cannot look away from something terrible, and you remember all of it. Title "Saw It All" (Instinct +2, Discipline -1).
- *Choices.* Read the contract, asked about the boots, looked through every window, asked Margit, told June the truth.
- *The moment.* June is lying to you about something small. You watch her do it. You will remember her face doing it for the rest of your life.

**9. Understudy** (gate name: Stand-in)
- *Identity.* You fit wherever you are put.
- *Signature.* "Stand In" (support): swap places with an ally and take a shield for 1 round. A passive: +1 to the stat the party's highest-level hunter has most of, chosen at each session's start.
- *Mark.* Your reflection is a beat late. People call you by the wrong name, and you answer. Title "Wears It Well" (Agility +2, Presence -1).
- *Choices.* Tried on the coat, lifted their arms, stood still for the hands.
- *The moment.* In the van's window you see yourself. You raise a hand. A moment later, so does your reflection.

**10. Signatory** (gate name: Seal)
- *Identity.* You make deals, and deals keep you.
- *Signature.* "Terms" (support): an ally gains Hasted and Strength now, and when it ends, they take damage (a delayed end).
- *Mark.* You owe. Once a session the GM may offer you a deal; refuse, and you lose 1 HP per level until the next session. Title "Under Contract" (Strength +2, Discipline -1).
- *Choices.* Signed unread, took the advance, lied to June, left Teo with a promise, let another sit, sold the shards.
- *The moment.* June slides your pay across the bumper with a receipt to sign. You sign without reading it. Then you read it. It is longer than it was.

**11. Gleaner** (gate name: Pocket)
- *Identity.* Nothing is left behind while you are near it.
- *Signature.* "Pick Clean" (strike): moves up to 2 stacks of a foe's helpful status to the hunter.
- *Mark.* Your pockets fill with things you did not take: buttons, paper tickets, a tooth. Title "Light Fingers" (Agility +2, Presence -1).
- *Choices.* Took the advance, pocketed shards, took the tape off the goblin, cut ears, searched the crew, sold the shards.
- *The moment.* You put your hand in your pocket for your keys. There is a button in it. Blue, with four small holes. You did not take it.

**12. Keener** (gate name: Mourner)
- *Identity.* The dead are not done talking, and you listen.
- *Signature.* "Lament" (support, an aura of 2 tiles): cures frightened on allies and gives them Discipline. Foes that were once people take Discipline -.
- *Mark.* At the edge of sleep you hear the dead. They want their names said. Title "Hears Them" (Discipline +2, Strength -1).
- *Choices.* Asked about the boots, rubbed the chalk off, left Teo with a promise, spoke names, closed Margit's eyes, kept the tags for the families.
- *The moment.* By the freezer door, you crouch and straighten one boot that has fallen over. Seven people, somewhere, say thank you.

**13. Last Friend** (gate name: Mercy)
- *Identity.* When it has to be done, you are the one asked.
- *Signature.* "Mercy Stroke" (strike): more damage the lower the target's share of HP (an execute scaler). Nothing kills outright.
- *Mark.* People ask you. The dying find you. Title "The One Asked" (Strength +2, Presence -1).
- *Choices.* Killed the goblin that asked, took the shears, ended Teo, closed Margit's eyes, let Teo or the shaman sit.
- *The moment.* A gull in the yard has a broken wing. It walks straight to you, and waits.

**14. Deserter** (gate name: Back Door)
- *Identity.* You know where the way out is. Always.
- *Signature.* "Gone" (support, self): Hasted and untargetable for 1 round. A passive, Agility +2.
- *Mark.* When you run, someone behind you pays. The System keeps count of the times you left, and shows it. Title "The One Who Left" (Agility +3, Presence -2).
- *Choices.* Wanted June's half pay, ran by the stair, left by the stair only they could see.
- *The moment.* The yard has three gates out. You know, before you look, which one you would use. You have already counted the steps.

### How a table of five comes out different

With twenty-three possible results (seven jobs, fourteen new classes, Unfitted and Captain) and the one-office rule, no two hunters at a table of five can come out the same. A player who wants a class can steer toward it, and the Fitting quest shows them how close they are. That is the gate's bargain made visible: the closer they steer, the more they are choosing to be fitted.

## 6. What stays wrong afterward

1. **The Vacancy.** The Captain's office went out in a coat, or someone sat in the chair. If a hunter wears it, gates begin to wait for their order, and one day a gate opens near their home with a door painted green. If a hunter sat, they are down there, and once a session their old player may receive a System window from them. A rescue story, later, is the one place this campaign's Resurrection Crystal might be earned.
2. **June knew.** Four crews this year, a ledger of who came out and what they became, and a fee from the Association's placement desk for each classed hunter. The Job Change dungeon is a fitting room the Association keeps. Who at the Association knows, and how high it goes, is a later story.
3. **One more in the count.** Rudy Fenn, the Quiet, came out with them. When they number off, there is one more. In photos there is a blur at the back. He is not hostile. He is waiting to be given an order, and he does not know by whom.
4. **The buttons.** June sold the Pressed Shards on. They turn up sewn onto a C-rank boss's coat, in a gate far from the river. Every gate that has one has a coat in each hunter's size.
5. **A name nobody should know.** The last page of the Pattern Book has a name in it, the name of whoever cuts the patterns. The GM picks it and never writes it in the app. Whoever read it knows it. If it is said aloud, the shears stop everywhere, for a moment, and then start again closer to the one who said it.
6. **Teo.** If carried out, he lives, in a hospital ward, mending the sheets at night. He wants to go back. He says it is nicer there. If he sat in the chair, he writes, sometimes, on hospital notepaper that nobody brought him.
7. **The gate does not close.** It reads detected every morning, rank E. If the chair was burned, it broke instead, and something walked out into the cannery: no stat block, just a figure with long arms seen once on the pier at dusk, carrying a bundle of coats.
8. **Your order is ready.** Anyone who took their ticket gets a System window months later: **YOUR ORDER IS READY**. Body: a place, and a time.

## 7. Running it

### Pacing horror at a table with phones

- **Phones face down.** Ask the table to keep phones face down until one buzzes. A buzz then means something. Never send a window just to fill a gap.
- **Slow at the start, fast at the end.** Chapters 1 and 2 should feel almost too long. Chapter 8 should feel like there is no time to think.
- **One wrong detail, once.** Describe it plainly, once, and move on. If they ask, answer flatly. Never underline it.
- **Silence is a tool.** After a wrong detail, say nothing for five seconds. Players fill silence with their own fears, and theirs are better than yours.
- **Light and sound.** Dim the room. Keep one small bell or glass for chapter 8 and use it for nothing else. Say "snip" yourself.
- **Never explain the Cutter.** If someone asks what it is, say "You don't know." It is the most frightening sentence a GM has.

### Using the app to frighten

- **A message only one player gets.** A System window sent to one hunter alone: the hunter at the top of the stair (chapter 4), "ARMS UP" (chapter 5), Margit's true things (chapter 7), the class moment (chapter 11). Let the others see the player read it and not know what it said.
- **A status nobody asked for.** Measured, put on a hunter's sheet outside any fight with no word (chapter 4). Let them find it themselves. If they ask, "You don't know when that happened."
- **A quest that should not exist.** The Fitting quest, sent with numbers already in it (chapter 5). The later Job Change quest that arrives with its objective already done (chapter 11).
- **The board.** The gate stays rank E while it becomes hell. Its break day passed and it did not break. Next session, it is back.
- **The fight log.** In chapter 3, the log of passed goblin turns is more frightening than any monster.

### Safety, in three sentences

Before the first session, ask each player for anything they do not want at the table (body horror, being trapped, harm to animals, anything else), and cut it from this story without asking why. Put a card or a tap on the table that anyone can use to skip a moment, no explanation needed, and honor it at once. At the break and at the end, ask how everyone is doing, and mean it.

### Two players

- Waves: wave 1 is Chalk Hand 3; wave 2 is The Door alone; wave 3 is The Keeper with half the Hemmed Goblins.
- Ines is an elite, not a boss. Drop "Shift's Over".
- Make Teo able to fight beside them if carried: add him to their side as a row with "Mend".
- With two hunters the one-office rule rarely matters, so lean harder into the marks.
- Have the one at the top of the stair be Teo's voice on the radio, instead of a hunter left alone.

### Six players

- Add one Chalk Hand per hunter above four to each wave, and one Hemmed Goblin to wave 3.
- Ines: HP factor 1.6, and The Door fights beside her whether or not he survived chapter 8.
- Run the Quiet fight in chapter 7. With six, someone is always alone.
- Keep the items at more than the number of hunters, so there is always something to want and someone left without it.
- Split the table for chapter 7: half go to Margit, half go after the count. Cut between them.

## 8. What the app needs to run this

### What exists today

- A gate on the board with a rank, a type (normal, red, double, break) and a break day, and the GM's edit to change type and status (chapters 5, 10, 12).
- The built-in monsters used here: Giant Centipede, Goblin, Goblin Shaman, Hobgoblin. The monster maker for the new ones, with abilities by kind (strike, shot, blast, curse, shield), starting statuses, and an HP factor.
- The built-in statuses used here: bleeding, burning, frightened, prone, restrained, slowed, stunned, blinded, stealth, hasted, regenerating. The status maker for Measured, Chalked and Hemmed, with stacks and a threshold that bursts.
- The built-in items used here: Mana Crystal Shard, Iron Dagger, Padded Cap, Hunter's Leather, Health Potion. The item maker for the rest, with a worn status where an item needs one.
- The skill maker for each new class's signature, with fields, auras, a delayed end, guard, swap, untargetable, scalers and stacking curses.
- A quest sent to chosen players with objectives and progress already set (the Fitting quest), a quest of kind job whose reward names any job, a title with a bonus, an Award of gold and items.
- A System window sent to one player or to all.
- A status put on a player's sheet outside a fight.
- The Job Change chain for the seven built-in jobs, whose standard steps are three waves and a Knight Captain.
- The odds of a fight before it starts, the fight's Add to the fight for waves, passing a turn, and fields.
- Battle maps, if the GM wants them.

### What the story feature will provide

- This story as content: a name, chapters with their words, turned on by the GM for the table and moved chapter by chapter by the GM's hand.
- A ready sign per chapter, shown on the GM's phone only (a fight ended, a quest held by every hunter, a boss defeated).
- A quest or a reward attached to a chapter, given when the GM opens it.
- A chapter's words sent to one hunter only.

### What would be new

- **The tally.** A place on the GM's phone to mark each hunter's choices against offices, and see the leader. Today it is paper.
- **Changing progress on a quest already on a player's phone.** The Fitting quest should move as the gate measures. Today the GM sends it once, with its numbers, and sends a second one if needed.
- **A quest the player sees but cannot tick.** Today a player may tick a GM's quest up, which this story turns into a measurement, but a GM might want it locked.
- **The new classes as jobs.** Today a job quest's reward can name any class. Giving the new classes their own Job Change chains would mean entries in jobs.ts.
- **A ready sign that reads a bag or a sheet** (the Shaman's Knot held, Measured on a hunter). Today a sign that needs these is the GM's own eye.
- **A title that carries a status**, so a class's mark can be a worn status as well as a stat line.
- **A private line that is not the System's.** A GM's whisper to one phone, plain and untagged, for the footsteps at the back of the line. Today a System window does it, with its tag.
