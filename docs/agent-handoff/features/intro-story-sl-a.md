# Next of kin

The opening mission, version A. It is set in the world of Solo Leveling, with people and places of our own. It is written for four or five D-rank hunters at level 10 and a GM, over two sessions of about four hours each.

The players call the job the Tanner Street sweep. "Next of kin" stays the GM's name for it until chapter 6, when a System window says it to one phone.

## 1. For the owner

### The premise

The hunters are D rank and behind on what they owe. Pruitt Reclaim hires them at piece rate to sweep an E-rank gate in the boiler room of a coin laundry. The work is to pry crystal shards out of the walls, cut the essence stones out of the goblins, and bring back the tagged gear that a crew of eight left inside nine days ago. Dee Pruitt says that crew walked off the job.

The gate lets people in and shows them no way out. Its whole inside is four rooms. Three of the eight are still alive in the second room, after a hundred and thirty-one days by their count. They have found out what happens in a place that small when a hunter dies, and they have lived in one room with each other ever since.

### The law this story breaks

A hunter is measured on the day they awaken, and the rank they are given is the rank they die with. Years of training and good gear make a hunter strong inside the rank. The Hunter's Association has re-measured thousands of hunters who swore they had grown past theirs, and it has never moved one.

Chapter 1 spends its time on this before anything goes wrong. A license card has the rank pressed into the plastic. Pruitt pays by rank. Her checker has been E for nineteen years. Across the street a guild team of the hunters' own age is unloading for a real gate.

### What is truly wrong in this dungeon

The players can piece this together. Nobody tells them all of it.

1. When something awakened dies, what it had does not die with it. It passes whole to the nearest one of its kind that can hold it.
2. In the open, that is never the person beside you. A hunter is already full, there are thousands of ordinary people within a mile of any death, and an empty person is easier to fill. In an ordinary dungeon there is a whole country past the gate, and what the dead had is gone into it before anyone feels a thing.
3. This gate sealed behind the first crew that entered it. Hunters call a sealed gate a red gate. The Association has never recorded one below C rank, or one this small. Inside there are four rooms and no empty people, so the nearest one who can hold a dead hunter's awakening is the hunter holding their hand.
4. Two of a rank make the next. A D-rank who receives a second D-rank's awakening reads C on any meter in the world. It takes one death per rank, beside you, in a room nobody can leave.
5. The goblins live by the same rule. Every goblin the first crew killed made the nearest goblin heavier. That is why no body in this dungeon has an essence stone in it. The last goblin sits behind a stone door holding thirty-one others, and the crew cannot kill it at the ranks they have. Somebody in the room has to hold more, and they all know where more comes from.

Point 2 is a dead man's theory, chalked on a wall and rubbed out. If it is true, every awakening in the world is an inheritance from a stranger who died that day. The story never confirms it. It is yours to keep or cut.

So a rank comes from a person, and the story says which person every time. The System's window and the title on the hunter's sheet both name them, and a page in an old woman's notebook says who they left behind and what they asked for.

The clues, in the order they arrive:

1. A form that asks for next of kin.
2. Goblins that keep their distance.
3. Goblins that stop fighting and crowd in to touch a dying one.
4. A goblin that hits harder afterward.
5. Bodies with no stone in them.
6. A far knock after each kill.
7. A wall where the gate was, with 131 marks cut in it.
8. Bedrolls laid as far apart as a room allows.
9. An old woman who writes down the day each newcomer awakened.
10. Five graves, and a healer who was beside every one.
11. A foreman who counts the party twice.
12. Sums rubbed off a wall.
13. A rope with two frayed ends.
14. A window on one phone.

Five things are never answered: why this gate sealed, whether the theory in point 2 is true, whether Garrick Tull fell, why the last goblin knocks, and whether Nell Aldous sat with her dying because it was her job or because she had done the sum before anyone else.

### Why a hunter gets a class there

A second awakening does not lie flat on top of the first. It arrives with its person in it: their trade, their habits, the last thing they asked for. The System will not leave two people in one hunter unnamed, so it issues a Job Change, and the job is its name for what the two make together.

Usually that is the dead hunter's trade. A shield man's awakening makes a Tanker. But the way it came to you can mark you more deeply than the dead did. A hunter who took an awakening by force and a hunter who was asked to take it come out as different things, even from the same deathbed. Section 4 has the seven jobs the game ships and nine of our own.

### The shape

Chapters 1 and 2 are a bad shift at a bad job. In chapter 3 the gate is gone. Chapters 4 to 8 are people in a room, and nothing in them attacks unless a player starts it. Chapter 9 is the only chapter that is mostly a fight. Chapters 10 and 11 are the way out and the parking lot.

The fear starts when the players learn the rule. From then on every person in the room is worth a rank to every other person in it, and the GM keeps asking who is sitting nearest.

Session one ends with chapter 6, on the window. Session two is chapters 7 to 11.

## 3. The mission

### Chapter 1: Piece work

**Read aloud.**

> The Sunny Coin Laundry on Tanner Street has been shut since a gate opened in its boiler room. The Association measured the gate at E, the lowest there is, and sold the clearing rights for less than the price of a dryer.
>
> Pruitt Reclaim bought them. Dee Pruitt has a folding table in the parking lot, a cash box, and a stack of forms held down with a wrench. Next to her sits a man of about fifty with a kitchen scale in his lap. His license hangs from his neck with the E turned out, and it is plain he has been told to wear it that way.
>
> "Shards are fifteen each," Pruitt says. "Goblin stones, fifteen. Anything with a name tag on it is mine, and I pay a hundred to get it back. The last crew walked off and left it all in there. Tobe weighs what you carry out, and Tobe's count is the count. Sign at the bottom. Name, rank, license number, the day you awakened, next of kin."
>
> Across the street a guild van is unloading for a real gate. Six hunters in matching gray, none of them older than you. One of them looks over at the folding table and then looks away.

**What is really happening.** Pruitt has no idea what the gate is. She has lost a crew and eight sets of loaned gear, and she has already sent bills for the gear to the families on the last crew's forms. Tobe does not want to go in. He is going in because she docked him for the last crew's scale.

**Beats.** The Association's gate clerk waves a wand over each hunter at the tape and reads the rank aloud, a D for each hunter and an E for Tobe. Pruitt offers an advance of 50 gold against the pay, and one loaned Health Potion for the party, docked at 50 if it is used. Tobe, if anyone talks to him: "I woke up E on a Thursday. They told me the rank and they told me that was it, and it was. Nineteen years. You'll see. You get good at what you are."

**Decisions.**
- The form. Each player says aloud the day their hunter awakened and who their next of kin is. A real name, "none", or a lie are all choices.
- The advance. Fifty gold is a week's rent. Taking it means Pruitt holds a note on you.
- The guild team. One of them awakened the same week as one of the hunters and tested C. Ask that player who it is. They can cross the street and say hello, or keep their back turned.
- Who carries Tobe's scale and the empty bags. He is fifty-two and he will not ask.

**In the app.** The gate is on the board at E. Send the quest "Tanner Street sweep" to everyone. Award 50 gold to each hunter who took the advance, and the Health Potion to whoever holds it. No System window. The System stays silent until chapter 6.

**Ready when.** The last form is signed.

**How to frighten them.** You cannot yet, and you should not try. Write each next of kin on your tally sheet in front of them. Ask how to spell it.

### Chapter 2: The run

**Read aloud.**

> Inside, the gate is a goblin burrow. The tunnel is too low to stand in. It runs downhill in the dark, much farther than the boiler room it started in. The shards are where shards always are, stuck in the walls like broken glass in putty. You pry them out with whatever you brought. Tobe holds the bag and counts under his breath.
>
> There are goblins. You see them at the edge of the lamp, five or six, small and gray, keeping to the far end of the tunnel. They do not rush you. They watch you work, and when you move ten feet down the tunnel they move ten feet back.
>
> Near the first bend a hunter's bow leans against the wall, strung, with a strip of cloth tape on the grip. The tape has a name on it in marker. L. MARSH.

**What is really happening.** The first crew stopped killing goblins on day 62, when Wen worked out where each one went. The goblins learned the distance at which nobody dies and have kept it since. They are six of the last seven goblins in the gate. Each kill the party makes here goes to the nearest living goblin, and the sixth goes to the Last One, far below, which knocks once on its door each time. So every fifteen-gold stone the party goes after makes the Last One stronger, and there is no stone to collect.

**Decisions.**
- Kill them or leave them. A goblin stone is fifteen gold and there are six goblins. They will not attack first. Tobe says, "It's the job," and does not look at them.
- When the first goblin goes down, the others break off, even ones in the middle of a swing, and crowd in to put their hands on it. They are easy to kill while they do. Does anyone?
- Cutting for the stone. There is none. Tobe has opened a few thousand goblins. "Even a runt's got a stone." Does anyone open a second one to be sure?
- The tagged gear. The bow here, a pair of boots and a dagger further down marked H. ROE. Each is a hundred gold from Pruitt. Who picks up what?

**In the app.** Start a fight only if the party attacks: Goblin 5, Goblin Shaman 1. The goblins pass their turns until one is hit. Each time one is defeated, put a stack of Heavier on the nearest goblin left and let the players see the chip. The goblins nearest a body spend their next turn moving next to it. Award a Mana Crystal Shard for each shard pried, and the tagged items to whoever picks them up. Mark the tally.

**Ready when.** The party has a full bag, or the fight is over.

**How to frighten them.** After each kill, knock once on the underside of the table, softly, and say nothing. If asked: "Something a long way down." Describe the crowding once, plainly, as something that looks like grief.

### Chapter 3: No ring

**Read aloud.**

> Tobe takes the first full bag back up the tunnel to weigh it in daylight. He is gone a long time for a man with one bag.
>
> When he comes back he still has it. He sets it down and sits on it.
>
> "It's a straight tunnel," he says. "There's no turn in it. I went up to where we came in and there's a wall. I put my hands on it." He holds them up, gray with dust, as if you might want to check.
>
> At the top of the tunnel, where the gate was, the rock is scratched from the floor to as high as a person can reach. Somebody has cut marks into it, in rows, in fives. There are a great many rows.

**What is really happening.** The gate sealed nine days ago behind the first crew, and it sealed again behind this one. Time runs about fifteen times faster inside. Nell cut the marks, one a day, until her knife wore down, and then she kept the count in her book.

**Beats.** There are 131 marks. Anyone who counts them gets that number, and it takes a while. Tobe knows what a sealed gate is called. "Red gates are for A-ranks. They're forests. Mountains. Nobody's ever had one in an E." If any goblins are alive they have come no closer. If the party left one alive in a fight, it is the last of the six, it has up to five stacks, and it is no longer keeping its distance.

**Decisions.**
- Who counts the marks, and whether they tell the others the number.
- Tobe is not well. Someone can stay with him, or he can be told to pull himself together, or he can be left sitting on the bag.
- Finish the heavy goblin, or go around it. It fights like a Hobgoblin now. Killing it sends the sixth knock down the tunnel.
- Down is the only direction left. Who goes first?

**In the app.** Edit the gate's type to red on the board and say nothing. Someone will look. If the last goblin of the Run is fought, it has its stacks of Heavier.

**Ready when.** The party starts down.

**How to frighten them.** Make the counter count. Let the player say the numbers aloud by fives if they will. When they reach the end, tell them the last eleven marks are much shallower than the rest.

### Chapter 4: The kitchen

**Read aloud.**

> The tunnel ends in a room with a fire in it. Somebody has been living here. There are bedrolls, each against a different wall, as far apart as the room allows. There is a pot, a row of water cans, and five long heaps of stones laid side by side, with a license card set on top of each.
>
> Three people are looking at you. A gray-haired woman kneels by a girl who lies under two coats. A big man stands at the far wall with a sword that he does not raise. No one speaks for a moment. Then the woman laughs, once, and puts her hand over her mouth.
>
> "How many of you?" the man says. He counts you himself before anyone answers. Then he counts you again.
>
> "It's been a hundred and thirty-one days," the woman says. "Dee told you we quit, didn't she."

**What is really happening.** Nell and Bram have kept a truce for fifty-four days. Its terms are the house rules below, and every one of them is about distance. Bram wants Nell to die with him beside her, and he has said so to her face. Nell has kept herself alive for one reason: there were only two other hunters in the room, and one of them is a dying girl who cannot move. If Nell died, all five of hers would go to Bram. Now more hunters have walked in. To Nell they are enough hands to divide her dead among. To Bram they are that many more awakenings in the room.

**Beats.** They are generous with what they have, which is water and a gray stew nobody should ask about. Bram gives the house rules as if reading a fire notice. Nobody sleeps within arm's reach of anybody. Nobody sits with Pim but Nell. Nobody goes to the well alone, and nobody goes to the well in twos. On one wall there is a patch scrubbed paler than the rock around it, with a few chalk figures left at the edge: "2 D =" and under it "8 =". Over the stew Nell asks each newcomer what day they awakened, and writes the answers in a notebook without saying why.

**Decisions.**
- Where each hunter puts their bedroll. Ask each player, and put the tokens on the map. Bram watches where they go.
- Whether to share the lunch they brought. It is the first bread these people have seen in four months.
- The graves. Asking the names is a choice. Saying them back is another.
- Whether to keep the house rules without being told why. Someone will ask why. Nell says, "Because we're still friends, and I'd like us to stay that way."
- What to tell them about the goblins upstairs. Nobody has asked yet.

**In the app.** No fight. Give Pim the built-in Regenerating in your notes: it is Nell's mending, and it is what you take away in chapter 6. Mark the tally.

**Ready when.** Everyone has a place to sleep.

**How to frighten them.** Bram counts the party twice and smiles the second time. Nell shifts so that she is between him and the newcomers, and then looks surprised at herself. When a hunter sits down near another, one of the three glances at the gap between them.

### Chapter 5: The door

**Read aloud.**

> Past the kitchen the tunnel drops to a slab of stone the size of a garage door. A tower shield is jammed under its lip, bent nearly double. The tape on the grip says O. BRANDT.
>
> "That's the boss room," Bram says. "Kill what's in there and the gate comes back, same as any gate." He holds up his left hand. It has three fingers. "I went in on day twenty with Garrick. It's a goblin. It comes up to my belt."
>
> There is a gap at the edge of the slab the width of a hand. Through it you can see a round room, a pool of water, and a goblin sitting beside the pool with its back to you. It is no bigger than the ones upstairs.
>
> "How many did you kill up there?" Nell asks from behind you. "This morning. We heard it knock."

**What is really happening.** The Last One held thirty-one goblins before today. It now holds thirty-one and the party's count. Nell and Bram want the true number because it is the size of what they have to kill. They do not explain the rule yet. Bram says only, "They get stronger when you thin them out. We found that out late."

**Decisions.**
- The number. The truth is up to six. A lie is easy and nobody can check it.
- A look, or a try. The slab opens if the shield is pulled out. A party that tries the fight now meets a thing that hits far harder than its size, and the door can be shut again if someone holds it. Whoever holds it takes the last blow.
- The shield is tagged gear worth a hundred gold. Someone may want to take it now. Bram will stop them, flatly, with his hand on their chest.
- Bram makes his offer in general terms: "It needs one of us to be a lot stronger than any of us is. There's a way. Ask Nell why she won't."

**In the app.** If they open the door, start the fight with the Last One and its stacks, and edit the gate's rank to C in the middle of the first round. Let them run. A hunter who goes down here is carried out and Nell mends them: raise HP by hand.

**Ready when.** The party is back in the kitchen and somebody has asked Nell why she won't.

**How to frighten them.** The goblin never turns around while they watch through the gap. If someone speaks to it, it raises one hand and knocks once on the floor beside it. Nell's answer to "why won't you" is to look at Pim, and then at Bram's hands.

### Chapter 6: Sitting with Pim

**Read aloud.**

> Nell has kept Pim alive for forty-one days. She does it with her hands, twice a day. Each time it takes longer, and each time she sits down harder afterward.
>
> Tonight she does not finish. She takes her hands away, looks at them, and says, "That's all there is."
>
> Pim hears her. She is twenty-two, and she carried this crew's water. She looks around the room at each of you in turn, and at Bram, who has come to stand a little closer than he was.
>
> "Somebody sit with me," she says. "I don't mind who. I'd like to know who."

**What is really happening.** Pim will die before morning whatever anyone does. She knows the rule. She is asking who gets her, and she is too polite to say so. Her awakening is E. To a D-rank it is strength inside the rank and no more. Bram wants it anyway, because Tobe is also E, and two E's make a D. Nell would take it gladly to keep it from him, and it would be her sixth.

**Decisions.**
- Who sits. One hunter can hold her hand. Pim will choose if asked to, and she chooses whoever shared their lunch or asked her name.
- The Health Potion. It buys Pim one more day, and Pruitt docks 50 for it. It does not save her.
- Bram. He says, "She's E. It's half of nothing. Let me, and I'll stay clear of the rest of you." He is lying about the last part and may not know it.
- Stepping back. A hunter who wants no part of this has to be farther from the bed than someone else, and stay there. Whoever they stand behind is who they have chosen.
- Asking Nell what is going on. She says, "Sit with her and you'll know. I'm sorry. It isn't something I can say and have you believe."

**In the app.** Put the tokens on the kitchen map. Before the end, ask the table, flatly, "Who is nearest to Pim?" and count tiles. Pim dies. Send one System window to the nearest hunter's phone only. Tag: ! NEXT OF KIN. Title: Pim Okafor. Body: "Pim Okafor, rank E, has died. You were nearest. What she had is yours." Award that hunter 5 ability points and the title "Next of kin: Pim Okafor" (Presence +1). If Bram or Nell was nearest, send nothing, and describe Bram's eyes closing for a second.

**Ready when.** The window has been read. End session one here.

**How to frighten them.** Ask "who is nearest" before anyone knows why it matters, and write the answer down. After the window, do not explain it. Let the player decide whether to read it to the table. From this chapter on, ask who is nearest to whom at odd moments, including when nobody is dying.

### Chapter 7: The arithmetic

**Read aloud.**

> Bram waits until the stones are on her. Then he says it, since someone has to.
>
> "You felt it," he says to the one who sat with her. "So now you know what we know. It goes to whoever's nearest. Two E's make a D. Two D's make a C. Wen worked it out on that wall before he died, and Nell rubbed it off."
>
> He points as he counts. "Nell's holding five. I'm holding two. That thing behind the door is holding every goblin we ever killed, and yours. Somebody in this room has to hold enough to kill it, and there's one way to get it."
>
> He looks at each of you. He has done this sum many times.
>
> "Everybody in here dies sooner or later. All I'm asking is that it goes to someone who can use it."

**What is really happening.** Bram is right about the arithmetic and he believes he is the man to carry it. His one fixed plan is to be nearest to Nell when she goes. Whether he hurts anyone who stands in his way depends on them, and he has not made up his mind to. Tobe has understood that he is worth half a rank to anyone in the room who already has the other half, and one hunter may now be exactly that.

**Beats.** Bram produces the first crew's work order. On the back, in eight hands, is an agreement: whatever any of them holds at the end is owed in equal shares to the families of all eight. Five of the signatures belong to graves. He offers the party the bottom of the page. Later, Tobe finds one hunter alone: "Stay near me. If something happens I'd want it to be you. You'll tell Annie." Later still, Bram finds a different hunter alone: "Help me be the one beside Nell, and the next one's yours. There's the checker. He's old."

**Decisions.**
- Sign the agreement or refuse it. Signing means everything a hunter earns at the new rank is owed in shares, for life, to people named on forms.
- Tobe's request. Saying yes means staying within a few tiles of him from now on.
- Bram's offer. Send it as a private window to one phone and tell nobody else.
- The rope. Garrick's rope is coiled by the well and both ends are frayed. Bram wears Garrick's boots. Nobody saw. Does the party decide what happened anyway?
- Bram himself. They can leave him be, watch him in shifts, tie him, send him up to the Run alone, or kill him. He reads C, and if he dies in this room, Garrick and Bram go to the two hunters nearest.
- Each other. Somebody at the table will do the sum on the party. Let them.

**In the app.** Bram's offer and Tobe's request are windows to one phone each. If the party fights Bram, use his row and the kitchen map. A hunter who goes down within Nell's reach is mended. If Bram dies, send two windows a minute apart, nearest first: Garrick Tull (D), then Bram Hask (D). Each is a rank.

**Ready when.** The party has settled what to do about Bram and where everyone sleeps.

**How to frighten them.** Count the players the way Bram does, pointing. Send one private window with nothing in it but a title, "Stay near me", to the phone of the player Tobe chose. During the night watch, tell one player that when they woke, another hunter's bedroll was a little closer than it had been. Do not say whose. Nobody moved it on purpose.

### Chapter 8: Nell's book

**Read aloud.**

> Nell has a notebook, the kind with a spiral at the top. She opens it on her knee.
>
> "One page each," she says. "Name, rank, who they left, what they asked me for. I told every one of them yes. I was never going to manage any of it from in here."
>
> She looks at the things you carried down the tunnel: the bow, the knives, the boots. "Hold on to something of theirs. It gives them somewhere to go. Otherwise it's whoever is closest, and I'd like them to go to someone who knew their name."
>
> She lies back against the wall beside the heaps of stones. "I stopped mending myself this morning. It won't be long. They'll come out of me the way they went in, one at a time, last one first. Wen, then Lida, Hadley, Osk. Then me. Whoever gets one reads the page out loud and tells me they'll see to it. That's the price."

**What is really happening.** Nell took a wound at the door on day 62 and has held it shut with her own mending ever since. She has waited for more hands than Bram's, and she has them. She asked the hunters small questions all through chapters 4 to 7, and she has an opinion of each. She chooses who gets whom only if she is asked. Her own hand she gives to the hunter who seemed to want it least.

**Decisions.**
- Who holds what. Three of the dead have tagged gear in the party's hands. Osk's shield is still under the door, so Osk goes to whoever put a hand on it or held the door. A license card off a grave also counts when nobody holds that person's gear. The players now know what the gear is for, and they picked it up for a hundred gold apiece before they knew. Let them trade and argue over it. A hunter who hands their item to another is giving up that person.
- Whether to let her. Somebody may say no. Nell answers, "Then find me another way to get that girl's water carrier and that old man out of here. I've had four months to look."
- The promise. Each heir reads a page aloud and says they will see to it. A player may say it and mean it, or say it and tell you privately that they do not.
- Leaving the room. A hunter who wants none of it must be farther away than everyone else when each one comes, in a room a few paces wide.
- Bram, if he is free. He walks to the bed. Somebody has to decide what happens then.
- The book. Nell holds it out to nobody in particular at the end.

**In the app.** Tokens on the map. Send the windows one at a time, a full minute apart by a real clock: ! NEXT OF KIN, then the name and "rank D". Each goes to the hunter in the room carrying that person's tagged gear. If nobody carries the gear, it goes to the hunter holding that person's license card, then to the hunter with the most tally marks for that person, and failing all of those to the nearest. Nell's own goes to the hand she is holding. After each window set that hunter's rank to C. Send the "Owed" quest for that page (section 4). Hold the Job Change until chapter 11.

**Ready when.** Five windows have gone and the last page is read.

**How to frighten them.** Say nothing during each minute, so that the only sound is a phone. Nell talks between them while she can, to each of her dead by name, as they leave: "Go on, Wen. That one. He's all right." After the fourth she does not talk. When the last window lands, tell the hunter holding her hand that it has stopped holding back.

### Chapter 9: Thirty-seven

**Read aloud.**

> With the shield pulled out, the slab drops an inch and then swings. The round room is lit by the pool. The goblin has not moved from the edge of the water. It is old. Its ears are torn, and its hands are folded in its lap.
>
> It turns its head and looks at you, one at a time, the way Bram did.
>
> Then it stands up, and the floor under your boots takes the weight of it.

**What is really happening.** The Last One holds thirty-one goblins plus the party's count. It fights like a goblin: it runs at the nearest hunter and hits with both hands. If a hunter dies here, the rule still holds, and the nearest hunter gets heavier in the middle of the fight.

**Decisions.**
- Who pulls the shield, and who keeps it.
- Where Tobe is. In the kitchen alone, or behind the party.
- Where Bram is, if he lives. He fights well and he drifts toward whoever is hurt.
- When a hunter goes down, who moves next to them and who moves away. Both can be seen on the map by everyone.
- The last blow, and who is standing over the body afterward.

**In the app.** Edit the gate's rank to C if you have not. The fight is the Last One, with one stack of Heavier per goblin the party killed. Bram is an ally row if he is with them. A hunter at 0 HP is down and makes death saves as usual. If a hunter dies, send ! NEXT OF KIN to the nearest hunter by tiles, with every name the dead hunter held, and give that hunter one stack of Heavier for the rest of the fight. When the Last One is defeated, no goblin gets a stack. The body holds one item: the Last Stone.

**Ready when.** The Last One is defeated.

**How to frighten them.** While a hunter is making death saves, move Bram's token one tile toward them each round and say only, "Bram is coming to help." Before each save, ask, "Who is nearest?" When the goblin dies, pause and look at the map as if waiting for a chip to appear. Then say that nothing comes to touch it.

### Chapter 10: The hour

**Read aloud.**

> The goblin lies beside the pool, and there is nothing left in here of its kind.
>
> There is light coming down the tunnel, and it is daylight. The gate is back. Every hunter learns the rule in the first week of licensing: when the boss dies, a gate stays open for about an hour.
>
> The tunnel is twenty minutes on your knees each way. In the kitchen there are the heaps of stones, each with a card on it. There is a notebook. There are bags of shards that are your whole pay, and gear with names taped to it at a hundred gold apiece, and each of you has two arms.

**What is really happening.** There is time for each hunter to make one trip with a full load, or two trips if both are light. What stays is gone when the gate closes. Pruitt has already billed the families for the gear, so gear left inside is a debt on eight households.

**Decisions.** Each hunter picks two loads.
- A body. One hunter carries one. A family can bury what comes out.
- A bag of shards. It is the pay, and for some of them it is rent.
- Tagged gear. It is worth a hundred gold from Pruitt, or it goes back to a family for nothing, or it stays with the heir.
- A person. Tobe if he is hurt, Bram if he is tied or down, a hunter who cannot walk.
- Small things ride free: the license cards off the graves, the book, the stone, the work order.
- Bram, if he lives and is free, takes nothing. He stands at the pool looking at the goblin, then walks out past everyone without a word.

**In the app.** Start a real timer for the table: ten minutes to decide and say what they carry. Award items as they are picked up. Complete the objectives of "Tanner Street sweep" as counted. No fight.

**Ready when.** The timer ends. Anything not named stays.

**How to frighten them.** Do not hurry them in words. Put the timer where they can see it and keep quiet. Read the names off the cards of the graves nobody chose, once, as the party crawls away.

### Chapter 11: Measured

**Read aloud.**

> It is evening on Tanner Street. The guild van across the road has not finished unloading. By Dee Pruitt's watch you were gone five hours and ten minutes, and she tells you so.
>
> Tobe weighs the bags. Pruitt reads the tags and counts out notes, and then takes some back for the potion.

**What is really happening.** The wand is right. The clerk's form has one box for rank, already filled in from the morning, and no line for a correction, because no clerk has ever needed one. His first thought is a broken wand. His second is license fraud. Across the street, a hunter in gray has stopped unloading to watch.

**Decisions.**
- What to tell the clerk. The truth takes a long time to say and names the dead. "Your wand's broken" works until he fetches a second wand.
- What to tell Pruitt. She wants her gear, and she wants to know why eight people's kit came back with five strangers. If bodies came out, she is the one who has to phone the families, and she has already sent them bills.
- The tagged gear, one last time. Hand it over for a hundred, or keep it.
- The stone. Pruitt pays fifteen for a goblin stone. This one is the size of a fist and warm.
- The guild hunter is crossing the road. A hunter can stay and talk or leave now.

**In the app.** Pay out: an Award of gold per hunter. Then send the Job Change, one phone at a time, at your own pace: the job quest for each hunter's class, already complete, so the reward window opens with the job, its skill and the title "Next of kin: [name]". Raise each hunter's level to the first level of their new rank with the level edit, or over the next sessions if you want it slower.

**Ready when.** Every hunter has a job. End on the clerk writing a letter over the D on his form.

**How to frighten them.** Send the Job Change windows while Pruitt is talking about money. Do not pause the scene for them. The last thing Pruitt says: "I'll need next of kin for the eight. You lot seem to know them."

### Nine new classes

### Refuses everything

A hunter with three or more Refused marks who stood farthest from every bed walks out D. They are the only one whose license matches the wand. The cost of refusing was paid in the room: every time, they had to keep somebody else between themselves and the dying, and the table watched them do it.

Give them the title "Owes nothing" (Discipline +3, Vitality +2), which makes a D-rank about as strong as a D-rank gets. They hold no Owed quest and no habit. Offer them the standard Job Change chain for any of the seven jobs, to be earned in the ordinary way, in their own name. In the campaign they are the one hunter in the party the Association believes, and the only one who can stand in front of a second wand.

If the whole table refuses, they fight the Last One at D with Nell mending behind them. You ran those odds: about one in five. Whoever falls in that fight makes the nearest hunter heavier, and that hunter is given no choice about it.

### Takes everything

A hunter who was nearest to Pim, took Bram's offer, killed Bram for his two, and stood closest to Nell with no tagged thing in anyone else's hands can walk out holding five or six. That hunter reads B, and at least two of their friends walk out D because of it.

Job: Sole Heir. Skills: any two of the base jobs' built-in skills, since they hold the trades. Status: Crowded, put back on the sheet at the start of every session. Title: "Next of kin" with every name on it. Every page is theirs to read, and every Owed quest is theirs to carry. Once a session, two of the dead want different things, and the hunter loses a turn while they settle it. A B-rank who was a D last week is the most interesting person in the city.

### If a player's hunter dies inside

Their awakening passes like anyone's, to the nearest hunter, with a window that names them. Your Revive or a Resurrection Crystal still brings the hunter back. When it does, the awakening goes home: send the heir a window titled "! RETURNED" and take the rank away again. After a table has seen this once, every heir has a reason to hope a dead friend stays dead.

### Five at one table

A table of five might leave like this:

- A Tanker who held the door and carries Osk's shield.
- A Vigil who sat with Pim and then received Lida.
- A Creditor who signed the page, sold the bow and holds Wen.
- A Namesake with Nell's own awakening, who never asked for anything.
- A D-rank who stood at the back every time and owes nobody.

## 6. Walking out a rank higher

In this world it has never happened. People will reach for the nearest word they have, which is "reawakening", a thing the Association investigates a dozen times a year and has never confirmed.

**The clerk.** He files a wand fault. A second wand says the same. By morning there is a form on a supervisor's desk with a letter written over another letter. The Association's first working theory is fraud: someone forged D licenses for C-rank hunters, perhaps to dodge a guild contract. The hunters are invited to a re-measurement. There is no such appointment in the building's system, so it is booked as a first measurement, under their own names, for the second time in their lives.

**The guilds.** The hunter in gray saw the clerk's face. Guilds pay clerks for less. Within two days a mid-sized guild sends a recruiter with a contract, and a large one sends a lawyer with a question. Both want to know how it was done, and neither doubts that it was.

**The one who does not ask how.** There is a guild master in this city, A rank, famous for a reawakening eleven years ago. He was the only survivor of nine in a sealed gate that the papers described as a cave-in. He has never told anyone what happened. When he meets the party he asks one thing, quietly: "How many of you went in?"

**The families.** Emil Brandt has a bill for a shield. Lida's mother has a daughter who made C. Peter Aldous has not spoken to his mother in six years and is about to be handed her notebook. Each is a session, and the heir arrives at the door with the dead person's habit.

**Pruitt.** She is owed gear, and she holds notes on at least some of the party. She has also worked out that five D-ranks went into her gate and something else came out, and that she owns the clearing rights to whatever happened. Play her as a woman with a filing cabinet who would like to be paid.

**Tobe, if he lives.** He is the only witness with no reason to lie, and he is still E. People will buy him drinks. Somebody should stay near him.

**Bram, if he lives.** He is a C-rank with two fewer fingers who knows the rule and the size of room it needs. The frightening version of the campaign is the one where someone with money believes him and builds a small room.

**Bram, if he died.** The Association knows how many licensed hunters went into that gate, and it counts cards. A C-rank foreman with a sword wound is a death someone has to sign for, and a Claimant's license scan says "Took Bram Hask" to any clerk who runs it.

**The stone, the book and the sums.** Each of the three holds the secret and fits in a coat. The sums ask every awakened person in the world a question about the day they woke. If the players check their own dates, decide beforehand what they find.

**A hunter who died alone.** Hadley Roe wrote "none". If a hunter ever dies in a sealed place with nobody of their kind near at all, the rule says what they had has nowhere to go. Goblins in that position leave a stone.

## 7. How to run it

### Dread at a table with phones

- Keep the first two chapters boring. Count shards. Do the pay arithmetic aloud. The players should be a little impatient when Tobe comes back with the bag.
- Phones stay face down until one buzzes. Never send a window to fill a silence.
- Ask "who is nearest?" in the same flat voice whether or not it matters. Write the answer down each time. By chapter 8 they will be answering before you ask, and moving their chairs.
- Watch the real table. If two players are sitting close, have Bram notice it about their hunters.
- Play every person straight. Nell is kind and may have planned all of it. Bram is reasonable, and he may have cut a rope. Tobe would take Pim's half if it fell to him, and he is the most decent person in the story. Give nobody a villain's voice.
- Say a wrong thing once, plainly, and move on. Answer questions about it with the fact and nothing else.
- When asked why the gate sealed, why the goblin knocks, or whether Wen was right, the true answer is that nobody in the room knows. Say that.
- Violence is short and described by its result. Nobody is cut open on the page except goblins, and that is work.

### Using the app to frighten

- A window on one phone. The first NEXT OF KIN goes to one player, and the table watches them read it.
- A minute by a real clock. Five windows, sixty seconds apart, with you silent between them.
- A title that is a person. The hunter's sheet says "Next of kin: Lida Marsh" from then on, and the chronicle keeps the day.
- The map. Distance to a dying body is counted in tiles, in front of everyone. During death saves, tokens move.
- A chip the players can see. Heavier appears on a goblin when its neighbor dies. Later it appears on one of them.
- The board. The gate turns red with no announcement, and changes rank in the middle of a round.
- A quest with no end. "Owed" sits in the quest list for the rest of the campaign, among the dailies.
- The fight log. Six goblins passing their turns while the party decides whether to earn ninety gold.

### Before you run it

This story has deathbeds in it, and it asks players to weigh people by what their deaths are worth. Tell the table that much beforehand, without the rule. Ask each player privately whether a deathbed is something they want at a game night. Put a card on the table that anyone can tap to skip a moment, with no reason given. Nothing sexual happens in this story, and no child appears in it.

### For three players, or six

With three: Goblin 3 and the Shaman. Hadley's things are missing from the Run, and Hadley goes to whoever is nearest. With six: there are not enough dead for everyone, and you should not add any. Say so with Bram's voice in chapter 7. Six hunters and five awakenings is the version I would most like to see run.

## 8. What the app does today, and what is by hand

It does today:

- A gate whose rank and type you edit in play.
- The registry's Goblin, Goblin Shaman and Giant Centipede, and the monster, status, item and skill makers for everything marked new.
- Stacking statuses the players can see.
- A System window to one phone or to chosen phones.
- A status and a title put on a sheet outside a fight.
- A job quest whose reward names a class, a skill and a title.
- The GM's rank edit and level edit.
- The map, with tokens you move, and HP changed by hand.
- The odds of a fight.
- Revive and the Resurrection Crystal.

By hand for now: the tally sheet is paper. "Who is nearest" is you counting tiles. The minute between windows is your watch. Putting Heavier on the nearest goblin after a kill is a tap each time, where a trigger on a foe's defeat would do it for you. The tenth a Creditor owes, the habit, and Crowded returning each session are your memory. The story maker, when it lands, should hold the eleven chapters with their read-aloud text, a ready sign for each on your phone only, and a window that fires to "the hunter nearest this row".
