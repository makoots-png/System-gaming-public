# After launch: the combat and stats rework (the owner's ideas, Nov 13)

Not started. It now comes before the phone test and the launch (see "Order of work" below). The owner raised this in the UI lead's session on Nov 13 while the UI run was finishing. The lead's view, which he did not object to: build it as its own phase after launch, because it changes what a turn is and every monster and item number assumes AC today. This file holds his words and the lead's first answers so the next lead does not start from nothing. It is not a brief. Write one from it, and ask him the open questions at the end first.

## What the owner said

- "I'm thinking about something more dynamic. Where maybe status effects will slow a player, decrease their speed. A character with much higher agility might have turns more often. Maybe a passive will allow a player or monster a chance to get an extra turn."
- "Mana for op strong abilities, and maybe stamina for physical abilities and aura for magical ones. Physical and aura will recover slightly after each fight/team abilities/with potions, mana will only recover with potions/team abilities/after dungeon clear." Later: "We would probably be good with just fatigue."
- "How would you feel about swapping out AC with something else, maybe like armor/MR from League (might scale with beefy stats and come from items/potions) combined with ability to dodge with high enough movement? (Agility and weight combination)?"
- "I want to make it so that some classes will naturally beat others in a rock paper scissors. If someone overindexes on speed they'll do no damage and have too little defense. This is fine in parties if we have someone picking off the weaklings. We do need taunt effects or something of that nature. Weight is good to help with the counterbalance, can't equip too much beefy stuff."
- "There were four additional stats that existed in my other DnD campaign ... instinct, discipline, power, and control." Their descriptions are in the Mechanics tab of the Google Sheet "ToG - Act 3" in his Drive (owned by a friend, shared with him).

- "There should also be abilities which scale with these stats or attack specific enemy stats (lowering them) (or doing more dmg the bigger the diff between the two stats) feel free to push back. Idc if it's big changes so long as the architecture remains ROCK solid. I love presence over charisma."

## What the ToG sheet has (Mechanics tab, read Nov 13)

Stats: Strength (physical prowess), Agility (physical acumen), Vitality (constitution), Instinct (intuition), Discipline (mental fortitude), Charisma (social adeptness), Control (precision and skill at manipulating forces), Power (raw potential and hold on forces). Derived: Health, Stamina, Aura, Mana, a regen for each of the first three, Physique and Mystique (physical and magical damage), Armor and Resist, flat and percent penetration of each, Movement, Tenacity (the floor of Movement; every 5 adds 1 to saves), Crit Chance, Vamp. Conditions: standard and stacking, positive and negative (Slow and Fast move the initiative roll per stack; Shackle halves initiative speed; Animate doubles it). Actions: Dash, Disengage, Parry, Transfer, React.

## The lead's first answers

- **Turns by speed.** One mechanism: each combatant fills a gauge by its speed and acts when it is full. Slow and Haste change the rate. An extra-turn passive is a chance to refill part of the gauge. Speed needs a cap or diminishing returns, or Agility is the best stat.
- **Stats.** Eight, not ten: Strength, Agility, Vitality, Instinct (takes Perception's place), Discipline (new), Presence, and Power and Control in place of Intelligence. Each feeds at least two numbers: Strength physical damage and carried weight; Agility speed and dodge, less the weight worn; Vitality HP, armor scaling and fatigue capacity; Instinct crit and reacting first; Discipline resist and tenacity against slows, stuns and taunts; Presence taunt strength, team abilities and shadows; Power magic damage and the mana pool; Control accuracy, how well statuses land and lower costs.
- **Defence.** Armor and Resist reduce damage in place of AC, so every damage source needs a type (one field on an op). Dodge is small and separate, from Agility less weight.
- **Rock paper scissors.** Speed beats the cannon (acts first, dodges); the tank beats speed (it cannot get through armor); the cannon beats the tank (penetration).
- **Taunt.** A status: "must target whoever applied it", enforced by the target picker, shortened by Discipline.
- **Resources.** HP, fatigue and mana, the three bars of today. Fatigue pays for physical and aura abilities and comes back after each fight; mana is scarce (potions, team abilities, a dungeon clear).

- **Abilities and stats (his second message).** Three typed options on an op, each read by the op's parser, each with a maker row and a preview line; no formula a GM types. (1) Scale: an optional stat and an amount per point ("2d6 + Power"). (2) Lower a stat: a status op, the shape of today's "AC -2", under the stronger-wins rule of phase 4. (3) Gap damage: a bonus per point the user's stat is over the target's, with a cap the op must carry ("+10% per point of Power over their Discipline, up to +100%"); uncapped it is nothing between equals and a one-shot against a mismatch, and stat-lowering widens the gap. The phase 4 simulation gains the three archetypes. Presence keeps its name.
- **Formulas: the owner overruled the lead (his third message).** "Keep the GM typing. Give me a recommendation for a formula based off things I tick, but give me the ability to overwrite it. If I want to break the game balance I can do so." So: the maker builds a recommended formula from ticked options (scales with a stat, targets Armor or Resist, grows with a stat gap, a cap) and shows it as text; the GM may type over it, with an "edited" mark and a Reset. A formula is data with one parser in core: numbers, dice, the user's and the target's stats, + - * /, min and max. The parser refuses only what it cannot read (an unknown name), with a message in the maker; it never refuses a formula for being strong. The preview rolls it at three ranks against a sample target. Balance tests cover built-in content only. This replaces "no formula a GM types" above and the customization phase's "no formulas".

- **Channeling and exponential scalers (his fourth message).** "Ok also add exponential scalers. I don't mind players especially breaking the game a little, it can be fun. But like have those be on maybe abilities which scale the longer they're concentrated on ... if a heavy power character can focus on an ability for 5x turns his next one should probably oneshot someone lol." So: a channel is a state on the combatant (a status with a count that grows each of its turns, shown on the card and on every phone); the formula language gains `charge` and a power operator (`2d8 * 2^charge`); the maker has a tick "grows while channeled", linear or exponential. A stun, a taunt or a hit over a threshold breaks it, and Discipline holds it. Monsters can channel, which gives a boss a countdown. Settled by the owner ("Maybe it just fires early with a penalty."): a broken channel fires at once with the charge it has, less a penalty; half the charge is the starting number, a rule number he can change.

- **Ideas from other games (his sixth message).** The lead offered six; the owner: "Statuses that react with each other will get complicated quickly. I like every other idea and we should implement them directly. We just need to flesh that out especially with custom statuses." In: a heavy ability delays the user's next turn on the timeline (Final Fantasy X); a monster shows its intent for its next action (Slay the Spire); a stagger bar on bosses that weak hits fill, costing the boss turns when full; shields as a layer of temporary HP that expires; a threat table that suggests each monster's target, with taunt as a burst of threat and the GM free to overrule. Out: statuses that react with each other.
- **Custom statuses in the rework.** Every new mechanic is an op the status maker offers, so a custom status can do what a built-in does: change speed, delay the next turn, taunt or add threat, break or protect a channel, give a shield, change how fast a stagger bar fills, lower or raise a stat. Each by rank and with the typed formula. To propose to the owner on one page each before building: who resists what (the lead's start: Discipline shortens control effects, bosses have a flat resistance); whether speed changes stack (start: the strongest slow and the strongest haste apply, as in phase 4); what intent shows for a monster's custom skill (start: name and target, numbers hidden unless the GM reveals them).
- **Order of work (the lead's proposal, same message; the owner did not object).** Finish the UI run, verify and open the PR as a mergeable checkpoint, then this rework, then the phone test once on the final product, then launch. The phone test would otherwise walk the sheet and the fight twice.

- **First principles, avoidance, AoE and a map (his message before a flight, Nov 13).** "I want this grounded in first principles. Things that shouldn't be dodgeable shouldn't be dodgeable. Also, like maybe with AOE's have it scale with how far away you are for some, not for others (this should be easy to change for the gm). Ideally also we build an interactive, highly customizable battle map as well. That can come after we do our big commits." So: every rule has a written reason before a number (dodge exists because a fast body moves out of the way, so only what a body can move out of is dodged). An op or skill carries how it is avoided, one of dodge (aimed attacks), resist (acts on mind or body directly; Discipline answers it) or nothing (an effect already on the target, an aura, anything the GM marks); the default follows the ability's type and the maker lets the GM change it. An area ability has a tick "falls off with distance" and an editable amount; with no map the target picker asks close or far per target. The battle map is a later phase, after the rework is committed, and optional: a table without it loses nothing. The rework stores range and area on abilities so the map has something to read.
- **Escaping an area (his next message).** "With AOE you should be able to dodge so you can get out of the radius, or at least minimize damage by reaching an outer layer quickly." A fourth kind of avoidance, escape: an area has a centre, a radius and an optional outer ring; the target's movement (Agility less weight) against the radius gives out (no damage), the ring (a share of the damage) or caught (all of it); a slowed or rooted target does not get out. Per ability the GM edits the radius, the ring's share and a "no escape" tick. With no map it is worked out from speed and radius; with the map, from where they stand.

- **The governing rule for avoidance (his next message).** "First principles, based in reality for every ability type and how their mechanics work. I understand there will be a certain level of bullshit with magical abilities and such but I mean just wherever the target's speed can put them essentially just is where the dmg should be calced from." One rule in place of dodge, escape and falloff as three: every ability has a time to land (its wind-up plus its travel over the distance); in that time the target moves as far as its speed allows (less when slowed, rooted or heavy); damage is worked out from where it ends up against the ability's shape (a touch, a line, a radius with an optional ring). So a sword swing is not outrun, an arrow is at range, a blast is escaped or half escaped, a slow slam is easy to leave. An effect with no travel (a curse, a mind effect) is resisted by Discipline instead. Per ability the GM edits wind-up, travel speed and shape; the default follows the ability's type. It needs distance: with no map the target picker asks close, mid or far; the map reads it. This replaces the four kinds of avoidance listed above as separate rules: they are outcomes of this one.

- **The map moves into the rework (his next message).** "Yes we should optimize for the battle map I think so we don't do it twice." So positions are in the fight's data from the start (each combatant has a place; distance, range, radius and movement are computed from it), and there is no close, mid or far stopgap. The first map is plain: a grid, tokens, tap to move, an ability's reach or radius drawn when picked, the same on every phone in the room. Customization (backgrounds, walls and cover, terrain that slows, saved maps per gate) is added on the same data later. A fight without the map still runs: everyone stands at set distances, a default on the same data and not a second system. Order inside the rework: (1) the paper design and the simulation, positions included; (2) the engine: timeline, positions, the avoidance rule; (3) stats, armor and resist, resources; (4) the plain map and the fight screen around it; (5) channeling, taunt and threat, stagger, shields, intent; (6) map customization. The lead told him this is the largest phase so far, several sessions, and that launch waits for it unless he merges the checkpoint PR first.

- **While the owner flies (Nov 13).** The lead told him the defaults it will use, since it cannot ask: a speed gauge, dodge in and small, a new campaign (old saves not carried), and the first milestone is the design on paper and the balance simulation with no screen changes until the numbers hold. He did not object before boarding.

- **His bar, restated for the map (his next message).** "With the map everything will get complicated so we need to make sure everything is defined, easy to find for you, and we are good with using our pstack skills." What the lead committed to: each new idea is one named type with one parser (a position, a shape, terrain, a formula, a channel); the fight stays one pure function, movement, timing and avoidance are commands and rules inside it, and the map view only draws and sends taps; every machine has a chart a test keeps current; `AGENTS.md` gains a "where it lives" row and a recipe per new kind ("a shape", "a terrain type"); lint tests hold the boundaries (only the fight changes a position, no view computes damage); design on paper first, two or three variants for the timeline and the map model, types and tables before logic, one unit at a time ending in a check, the simulation on the real reducer, every number with its reason. The first milestone is the design and the simulation only, with no screen changes.
- **His bar for the phase.** Big changes are fine "so long as the architecture remains ROCK solid": every new thing is a field with a parser line, a table entry or a command with a test, never a special case in a view.

## What to balance, and how (given to the owner Nov 13; he asked that the principle skills be used wherever they apply)

Levers and the risk of each: speed to turn rate (Agility the only stat); turn delay per ability (heavy hits free or never worth it); Armor and Resist against penetration (tanks unkillable or armor pointless); dodge from Agility less weight (fast builds win twice); stat scaling per point (one stat outgrows the rest by S); gap damage and its cap (one-shots between normal builds); channel growth and interrupt (never completes or always does); stagger bar size (a boss stunned for good, or a bar that never fills); shields (stacked shields replace HP); threat and taunt (the tank holds everything, or nothing); fatigue back per fight and mana scarcity (casters dry by fight two, or mana without end); control effects against Discipline (stun-lock, or Control useless).

Method, by principle: bounds written before a run, each number with its reason (explain-the-number; `p4/design.md` is the model); one simulation of the three archetypes against each other and against monsters at E, C and S, run on the real reducer (build-the-lever, prove-it-works); the rock paper scissors as a table the test reads, who beats whom and by how much, so a class adds a row (model-the-domain); one lever at a time, speed, then defence, then resources, then the extras, the simulation green before the next (sequence-verifiable-units); two or three timeline variants compared on paper numbers before building (exhaust-the-design-space); a bound on fight length, 4 to 8 rounds for a normal fight as a start (experience-first); a typed formula checked once in the maker and trusted after, balance tests on built-ins only (boundary-discipline). The first bound offered to the owner: a pure speed build loses to a tank one on one in 60 to 75 per cent of fights. What tests cannot show is how it feels at the table: a session or two of play, with every rule number overridable.

## Where it lands in the code

- The fight: `src/core/combat/encounter.ts` and `encounter-types.ts` (`Order`, `turnCmp`, the `turnStarted` rule). New moves are members of `Command`. `tests/encounter-model.test.ts` plays 2,500 random fights and will hold the new rules to "a status never ticks twice".
- Stats: `STAT_KEYS` is declared twice (`src/core/rules.ts:11` and `src/core/save.ts:51`). Merge them first. 16 files name a stat.
- AC: `Perk` and `PERK_KEYS` in `rules.ts`, `HunterView.ac` in `encounter-types.ts`, monsters' stat blocks (`statBlock`), the attack roll in `outcomesOf` (`src/core/combat/use.ts`).
- Resources: `Vitals` in `src/core/hunter/vitals.ts`, the rest rule in `rules.ts` (`rest`).
- Balance: `tests/` holds the phase 4 simulation (a status build against a damage build at three tiers); the rework needs the same for the three archetypes.

## The map (the owner, Nov 13, in the rework lead's session, while the wiring wave ran)

- His words: "Gimme map ideas. How is the best way to do this? Hexes? Squares? No grid at all? I envision a radius showing movement, a radius showing ability splash/damage, if there are lasting fields they show on the map, maybe an ability to do combat from within the map option (hotbar for abilities/consumables) push back on stupid shit."
- The lead's answer, to build by unless he says otherwise:
  - **Squares in the rules, no grid lines on the screen.** The fight's places stay whole 5 foot tiles (`space.ts`, already tested by 2,500 random fights), and the map draws movement and splash as shaded areas with soft edges and snaps a token to a tile's centre. Grid lines are a toggle, off by default. Reasons: a tile is a tap target a thumb can hit, a free point is not; rooms and walls are rectangles, which hexes cut badly; with true free placement a pixel decides whether a blast catches someone, and core would need collision and path-finding in continuous space for no gain a player sees.
  - **Hexes: no.** Their one gain is even distance, and the 5-10-5 rule already makes a radius near round. They cost corridors and rectangular rooms.
  - **Movement shown as two shades**: where this action can take the token (a stride), and lighter, where a dash can. Picking an ability shows its reach, and aiming shows the splash and its outer ring before the tap is confirmed.
  - **Fighting from the map: yes, as a second view of the same fight, not a second way to run it.** A hotbar of the default attack, a few pinned abilities and a drawer for the rest and for consumables. Tap an ability, see reach and splash, tap a target or tile, confirm. The confirm stays because a use spends the turn and there is no undo. The list view and the map send the same commands. On a 360 px phone the map is its own mode of the fight screen with the turn order as one line above it; cards open on a tap.
  - **Lasting fields: yes, and they are a new typed thing, a zone**: a shape on the ground with an owner, an end time and ops (burning ground, fog, mud that slows). It applies its ops to whoever stands in it at their turn start, pro rata by time like any per-round op, and on entry. Terrain is a zone with no end. Zones do not react with each other, as with statuses.
  - **Pushed back on, not built unless he asks**: fog of war and per-player line of sight (hidden information in a shared room doc is a large cost for a table that sits together); elevation; free drawing tools; animation beyond a token sliding and a splash flashing.
  - Needed that he did not name: pinch zoom and pan on a phone; a GM drag that moves any token free of time (`place` exists); the map remembering its layout per gate (milestone 6).
- **Fog of war is in, shared by the party (his next message).** "Fog of war I don't mind, especially behind terrain. However all players should share the same view." So: one view for the whole party, the union of what any hunter who is up can see; a wall or blocking terrain hides what is behind it; the GM sees everything, with what the party cannot see drawn dimmed. The GM's phone works it out and leaves hidden monsters and zones out of the room doc, so no player phone holds what the party cannot see. It needs `blocksLine` (already a column of `TERRAIN` in `space.ts`) and one pure function `seenBy(side, bodies, grid)` in core. It lands with walls and terrain in milestone 6, and the per-player view the lead pushed back on stays out.

## Fields that move, spread, and the map's size (the owner, Nov 14)

His words: "The fields are autoplaced if someone puts down an AOE with a lasting effect right? What about if you give someone a radiating like "sickness/spreading effect" how about like a player aura ability where it heals or does dmg surrounding them and they move? Also can we make it adaptable to make there be more or less tiles"

The lead's reading, to build as one unit after the last unit lands (it touches zones.ts and the map):
- Auto-placed: yes already (`Ability.zone`, rule 18b).
- An aura: a zone anchored to a body (a radius around its owner), its tiles worked out again by `zoneSync` whenever the owner moves; it ends with its owner. Made in the skill maker as "an aura around the user" next to "a field where it lands"; a status can carry one too (a GM gives "Radiating sickness" to a monster).
- A field that spreads: a zone that grows by a number of tiles of radius each round up to a cap.
- A sickness that spreads between bodies: a status op `spread` (each round, bodies within N tiles resist or catch the same status at one tier lower duration; never back to one who has had it this fight, so it ends).
- More or fewer tiles: a saved map is 4 to 40 a side today (the maker's Width and Height). Add: the same Width and Height in the fight's Fight tools for a fight under way (growing adds floor at the right and bottom; shrinking refuses to cut off a body), and the open arena's default size as a plain setting.

## Dice typed the Roll20 way (the owner, Nov 14)

"Ok for dice rolling we should copy roll20 formulas. They/I can type a custom formula like /r d20+4 (roll a d20 add 4 to the result) or /r 2d20kh1 or /r 10d20kh4 (roll 10d20 keep the highest 4)"

The lead's reading: the one formula parser (`src/core/formula.ts`) learns Roll20's dice notation, so the same text works in the Dice view's typed field, in a check, and in an ability's or status's formula. In: `NdS` with N optional, keep and drop (`kh`, `kl`, `k` as keep highest, `dh`, `dl`, `d` as drop lowest), exploding (`!`), reroll (`r<n`, `ro<n`), counting successes (`>n`, `<n`), `d%`, parentheses and `+ - * /`, `floor`/`ceil`/`round`, an optional leading `/r` or `/roll`. The Dice view shows each die rolled with the dropped ones struck through and the total. Advantage (kits design 8.2) is `2d20kh1` and disadvantage `2d20kl1`: one rule, not two. Bounds: at most 100 dice and 1000 sides a term, an exploding die at most 10 times. To build after kits build 1 returns (it is changing `formula.ts`).
