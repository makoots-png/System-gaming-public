## What the lead has decided (build on these; overturn one only with a written reason)

The four kits and Glas reduce to a few blocks. Do not add one block per ability; find the smallest set that makes every listed kit expressible, and show the mapping.

1. **A stack count on a status.** Today a body holds one instance per status key. A status def may be `stacking`: the instance holds `stacks` (1 to a cap), each application adds, its ops scale per stack, and it says how stacks leave (never in the fight, one per round, all when its time ends, all on a named event such as the bearer's crit). This is his "Stacking conditions" table, Glas's Resonance, Rot and Vision, Maro's Corruption, Nikolas's counters, and (cap 1) Hwei's mark. A counter on self that is a resource (Hwei's Baang, Pain, Haste) is the same thing held by its owner.
2. **A threshold on a stacking status.** At N stacks (a number, or a formula over the bearer such as its Vitality) something happens once: an op list lands (damage, another status, an execute), and the stacks are spent or kept. An execute is bounded by rule (never on a boss above a share of HP; say the bound and why).
3. **A trigger: when an event, then ops.** Events are a closed union the reducer already knows or can name: a hit dealt, damage taken, a crit, a kill, an avoid (his Parry), a turn's start and end, an entry into a zone, a status applied or ended, an ability of a tag used. A trigger lives on a passive skill, an item, a status or a zone, has a limit (each time, once a round, n a fight) and lands ops on self, the other party or an area. Bound the chain: a trigger's ops raise no further triggers past a depth, and say why that depth.
4. **Costs and gates on an ability.** A cost in HP (flat or a share of max; never the killing blow), in stacks of a status on self or on the target (spend n, or all with the payoff per stack). A gate: needs a status on self (a mode) or on the target, at least n stacks, uses left this fight or day.
5. **New inputs to a formula.** Stacks of a named status on the target, on the user, across the field; missing HP share of user and target; enemies hit by this use; kills this fight; rounds the user has kept a status. Each bounded, with what the parser refuses.
6. **A delayed effect.** Ops that land when a status ends or after n rounds (Hemoplague, rends): a status whose def has `onEnd` ops, with what a cleanse does to it.
7. **A mode** is a status on self with an entry cost and a gate others read: it should need nothing new after 1, 3 and 4; show it (Crimson Pact, a stance, Hwei's mood from the last ability's tag).
8. **Zones**: what the kits add to what exists (auras, growth): an upkeep cost per round, a cap on how many one owner holds, ending one early for a payoff.
9. **A timed summon** (a ghost for 2 rounds, a sprite): how it joins the fight as a row, whose turn it takes, when it leaves; reuse the shadows' row.
10. **Not now** (say so in the file and why each is left): a rewind to an earlier state, a retried action, permanent stats from kills (offer it as a GM reward line instead), collectable sets, perk trees with prerequisites, a trigger on the order of abilities in a round.

## Steps

1. **The blocks, exactly.** For each of 1 to 9: the rule in numbered sentences, its TypeScript type sketch as a union the compiler checks (wrong states unwritable: a non-stacking status has no `stacks`; a threshold exists only on a stacking def), where it lives in the modules AGENTS.md names, what the reducer does and in what order inside rule numbers of design 3.1 (give new rule numbers), what a parser bounds (caps in one table), what the room doc carries for a player's phone and its size against `DOC_MAX`. Three or more variants weighed where the choice is real (where stacks live; how a trigger is ordered against the hit that raised it; snapshot or live reading of stacks in a formula), one chosen with its reason. Commit.
2. **The state trees.** A table and a chart sketch for each thing with states: a stacking status's life (absent, held at n, at threshold, spent, ended), a delayed effect (armed, landed, cleansed), a limited use (uses left, spent, restored at fight end or day), a timed summon, a trigger's limit, a zone with upkeep. States, events, each cell. These become `TABLE`s. Commit.
3. **The budget.** Balance on paper before any build: what one stack, one trigger firing, a threshold payoff, an HP cost, a delayed hit and a mode are worth in the same unit design section 6 uses for a single hit and an ability kind's recommended formula (`recommended`, `averages`), so the maker can show a made ability's or status's worth against its kind's bound and warn (not refuse) past it. Give the expected damage over a 4 to 8 round fight of three example kits built from the blocks (a stacker like Glas with execute, a mark-and-detonate caster like Hwei, a counter-spending bruiser) at E, C and S against the existing speed, tank and cannon builds, by closed form in the script, inside section 6's bounds or with the lever that brings them in. Name the degenerate loops (doubling stacks twice, a trigger that feeds itself, execute on bosses, HP cost with full heal) and the rule that stops each. The rows the simulation must gain. Commit.
4. **The mapping.** A table: every ability of Glas, Nikolas, Einar, Maro and Hwei in the summaries, the blocks that make it, or "not now" with the reason, or "undefined in the sheet". Count how many are makeable. If a block is used by fewer than two abilities, argue for cutting it. Commit.
5. **The makers.** What the status maker, the skill maker's ability form, the item maker and the monster maker each gain, as fields and rows by the maker recipes (a GM on a 390 px phone; a collapsed group per block; sentences from `describeStatus`/`describeAbility` that read as plain rules), with three built-in examples a GM can open and copy. Then the build order as units for helpers: each one module or one wiring step with its test file, what runs in parallel, the AGENTS.md rows and recipes. A one-page summary of every decision at the top, and a section for the owner in plain words: what he can make now, what he cannot yet, each default he might want otherwise and what to say to change it. Commit.
