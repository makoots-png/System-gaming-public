## Steps

1. **The grammar.** In `formula.ts`: a dice term `NdS` (N optional, `d%` is d100) with modifiers in Roll20's order and meaning: keep and drop (`kh n`, `kl n`, `k n` as keep highest, `dh n`, `dl n`, `d n` as drop lowest), exploding (`!`, `!>n`, `!<n`), reroll (`r n`, `r<n`, `r>n`, and `ro` once), successes (`>n`, `<n`, `=n` counting dice, after keeps), plus `floor`, `ceil`, `round`, `abs` beside the existing `min`, `max`; an optional leading `/r` or `/roll` and surrounding spaces. Check Roll20's own dice reference for the exact semantics you are unsure of (the order of modifiers, what `d` alone drops, how `!` and `r` interact) and write each in the file's header; where you cannot check, choose the plainest reading and say so in the notes. The existing formula language (stat names, `charge`, the kits' names) keeps working unchanged: every existing test passes untouched. The parser refuses only what it cannot read, with a message a person can act on ("kh needs a number: 2d20kh1"). Bounds as named constants with reasons: at most 100 dice and 1000 sides a term, an exploding or rerolling die at most 10 times, a formula at most its present length cap.
2. **Rolling stays outside core.** Core says what dice a formula needs (the existing "needs" shape, extended: a term may need more dice than N because of explosions and rerolls, so the evaluation takes a source of dice numbers from the edge the way the fight's rolls do today; choose between "edge supplies a bounded stream" and "evaluate reports it needs k more", weigh both in the notes, keep core pure and deterministic given the numbers). `evaluate` returns the total and a typed breakdown per term: each die's faces with its fate (kept, dropped, rerolled, exploded, a success), so a view can strike through dropped dice. `averages` gives the true expected value for each modifier (closed form where there is one, exact enumeration under a bound otherwise; say which) because the makers' budget reads it.
3. **Advantage is this.** Show in a test that `2d20kh1` and `2d20kl1` are the edge and its opposite of design 8.2, and leave a line in the notes for the kits wave 2 unit 22 to use the same term rather than a second rule.
4. **Tests.** `tests/formula.test.ts`: a table of formulas with fixed dice and the expected total and breakdown, the owner's three examples first; every refusal; the bounds; a property test (random formulas from the grammar, random dice: the total equals the breakdown's sum; kept count is right; never throws). `tests/roll.test.ts`: the edge's roller feeds it.
