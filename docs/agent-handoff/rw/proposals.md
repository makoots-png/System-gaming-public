# Three things to decide, for the owner

Each is at most a phone page. The design already does the thing under "What it does now", so nothing is waiting on an answer. Say the word under "To change it" and one constant or one rule moves.

## 1. Who resists what

**What it does now.** There are three ways an ability can fail to land fully, and which one applies comes from how the ability reaches you, not from a tick on the ability.

- Something travels at you (a swing, an arrow, a blast). Your **Agility** answers it. You roll one d20, and that roll says how much of the ability's flight time you use to move. Where you end up against its shape decides whether you take all of it, the outer ring's share, or nothing. A swing is too fast to leave; a slow blast you walk out of.
- Nothing travels (a curse, a mind effect, a drain). Your **Discipline** answers it. You roll one d20 and resist on 15 or more after your edge. A resisted curse still deals half, and applies no status.
- The ability is marked sure (an aura, something already on you, anything the GM marks). Nothing answers it.

Control effects (a stun, a root, a slow, a taunt) always land when the ability lands, and your **Discipline** shortens them: at most 37% shorter for the most disciplined build. An elite takes them at three quarters and a boss at half.

**To change it.** "Bosses should be immune to stuns" (a flat immunity instead of half), or "a resisted curse should do nothing" (0 instead of half), or "Instinct should answer curses too" (one stat for both instead of Agility and Discipline).

## 2. Whether speed changes stack

**What it does now.** Only the strongest slow and the strongest haste on a combatant apply, and they multiply: a 30% slow and a 20% haste give 0.7 times 1.2, which is 0.84 of the speed. A second, weaker slow adds nothing while the stronger one is up. This is the same rule as statuses today: one instance per key, the stronger source wins.

Speed is clamped to 40 to 250 whatever happens, so the slowest body still acts once every two and a half rounds and the fastest acts two and a half times a round.

**Why.** Two stacking slows from one caster would take a body to a crawl and end its turns, which is the one thing that stops a player playing. The clamp is the floor under that.

**To change it.** "Let them stack" (every slow multiplies, the clamp still holds), or "let the clamp go to 30" (a slowed body acts once every three rounds), or "slows should stack but only from different casters".

## 3. What a monster's intent shows

**What it does now.** On its turn the GM may set a monster's next action. Every phone then shows the monster's card with the ability's **name** and **who it is aimed at**: "Crushing Slam, at Brakk". The numbers (damage, radius, how it is avoided) stay hidden until the GM taps Reveal, which shows them on every phone for that one intent. A monster that is channeling shows its channel and its charge, because the table can see it winding up.

**Why.** Knowing that the slam is coming and at whom is what makes the next turn a decision: step out of the radius, shield the target, taunt it off. Knowing the damage turns the turn into arithmetic, and takes away the GM's room to improvise.

**To change it.** "Always show the numbers" (reveal by default, with a hide button), or "show only that it is attacking, not whom" (less to draw, less to plan around), or "let the monster's maker carry its own choice" (a field per monster, so a boss can be the one that hides its intent).
