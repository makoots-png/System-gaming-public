## Why (the owner's goal)

the owner is the GM of a tabletop game played on phones. He wants a fight that is "more dynamic": turns from speed, eight stats, Armor and Resist in place of AC, damage worked out from "wherever the target's speed can put them", formulas he can type over, channeling, taunt and threat, stagger, shields, intent, and a battle map with positions in the data from the start. "I want this grounded in first principles." "Idc if it's big changes so long as the architecture remains ROCK solid." Every rule gets a written reason before a number. The next helper builds the rules as pure core code and a simulation from your file without asking anything, so the design must leave no rule undefined.

## What the lead has decided (do not reopen; build on these)

1. **All fight numbers move into core.** Today every roll and derived number is in a view (inventory 6.2). After the rework a view draws and sends taps. New pure modules, named here so the design uses the names: `src/core/hunter/derive.ts` (stats, gear, perks and statuses to one `Sheet` of derived numbers, for hunters, monsters and shadows alike), `src/core/combat/timeline.ts` (who acts next), `src/core/combat/space.ts` (positions, distance, shapes, movement), `src/core/combat/resolve.ts` (an ability, the user's and target's `Sheet`, their places and the dice numbers in, typed outcomes out), `src/core/formula.ts` (the one formula parser and evaluator). The reducer still never rolls: a command carries the dice numbers the edge rolled, and core works out everything from them.
2. **Every combatant row carries the same things**: a `side`, a `Sheet` snapshot, a place, its timeline value, its statuses. One home for a combatant's fight state, for every kind (this ends the two homes of inventory 6.3; say how a local hunter's HP still reaches its sheet through an `Effect`).
3. **Eight stats** with keys `str agi vit ins dis pre pow ctl` (Strength, Agility, Vitality, Instinct, Discipline, Presence, Power, Control). One `STAT_KEYS`. Each feeds at least two numbers, as in the notes. Every stat-to-number curve has diminishing returns or a cap, and the design says which and why (the inventory shows the linear `mod` breaks the d20 at rank C).
4. **Three bars**: HP, fatigue, mana. Fatigue pays physical and aura abilities and returns after a fight. Mana is scarce.
5. **Damage has a type** (physical or magical; say whether "true" exists and why) and Armor or Resist reduce it. Start from `damage * K / (K + armor)` because each point of armor then adds the same effective HP and immunity is never reached; choose K and say why. Penetration is percent then flat.
6. **The avoidance rule is one rule**: time to land (wind-up plus distance over travel speed), the target moves as far as its speed allows in that time, damage is read from where it ends against the shape (touch, line, radius with an optional ring). No travel means Discipline resists it. Positions are real from the start; a fight without the map uses default places on the same data.
7. **A new campaign.** The save goes to `schema: 4`; an older save is not carried into it and is kept in the backups with a plain message. Old rooms need not work.
8. **Formulas** are data with one parser: numbers, dice, the user's and target's stats and derived numbers, `+ - * / ^`, `min`, `max`, `charge`. The parser refuses only what it cannot read.
9. **Out of scope**: statuses that react with each other. No new dependency. No screen design here beyond what data a screen needs.

## Steps

1. **The timeline: three variants on paper.** In `design.md` section 1 compare (a) a gauge that fills by speed each tick, (b) a next-action time per combatant (act, then `at += cost / speed`), (c) initiative re-rolled each round with speed as a bonus. For each: the data on a row, how a slow, a haste, a delay from a heavy ability, an extra-turn passive, a stun and a combatant joining mid-fight work, what "a round" becomes for status durations, cooldowns, the log and the player's turn cue, what the players' phones need to draw the order ahead, determinism and replay. `scripts/rw-paper.ts` prints, for each variant, turns taken in a fixed span by speeds 80, 100, 130 and 200 and the turn sequence of a sample five-combatant fight. Recommend one. The lead leans to (b) because it is a pure function of the rows, shows the order ahead and makes delay a plain addition; overturn that only with numbers. Define time units, the tie rule, and how durations are counted (in time, not in the bearer's turns, unless you show why not). Commit.
2. **Stats and the `Sheet`.** Section 2: the full list of derived numbers (max HP, max mana, fatigue capacity, speed, movement, carried weight and its effect, armor, resist, penetration, physical and magical power, accuracy, crit chance and size, tenacity, threat, and whatever the rules below need), each with its formula, its reason in one sentence, and its value for five builds at E, C and S printed by the script: the speed build, the tank, the cannon (Power), a physical bruiser, a support (Presence and Discipline). Monsters: how a rank gives a monster its `Sheet` (replace the `rankTable` columns), normal, elite and boss. Gear: what weight is, which slots carry it, how it lowers speed and dodge, with numbers for light, medium and heavy kits. Commit.
3. **One use, end to end.** Section 3: the exact order of resolution for one ability use, as numbered rules a programmer can type: cost paid, range and shape checked from places, time to land, the target's movement and where it ends (say exactly what is rolled and by whom: the lead wants one reaction roll so the table still rolls dice, Instinct against the user's Control, deciding how much of the land time the target uses; propose the roll and show its spread), caught, ring or out, crit, the formula evaluated, Armor or Resist and penetration, shields, HP, threat added, stagger filled, a channel broken, statuses applied and resisted (tenacity shortens; a boss's flat resistance). 4. **The extras, each as a small machine or table.** Section 4: channel (states, growth linear or exponential, what breaks it, firing early at half charge), taunt and threat (the threat table, what a monster's suggested target is, how taunt overrides, how Discipline shortens), stagger (bar size by rank, fill per hit, what a full bar costs the boss in time), shields (a layer with an expiry, how two shields combine: the lead's start is the larger one holds, not a sum, because stacked shields would replace HP), intent (what is stored for a monster's next action and what players see), movement as an action (how far, what it costs on the timeline, zones a slow or root changes). 5. **The map's data.** Section 5: the grid (square tiles; say the tile size in game terms and why), a place as a typed pair, distance (say which metric and why), default places for a fight with no map (two lines facing each other at a set distance, on the same data), what the first plain map must store (size, tokens) and what later customization adds on the same data (walls, cover, slowing terrain, saved maps per gate) so that nothing is redone. 6. **Balance bounds, written before any run.** Section 6: the table the simulation will be held to. ## Rules

- Every number has its reason next to it and comes out of `scripts/rw-paper.ts`, which runs with `bun scripts/rw-paper.ts` in a few seconds, imports nothing from `src/views` or `src/edge`, and prints each table under the design's section number. - Type sketches are TypeScript and follow `principle-type-system-discipline` (unions the compiler checks, a place and a time as their own types).
- Plain whole sentences (unslop). No long dashes. Keep `design.md` under about 700 lines.
- Notes file as you go: what you were unsure of.
