## The lead's design decisions

1. **Terrain is a table.** `TERRAIN` in `space.ts` gains the kinds a GM needs and no more: floor, wall (no standing, stops sight and lines), cover (standing allowed, stops no movement, an ability that travels through it deals a share less to who stands in it; the share is a rule number with its reason), rough ground (each tile costs two to enter), and a pit or water that cannot be stood in but does not stop sight. Each is one row: its character, whether a body may stand, its cost to enter, whether it stops a line, whether it gives cover. Movement, lines and sight read the table; nothing branches on a terrain's name.
2. **A lasting field is a zone**: a new typed thing on the fight, `Zone { id, shape placed on the grid, owner (a combatant or the GM), side it spares or none, until: Tick | null, ops }`. Its ops are the status ops that already exist (damage each round by type, speed, root, a stat down, heal and the rest), applied to whoever stands in it. Rules, each with its reason written in `design.md` section 4 (a new 4.7): a body in a zone at the start of its turn is paid the zone's per-round ops pro rata for the time it spent inside since it last paid, exactly as a status settles, and holds the zone's standing ops (speed, root, stat) only while inside; entering a zone pays its on-entry part once per entry; a zone ends at `until`, or when its owner goes down if the ability says so; zones do not react with each other, and two zones on one tile each apply (the stronger-wins rule applies per op kind, as with statuses, so two slows do not stack). An ability makes a zone through a new op an ability may carry (`zone { shape from the ability's own, duration, ops, endsWithOwner }`); the GM can also paint one by hand. Terrain that hurts (lava) is a zone with no end and no owner, not a sixth terrain kind, so there is one rule for "the ground does something".
3. **The party shares one view.** One pure function in core, `seenBy(side, bodies, grid)`: the set of tiles any combatant of that side who is up can see, by straight lines that a wall stops, out to a sight range that is a rule number (start generous: the whole default arena; a GM can lower it for a dark gate). The GM sees everything, with what the party cannot see drawn dimmed. The GM's phone builds the room's fight doc from the party's view: a foe, a zone or an intent on a tile the party cannot see is not in the doc at all, so no player's phone ever holds it; what the party has seen before but cannot see now is remembered as terrain only (walls stay drawn, creatures do not). A monster that steps into view appears; one that steps out is gone from the doc. Fog can be turned off per map (an open field needs none).
4. **A map is a saved thing.** `BattleMap { id, name, w, h, tiles, zones (the permanent ones), starts for each side, sight, fog on or off }` in the GM's library with one parser (`parseBattleMap`), a cap, a default (the open arena of today), and its line in the pack so it can be shared like other custom content. A gate may name a map; a fight started from that gate uses it, and a fight with no map named uses the open arena, on the same code path. The map editor is a mode of the map view for the GM: pick a brush (a terrain kind, a zone from the status ops, a start tile for a side, an eraser), drag to paint, with the size set by steppers; Save, Save as, Rename, Delete in the Workshop's new "Maps" section through the maker shell. During a fight the GM may also paint (a wall falls, a fire spreads) as fight commands, so it reaches every phone.
5. **What is not built**: background images, elevation, doors as a kind (a GM erases a wall), light sources, per-player sight. Leave nothing half-made for them.

## Rules

- One rule for each thing: terrain is the table, a field is a zone, sight is `seenBy`. No view computes any of them.
- Hidden information is hidden structurally: what the party cannot see is not in the doc a player's phone receives. A test reads the player's doc.
- Every new machine or lifecycle is explicit (a zone's life is short enough to be a rule with tests; if you find it has states, give it a `TABLE` and a chart).
- No dependency. No test skipped, loosened or given a retry; no fixed wait. Only `transform` and `opacity` animate.
- The open arena with no map, no zones and no fog must play exactly as today: every existing test passes unchanged.
- Where a decision cannot be built as written, build the nearest thing that serves the owner's words and put the difference at the top of the notes.
