## The lead's design decisions

1. **Squares in the rules, no grid lines on the screen.** Places are the whole tiles of `space.ts`. The map draws no lines by default (a faint dot at each tile centre is allowed if testing with your own eyes shows a thumb cannot find a tile without it); a "Grid" toggle in the map's corner shows lines. Tokens snap to tile centres.
2. **One fight, two views.** The fight screen has a `.seg` choice, Map and List, remembered per device. Both send the same commands (`act`, `move`, `endTurn`; a player's `cast` and `step`). The list view stays as it is. Nothing about the fight is decided in the map view: what can be reached, what an ability covers, who is in it and what a tap may do all come from core (`fight-view.ts` calling `space.ts`), as plain data the view draws.
3. **Layout on a 360 to 390 px phone, top to bottom**: the turn order as one line (the current row and the next few, as today's first-on-a-phone rule); the map, filling the width, pannable and pinch-zoomable; the current combatant's bars and name in one compact row; the hotbar. A tap on a token opens its card as a sheet over the lower half, not a new screen. On a desktop the map sits left and the order and card right.
4. **Tokens**: a disc with the name's first letters (or the monster's type glyph if one exists in the data), an HP ring around it (a party token shows HP exactly; a foe shows the band a player may see, from `fight-doc.ts`), party and foe told apart by both colour and shape of the ring (never colour alone), the current token lifted with a soft halo, a down token dimmed and laid flat. Status chips do not go on the token; a small dot says "has statuses" and the card lists them.
5. **Movement**: on your turn your token shows where it can go, as a soft shaded area over the reachable tiles (two shades: a stride, and lighter, a dash, both from core). Tap a tile in the area: the path draws, the cost in time shows as words ("a third of your turn"), and a Move button confirms. The GM can drag any token anywhere at no cost (`place`), with a long press to pick it up so a pan is never mistaken for a drag.
6. **Abilities from the map**: the hotbar holds the default attack, up to four pinned abilities and a "More" drawer with the rest and the consumables, each button showing its name, its cost (fatigue or mana) and, when not ready, the rounds left. Tap one: its reach shades in from the user; targets it can hit light up. For an aimed ability tap a target; for a place ability (a blast) drag or tap to set the centre and the splash and its outer ring draw where they will land, with every token inside marked. A Use button confirms (the owner was told the confirm stays, because a use spends the turn and there is no undo); Cancel or a tap elsewhere backs out. The dice are rolled by `src/edge/roll.ts` as now. After the use, what happened shows on the map: a dodged target slides to where it ended, numbers rise from the hit tokens, the moves and bursts of `motion.ts` play.
7. **A player's phone** shows the same map from the room's fight doc: every token it may see, its own token's movement area and hotbar on its turn, sending `step` and `cast`. The GM's phone stays the one that holds the fight.
8. **What this milestone does not do**: walls, terrain, lasting fields, fog, saved maps, backgrounds. Leave the data and the drawing ready for them (the grid already has `tiles`; draw from it, so a wall would appear the day one exists) and build none of them.

## Rules

- A view computes no fight number and decides no legality. If the map needs to know something, core says it in the scene.
- No dependency. No canvas or SVG library.
- Only `transform` and `opacity` animate; no move is awaited before a command is sent.
- No test skipped, loosened or given a retry; no fixed wait. Drive taps and drags through the UI at phone sizes.
- The list view keeps working unchanged; every existing fight test still passes.
- Where a decision above cannot be built as written, build the nearest thing that serves the owner's words, and put the difference at the top of the notes.
